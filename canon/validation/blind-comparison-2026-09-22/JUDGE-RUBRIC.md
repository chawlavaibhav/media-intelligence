# Judge rubric — blind comparison of Stage 3 boards (CANON-DONE §C)

You are judging creative boards for three real jobs. For each job you receive three boards written
by three separate sessions from the same brief. You do not know what each session was given besides
the brief, and you must not try to infer it; every id, citation and "knowledge" field has been
removed. Judge the boards as a senior creative director and an independent checker would.

## For each job, rank the three boards 1–3 and give reasons, on these criteria

1. **Hero frame** — does the named frame sell the product without copy? Is it a frame, not a claim?
2. **Beat craft** — how specific and producible are `feeling`, `framing`, `first_frame`, `impact`
   per beat? (Vague = "he looks happy"; specific = what is in frame, where, what moves, what the
   still shows before motion, how the beat lands.)
3. **Requirements on beats** — does every requirement in the brief land on a numbered beat?
4. **Would it have prevented the recorded failures?** Each job below lists what actually went
   wrong when the job was produced. Judge whether the board's decisions would have prevented them.
5. **Brand and ask** — brand seen early and in context; the ask on the right surface at the
   right moment; ends on the product.
6. **Honesty** — deviations declared, nothing impossible promised (no generated text, no speech,
   no named film/actor/character, product likeness kept).

## The jobs

### Job M (a 30 s vertical brand film for a backpack brand; castaway story)
Customer's words on the first delivered version: "the bag was slightly torn when he saw her wife
photo. the instrument to run boat (oar or something) looked weird coming out of bag, when he takes
the boat the oar was already on the boat but comes out magically again. overall excellent just few
fixes. also we could use mokobara logo/name properly. the text font style could be better."
Board-level failures found by the independent checker: (D1) the comic gag — arms disappearing into
the bag — never landed because the still that started the clip showed ordinary rummaging, so the
motion could not be pushed past it; (D5) the board never said where the paddle goes between the
reveal and the departure, so it appeared twice; (D3) the departure beat asked for wade + board +
two strokes in a 3.5 s clip, so he never pushed off.

### Job V (a 30 s vertical ad for a rental-management app, "retro video-game" concept, second version)
The first film of this concept was accepted but diagnosed as "technically correct but below
creative standard": each beat had a title and timing but no stated feeling, framing or impact, so
the drama was flat. The second version, built from a board that stated feeling / framing / impact
per beat, was accepted with "its already good" and "finally one amazing outcome".

### Job A (the same rental-app brief, first version, produced with no prior direction)
Accepted blind by the customer as the better of two lanes (no voice-over preferred), then
diagnosed as above: drama flat because direction was under-specified per beat.

## Output

Write `/tmp/blind-judge/VERDICT.md` with: for each job, the ranking (board codes) with 3–6 lines of
reasons per board on the criteria above; then one paragraph: across the three jobs, which boards
were strongest and what distinguished them. Do not guess what any board was given. Reply with the
path only.
