# Blind comparison — judge's verdict

Judged against JUDGE-RUBRIC.md on the six criteria. I did not open any other file and did not
attempt to infer how any board was produced.

---

## Job M — 30 s vertical brand film, backpack, castaway story

### 1 — M-137dd837

- Hero frame earns its place: the packed bag upright on sand with "the front bucket pocket open a
  finger's width so a slit of yellow lining shows with the corner of the photograph tucked inside"
  and "the empty space where a heap of coconuts, rope, fish and water tube lay a moment ago". It is
  a frame, not a claim — the absence of the heap does the selling.
- Beat craft is the tightest in this job. Screen direction is locked by rule ("land and palms are
  screen-left, sea is screen-right, and this holds for the whole film"), holds are timed against a
  stated test ("Held four seconds, longer than the frame takes to describe aloud"), and each
  `first_frame` is a genuine pre-motion state ("no hand yet").
- On the recorded failures it is the only board that prevents all three. D1: beat 4's `first_frame`
  is already "right arm in the open bag to the elbow" and the impact insists "the joke needs a still
  frame" — the still starts inside the gag, so motion has somewhere to go. D5: no oar exists; the
  raft carries "a sail made of a torn grey shirt" and beat 5 enumerates every object that goes in,
  so nothing can reappear. D3: beat 6 is 4.5 s of walking into the shallows toward the raft, with
  no boarding and no strokes asked of the clip.
- Brand and ask are best handled here: the wordmark lands at 5 s over plain sand "so the brand is
  named inside ten seconds", the bag is in every beat, and the film ends on the beat-5 hero still
  rather than an unrelated card.
- Honesty: four deviations declared with the brief clause that forced each, text is composed by code
  only, and the wordmark rule is strict — "placed as supplied, never redrawn or recoloured". It also
  answers the customer's "text font style could be better" with "three type ranks only" and measured
  contrast. One gap: nothing in the identity anchor or negative line forbids a torn or damaged bag,
  which was the customer's first complaint.

### 2 — M-ac072eae

- The best single hero frame of the three: the open bag "ringed by a neatly laid-out survival haul
  ... with a carved wooden paddle and a hand-made fishing spear lying across the foreground, both
  plainly longer than the bag". One still states the product claim with no copy, and the same image
  is the beat's `first_frame`, so the gag is in the still and not only in the motion.
- It is the only board that answers D5 instead of deleting it. The paddle is visible in the haul,
  "then the paddle, then the spear, slide in vertically and vanish", and at the departure he "push[es]
  a small lashed-driftwood raft" with nothing in his hands — the object's whereabouts are stated
  between reveal and departure. D3 is prevented too: beat 6 cuts "as the raft floats free of the
  sand" and beat 7 opens with him already seated.
- Beat craft is strong throughout — sea locked screen-left, cuts motivated by performance ("Cut on
  his jaw setting: the decision to go"), and a real opening image in the grab handle "standing proud
  of a fan of white surf foam".
- The serious fault is on requirements. The brief's joke is that "he could keep his arms in the bag";
  this board reassigns it, realising M5 as "his 'arms' (a fishing spear longer than the bag)". That is
  a substitution of the customer's own gag, it is not listed among the deviations, and the arms-in-bag
  beat never happens.
- Brand arrives late: the product is on screen from 0 s, but the wordmark and every string wait for
  the 28 s card, which then holds only two seconds for a mark, a product name, a capacity and a URL.

### 3 — M-8a8d89da

- The writing is vivid and the story reach is the widest — the tally-scratched palm, "he does not
  look back at the island", and a doorstep ending where "the door-open light is the last motion in
  the film and it lands on the bag, not on the reunion".
- But the hero frame is asserted rather than ordered. It describes the arm "inside it up to the
  shoulder, his cheek pressed against the top grab handle", while beat 6's own `first_frame` is "the
  castaway's hand holding the first coconut just above the opening" and beat 5's is "the castaway's
  two hands just entering it". The gag exists only as the end point of a motion — which is exactly
  the D1 failure: an ordinary-rummaging still that the motion cannot be pushed past.
- Clips are overloaded in the D3 manner. Beat 5 asks for eight items landing in a grid "one item per
  half-second" plus arms going in past the shoulders, in 4 s; beat 8 opens an entirely new location
  (a Mumbai corridor, a set-down, a rolling coconut, a doorbell and a door opening) in 3 s.
- Brand is the weakest of the three: the wordmark appears only on the composed card at 27 s, and the
  close abandons the island for a product still on a navy field.
- `"deviations": []` is not credible for a board that adopts a comic register, runs without voice-over
  and invents a home-arrival location the brief never asks for. Credit where due: it is the only M
  board to answer the customer's torn-bag note head-on — "the bag is unharmed and that is almost
  unbelievable", "the man is a wreck, the bag is not" — and it keeps the wife "never sharp enough to
  be anyone".

## Job V — 30 s vertical ad, rental-management app, retro video-game concept (second version)

### 1 — V-7cb5eaba

- Hero frame makes the product causal, not decorative: the phone held up is "the one blue rectangle
  in a grey world" and "the product in his hand is visibly the source of the light that is clearing
  the obstacles". No copy in frame.
- Beat craft is the most precise of all nine boards. Events are placed inside the clip to the tenth
  of a second ("The hit lands at 1.2 s", "Shatter at 0.6 s with a bright 8-bit chime; label holds
  1.2 s"), camera behaviour is stated every time ("Camera holds, world scrolls"), and departures are
  motivated ("his eyes drop to his left trouser pocket — the motivated departure into the cheat beat").
  Each `first_frame` is a true pre-motion state and says so: "no words in the generated still".
- This is the strongest answer to the diagnosed failure of the first film. `feeling` is never a face
  description; it is a position — "The grind of asking for rent again: all effort, no reward",
  "Calm: the noise lines up and files itself".
- All fifteen strings land on numbered beats, including the two duplicate placements, and every
  problem label is answered by its fix in the same screen zone.
- Honesty is the best in the set: seven deviations, each tied to a brief clause, including the one
  that costs it something — that fourteen beats is a parade of short scenes, owned openly ("14 beats
  are forced; beats 1–5 and 8–12 are declared action passages"). It also declares `native_audio: false`
  with SFX composed in post, and measures contrast ("white on #0038FF about 7.0:1").
- Cost: the mark is withheld to 9.0 s, and fourteen clips at 1.7–1.8 s is a real production load.
  Both are argued rather than hidden.

### 2 — V-081c5a26

- The most legible hero frame in this job: the flagpole with "a three-storey pixel PG building with
  every window lit warm yellow and a small cyan check-mark floating over each window" and "five small
  grey heaps where the obstacles used to be". It reads in a glance with no copy.
- Best brand timeline of the three: the wordmark sits in the HUD band "as the score plate for the
  whole game" from 0 s, on the phone at 12 s, on the flag at 23 s, and on the card at 27 s.
- The smartest copy mechanic anywhere in this job: each problem label is replaced by its fix in the
  same slot, "so the swap itself is the message" — TENANT VERIFICATION becomes DIGITAL KYC where it
  stood.
- Beat craft is concrete and well-paced at nine beats — "he lands with a two-frame squash-and-stretch",
  "Hold 0.7 s on the flat owner, one eye blinking, before anything else happens".
- Two things keep it second. Beats 6 and 7 each clear two or three obstacles with label swaps inside
  4 s, which is busier than the beats can comfortably hold; and contrast is asserted, not measured,
  in the one place it matters most — "contrast well above 4.5:1 over the busy game frame" is precisely
  the case where a number on a measured frame is needed.

### 3 — V-f1784420

- Hero frame is a generic action beat — sprint, bolts, bursting obstacles — and its selling line,
  "the thing that changed is blue-and-cyan in his hand", is an argument about the frame rather than
  something the frame composes.
- Beat craft slips into commentary in place of direction: "A cheerful start we are about to punish",
  "the emptiness is the joke". Several impacts name music and SFX but not camera or hold.
- Beat 8 is the worst-loaded clip in this job: 5.5 s carrying a level rewind, five obstacles
  re-entering in order, five destructions, the flagpole growing "from small to full height", and five
  supers stacking. That is the same over-ask that stopped a beat landing before.
- Brand is absent for most of the film — `first_mark_s: 18.0`, six problem beats before the turn —
  and unlike the board ranked first here, the late mark is never declared as a deviation.
- The ask is fragile: a 1.5 s close card with "RENTOK.COM below in cyan" on blue #0038FF, the one
  pairing in the palette that can fail legibility, with no contrast measured anywhere. Only one
  deviation is declared (the duration band).

## Job A — same rental-app brief, first version

### 1 — A-8d76de10

- Hero frame is specific and causal: the powered owner "caught at the top of a jump ... every obstacle
  he met is recoiling", with the hearts "just refilled from empty to full (last heart still flashing)"
  and "no copy anywhere in the play area". It sells the turn with no words.
- The five obstacles are written as named identity anchors (OBSTACLE_ID_GHOST, OBSTACLE_RUNAWAY_COINS
  and so on) with fixed descriptions, so the same sprite is reproducible when it returns in beats 7–8.
  That is the strongest continuity device in this job.
- Beat craft is consistently directive rather than descriptive: "he jumps at the ghost, bounces off it
  with a hit thud and a half-second white flash, lands on his back, one heart pops"; "the bulb swings
  once more and goes out (room goes black)". Feelings are written as the viewer's position — "Game
  over is one hit away. The viewer wants someone to give him a way out" — which is the direct answer
  to the flat-drama diagnosis.
- Brand and ask: the wordmark is in the HUD from frame one to 28 s, brand blue is the ground the whole
  level is built from, "the win condition is literally the brand" on the unfurling flag, and the close
  ends on the phone with the mark on its screen.
- Honesty: three deviations, including the one that matters most — the customer's literal "gun" is
  changed and declared, "the weapon is the phone firing tick-bolts rather than a firearm, to stay
  inside Reels/Shorts advertising norms". Contrast is reasoned per placement.
- Ding: beats 7 and 8 each clear two or three obstacles inside 3.5 s.

### 2 — A-a4753377

- The most distinctive direction of the nine — a "handmade stop-motion diorama" of a cutaway PG
  building climbed floor by floor, with the owner's own key ring doubling as the lives counter. The
  form is genuinely re-angled rather than borrowed.
- Its opening beat is the best in this job: a tight close-up held "the full 2.5 s so the face reads
  before the world is shown", cut in "already mid-hit", with no music until the emotion has landed.
  Beat 3's rhythm is stated as staging, not mood — "Three knocks make the rhythm, three door slams
  answer them; the third slam is the cut point."
- Hero frame is strong and causal: the spray "hits that outline and turns to cyan dust in mid-air"
  while "the five monsters stacked on the stair to the roof lean back from him".
- All fifteen strings land on numbered beats, contrast is measured (14.3:1 on the nameplate chips,
  7.0:1 on the card), and six deviations are declared — the most thorough honesty in this job,
  including the risk that a borrowed game form is cliché unless re-angled.
- Two things hold it below first. Beat 9 asks a 4 s clip to carry a tilt-and-track camera move, five
  kills in order and five supers stacking in — the one over-loaded clip here. And the win condition
  is a "plain blue #0038FF felt flag", so unlike the board above it the moment of triumph does not
  carry the mark.

### 3 — A-b2cf7ecc

- Hero frame is the weakest of the three and mostly asserted: "Everything says immune and winning:
  power in the hand, obstacles dissolving, brand blue under his feet and brand cyan in his fist" is
  a summary of intent, not a composed picture.
- Beat craft thins out in the second half. Beats 9 and 10 list events without stating camera or hold —
  "Two clean shots, two clean resolutions, faster music" — and beat 3 contains an instruction that
  cancels itself: "a coin counter would show nothing (no counter is drawn)". There are good moments
  ("the owner's shoulders drop in a two-frame sulk"), but they are not sustained.
- Requirements do all land on numbered beats, and the label-then-tick pairing across beats 2–6 and
  8–10 is sound.
- Brand is late and undeclared: `first_mark_s: 15.4`, so the mark is absent for the first half, and
  the deviation list says nothing about it. The flag carries the wordmark for only "the final half
  second".
- The ask is the most fragile of the nine: a 1.5 s card with "RENTOK.COM at the bottom in cyan" on
  blue #0038FF, unmeasured. Only two deviations are declared, neither covering the late brand, the
  all-caps supplied strings or the absent voice-over.

## Across the three jobs

The three strongest boards — M-137dd837, V-7cb5eaba and A-8d76de10 — win for the same three reasons,
and none of them is knowledge. First, their `first_frame` is a real pre-motion state that already
contains the idea, so the motion has somewhere to go instead of having to rescue an ordinary setup:
"right arm in the open bag to the elbow" before anything moves, "no words in the generated still",
"first tick-bolt already leaving the phone". Second, they say what happens inside a clip and when —
"The hit lands at 1.2 s", "Held four seconds, longer than the frame takes to describe aloud" — and
they keep each clip to one job, which is what stops a beat asking for three actions and delivering
none. Third, they declare what they departed from and measure text against the ground it sits on,
which is where the weaker boards quietly fail. M-ac072eae deserves a special mention for being the
only board in Job M to state where the paddle goes rather than removing it from the story, and
A-a4753377 for the most original direction of the nine; both lose on a specific, checkable point —
a substituted gag in one case, an overloaded clip and an unbranded flag in the other. The three
weakest — M-8a8d89da, V-f1784420 and A-b2cf7ecc — share a recognisable profile: a hero frame written
in prose that no beat's `first_frame` actually orders, one clip carrying three actions, the brand
mark arriving past the halfway point with no declaration, and a deviations list that is empty or
nearly so. A board that declares nothing is not a board with nothing to declare.
