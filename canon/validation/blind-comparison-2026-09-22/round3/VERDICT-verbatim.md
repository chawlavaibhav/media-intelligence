# Blind comparison — judge's verdict

Twelve boards, three jobs, four boards per job. Ranked 1–4 within each job on the rubric's six
criteria: hero frame, beat craft, requirements on beats, prevention of the recorded failures,
brand and ask, honesty. Length was disregarded; the two longest boards do not both finish first.

---

## Job M — 30 s vertical brand film, backpack brand, castaway story

### 1. M-910d9803

- The hero frame is the strongest single frame in the job because it is an argument, not a pose:
  the open bag with "a coil of tarred rope, a folded fishing net, two coconuts, a dented tin, a
  small brass compass, a rolled bleached shirt, a flint, and one small photograph" laid round it
  and "a lashed driftwood-and-cord fishing spear already longer than the bag is tall" still coming
  out, with the man "small in the top third… so his body gives the bag its scale." It names its own
  logic — "the whole argument is the visible arithmetic between one thirty-litre bag and everything
  that came out of it" — and needs no copy.
- It is the clearest answer to D1. The comic beat's `first_frame` *is* the hero frame, with "the
  spear already risen a bag's height out of the main compartment" before anything moves, so the
  still starts past the point where the produced version stalled on ordinary rummaging.
- D5 is answered by bookkeeping rather than luck: the spear comes out in beat 3 and is explicitly
  put back in beat 5 — "the spear is lashed along one side under a compression strap" — so the one
  long object has a stated location between reveal and departure.
- D3 is designed out: beat 6 asks for one action in 5.5 s, a walk "into the shallows towards frame
  right with the packed bag on his back," not wade-plus-board-plus-strokes.
- Its real cost is brand: `first_mark_s` is 27.5, the supplied wordmark appearing only on the close,
  which is thin against a customer who asked to "use mokobara logo/name properly" — though the
  composed name lands at 4.2 s and the type is the most rigorously specified in the job
  ("the ratio runs 11.4:1 to 6.2:1"), which does answer the font complaint.
- Honesty is exemplary: it declares what it is giving up — "This cut does give up three-dimensional
  continuity: we never saw the raft being built" — which is precisely the class of silence that
  produced the recorded failures.

### 2. M-258e5dc5

- Hero frame close behind: the pack centre-frame with "one straight line of everything that came
  out of it… The line of objects is longer than he is." Simple, countable, sells capacity in one
  still.
- Beat craft is excellent and the comic beat is protected the same way — the `first_frame` has "the
  line of objects already laid out and completely still… no motion anywhere in the frame," so the
  joke does not depend on the motion model.
- D5 is handled: the spear goes out in beat 3 and is explicitly stowed in beat 5, "the lashed wooden
  spear run along the outside of the body under the straps."
- It loses ground on D3. Beat 6 is the shortest clip in the film at 3.0 s and asks for two actions:
  "He swings the pack up; the black padded straps settle onto his shoulders… He walks left to right
  into the shallows." That is the same overload shape that stopped the produced departure.
- It also re-reads the customer's gag clause as armaments — "His arms are there as a lashed wooden
  spear and a dented tin" — which quietly drops the arms-into-the-bag image the customer asked for.
- Brand and honesty are strong: wordmark at 7.0 s, product in all seven beats, and a declared
  spatial-continuity sacrifice "taken from the bottom of the list."

### 3. M-137dd837

- The only board that keeps the customer's gag literally: "his arm sinks to the elbow, then to the
  shoulder, his cheek pressed against the top grab handle," with the `first_frame` already at
  "right arm in the open bag to the elbow." That is a genuine answer to D1, though the escalation
  still has to be earned by motion, unlike the boards whose stills are already impossible.
- Best brand timeline in the job: wordmark at 5 s "so the brand is named inside ten seconds," 30L at
  13.5 s, product name at 22 s, card at 26 s, product in every frame.
- The hero frame is the weakest of the four as an argument: a packed bag beside "the empty space
  where a heap of coconuts, rope, fish and water tube lay a moment ago." The proof has already left
  the frame, so the still leans on the viewer's memory of the previous beat.
- D5 is avoided rather than solved — there is no oar, spear or pole anywhere, so no object can
  appear twice, but nothing in the board shows the discipline that would have caught it.
- Two production risks go undeclared: beat 3 contains an internal cut inside a 4 s generated clip
  ("Two shots… Cut on the lift"), and beat 5 pushes six items plus the photograph plus both zips
  plus a hand-rest through 5.0 s.

### 4. M-67796eb4

- Craft is genuinely fine — the tally marks on wet basalt, the reveal done "by the water, not by the
  man and not by the camera" — and the lashing continuity is the most explicit anywhere: the spear
  is "lashed flat under the small top grab handle" in beat 4, still there in beat 5, "lashed
  upright in the bow" in beat 6.
- Then it commits the recorded failure in its own last act. In beat 6 "He plants a pole in the sand
  bar and pushes" — a pole that exists in no earlier beat and is accounted for nowhere. That is
  D5 exactly: a long object arriving from nothing at the departure.
- It splits the departure across two beats (shoulder 19.5–24.0, push-off 24.0–28.0), which is the
  best answer to D3 in the job, and deserves credit for it.
- The hero frame sells survival rather than capacity — the pack as "the only dark, intact,
  dry-looking thing in frame" — which is a good frame for a different proposition than the one the
  30L gag and super are built on.
- It also drops the arms gag ("His 'arms' are the carved spear-shaft"), its opening frame is
  deliberately unassertive — "the frame looks like an ordinary wide of an empty beach and announces
  nothing" — and the mark arrives latest of the three that place it in-film, at 9.5 s.

## Job V — 30 s vertical ad, rental-management app, retro video-game concept

### 1. V-222df3c5

- The hero frame is the only one in the job that puts the real product in a real hand inside the
  fiction: "A real hand holding a real phone a few centimetres above an open paper rent register…
  at the right edge of that light, where the pixel world starts again, the paper spike and the
  falling bells are already coming apart into loose cyan grain. One frame, two worlds, the product
  in a working hand between them."
- Beat craft is specific and adult throughout — "The flat, unglamorous grind of the first week of
  the month. He is quick and he is competent, and it still is not enough" — and it refuses the easy
  register: "No slapstick — he does not trip, stumble or gurn; the objects leave, he stays."
- Requirements land with a device that does the explaining: each clear "snaps square and locks down
  into the ledger row beneath as a filled cyan tile," landing "in the exact screen position its
  problem label held in beats 2 and 3."
- Brand is earliest and never lapses — the supplied mark "rides the game HUD's top-left corner from
  1.5 s to 22.0 s… the way a sponsor's mark rides a real game screen" — and the ask is rehearsed
  inside the story as the cheat code before it is made of the viewer.
- Honesty is complete: the firearm substitution, the one restless beat, the all-caps penalty "at
  label length only," and the refusal to invent a figure are each declared with their cost.

### 2. V-7a24f3e4

- A fine hero frame and the only one that carries no copy at all: the flagpole with "ten doors that
  were dark and scuffed at the start now each carry one small cyan light," the register "shut and
  clipped under his arm," and "a phone glow[ing] cyan in his other hand." It states its own test —
  "the frame says the mess became one glowing screen."
- The best burr image in the job and a first frame that earns the scroll: "three loose paper rent
  slips have already come out of his register and hang in the air… a PG owner recognises it
  instantly and it is the surprise that opens the film, not a logo."
- Strong performance direction where others give stage business: "he never celebrates mid-run: he
  simply stops flinching, which is the honest version of the promise."
- Two deductions. Brand is withheld for the first third by choice (declared, and defensible: "a
  cheat code that is already on screen is not a cheat code"), and the cheat is delivered by a brick
  that "cracks and opens downward like a hit question-block" while its own negative line bars
  question blocks — closer to the franchise than the board admits.
- The copy deck also mis-maps its own answers: `fix_3` "Answers obstacle_label_4's money problem"
  and `fix_4` "Answers obstacle_label_4's reconciliation gap" — two fixes claiming one problem,
  which the beats themselves get right but a checker would stop on.

### 3. V-6d38cd70

- Craft is close to the top of the job. The whole film is "ONE continuous scene, a single lateral
  travel at constant speed into which the events arrive," the low point is built by withholding the
  track, and the power-up is argued rather than asserted: obstacles are "not destroyed but laid flat
  into the floor so he runs across them," because "the work does not disappear, it stops blocking
  him."
- The photoreal man inside a flat 2-D world is the sharpest treatment idea in the job, and it is
  reasoned from the brief rather than styled.
- But a named requirement never lands on a beat. The customer asked for the flag; this board
  substitutes the roof — "The flag is beat 7… he takes hold of the steel frame of his own water
  tank." There is no flag anywhere in the film, and nothing declares the substitution.
- Beat 3 also carries three of the five problems in 3.4 s — tenant, pit and falling cement with
  three separate labels — which is the densest passage in the job and sits awkwardly beside its own
  warning about a parade.
- Brand and honesty are otherwise good: mark at 9.4 s, the brand blue as the floor "from frame one,"
  and the sound-off pairing declared "an uncovered case, not a solved one."

### 4. V-7cb5eaba

- Everything the brief asks for is present and cleanly mapped, and the power-up is the best-owned
  idea in the board: the owner's own keys become the weapon, "a heavy steel ring of a dozen keys"
  igniting into the KEY BLASTER. The hero frame is honest about the product's role — "The product in
  his hand is visibly the source of the light that is clearing the obstacles."
- But the direction thins exactly where this job's diagnosis said it must not. The feeling lines
  collapse into labels — "Satisfaction of a clean hit," "Ease," "Control," "Order," "Calm" — and the
  impact lines repeat verbatim across beats 8 to 12: "Camera holds, world scrolls."
- Fourteen beats at 1.7–1.8 s each is the parade the board itself concedes ("14 beats are forced"),
  and the clears are five near-identical shots differing only in which object is hit.
- The music "enters on the first footstep at 0.0 s at full game tempo," so nothing is held back for
  the turn; the other three boards all make the score arrive after the picture has made the feeling.
- Brand and honesty are adequate — mark at 9.0 s, measured contrast on the card, deviations listed —
  but the deviation reasoning is the thinnest of the four, and the blaster "firing fans of glowing
  cyan keys" is the most weapon-like reading of a clause the others resolved with light.

## Job A — same brief, first version, produced with no prior direction

### 1. A-9dd4722d

- The best hero frame in the whole set, because it is a rhyme and not a beauty shot: shot "from the
  same angle and the same stretch of handrail as beat 2," the stairwell "now blank ochre plaster
  where five doors used to be," and the phone "showing five cyan tick-rows stacked in exactly the
  positions the five doors occupied. The corridor of problems is now a list in his hand."
- It solves the missing-figure problem with a picture instead of a claim: NOTE_FIST, "a fist-sized
  stack of pale rectangular pixel rent-notes… countable at a glance," bleeding a note per door and
  refilling on the run — "countable objects rather than digits."
- Each problem gets its own physical form (a latch for a mouth, a door with too many keyholes, a
  door "standing open onto nothing," a door built of register pages, a door carrying bell-pushes),
  and each carries a purpose-built blank plate so the composited string has a measured ground.
- Brand is present from frame zero as the cartridge mark and never lapses through four asset types
  — "HUD mark, product screen in use, in-situ flag, closing card."
- Honesty is the best in the job: it accepts a known risk out loud — "The cartoon half of the
  objection is accepted knowingly" — and declares the gun-to-immunity departure "so the departure
  from the customer's word is visible rather than quietly dropped."

### 2. A-be820af8

- A superb hero frame that shows the mechanism, not just the mood: the phone "held out ahead of him
  like a lamp rather than carried like a prop," with blocks "already coming apart into falling coins
  at the exact edges the light touches, while the blocks outside the light are still solid."
- The strongest structural idea in the job is the ending: the film leaves the game entirely for "a
  real paying-guest owner's desk," so "the vehicle is dismantled on screen at 25.6 s so the viewer
  is left holding the product, not the game." That is the most direct answer anywhere to the risk
  that the pastiche eats the brand.
- Direction is built on removals as well as additions — the downward scroll in beat 2 exists so the
  upward reversal reads, and "The screen-shake that landed every block in beats 1–3 is simply absent
  now — the removal is the point, and it is felt before it is noticed."
- Brand reasoning is the most honest of the twelve: it refuses to claim the colour as attribution,
  "in-situ branding and not yet attributable, since the colour has not been tied to the product in a
  close shot."
- Two costs. The five problems are one interchangeable object, "PROBLEM_BLOCK… its face always
  blank," so the words carry the distinctions the picture does not. And the ask gets the shortest
  hold of any board here — the close card is 1.4 s for a mark, a two-line ask and a URL, against its
  own describe-aloud rule.

### 3. A-a134fc99

- The most director-like beat writing in the job. First frames are written as the frame before the
  motion and often carry the drama by themselves: "two more cubes hang unsupported at the top edge
  of frame… The viewer can see the next two seconds and he cannot."
- Performance is specified where most boards specify blocking — "one wasted movement per cube — a
  reach that becomes a step-back. No sigh, no comedy take, no look to camera" — and the feeling
  lines are precise: "Being outnumbered by small things. Nothing here is a catastrophe; the volume
  is the catastrophe."
- Its hero frame is excellent and product-first: the cyan cone crossing the frame, "the phone is the
  brightest object in frame and it is being used, not displayed… a stranger reads 'this app cleared
  the mess' with no word on screen."
- The substitution device is well built — each check composites "in the same lower-middle zone the
  problem label used — the answer literally replaces the problem in the same place on screen."
- What holds it at third is the same abstraction as the board above, in its purest form: five
  identical "one-metre dull concrete-grey cube[s]… no lettering, no icon, no face, no eyes." With
  the supers removed, nothing distinguishes tenant verification from complaints, and the brief's
  point is that these are five different lives of trouble.

### 4. A-a4753377

- Real strengths, and they are the obstacles: five distinct, memorable creatures — a faceless felt
  tenant holding a blank card, a rent stack with "a jagged torn-paper mouth," a suitcase "with two
  running felt legs in black socks," a ledger "spooling long strips of ruled paper like tentacles,"
  a tap and a hopping bell. The brand is also on screen from frame zero as a HUD chip.
- But the direction is the thinnest in the job. Impact lines run to mechanics — "Three knocks make
  the rhythm, three door slams answer them; the third slam is the cut point" — and several feelings
  are one-word states rather than a read of the man.
- The film's turn lands late: the cheat code runs 16.5–19.5 s of 30, which leaves 4 s for five kills
  and five stacked supers, and 2 s for the card. The payoff half is compressed to fit the setup.
- The hero frame is decent and product-in-use — the phone "like a blaster, the screen throwing a
  cyan glow across his moustache" — but the five monsters "lean back from him," so the frame shows
  intimidation rather than the problems actually resolving.
- Honesty is present but perfunctory beside the others, and the copy deck has a formal slip: the
  `hud_wordmark` placement carries `"contents": []` while its rationale describes a mark that runs
  for 28 s.

## Across the three jobs

What separated the top boards from the bottom was never vocabulary or length — the fourth-placed
board in each job was as fluent as the first, and one of the longest boards did not win its job.
Three things did the separating. First, the **still had to carry the argument**: the boards that won
wrote a `first_frame` that is already impossible, already before-and-after, already the mechanism —
"the line of objects already laid out and completely still," "the spear already risen a bag's height
out of the main compartment," blocks fraying "at the exact edges the light touches, while the blocks
outside the light are still solid," five tick-rows "in exactly the positions the five doors
occupied." The boards that lost asked the motion model to deliver the point, which is exactly how
the recorded gag died. Second, **objects were accounted for between beats, or the gap was declared**:
the top Mokobara board says where the spear is stowed and then volunteers "we never saw the raft
being built," while the bottom one reaches the water and plants a pole that exists nowhere else in
the film — the same magical-object failure the customer already complained about. Third,
**specificity had to reach the picture, not just the label**: boards that gave each of five problems
its own body beat boards that gave five identical grey blocks five different words, and boards whose
impact lines repeated "Camera holds, world scrolls" across five beats rebuilt the flat drama that
this whole second pass existed to fix. The winners also treated brand as a running condition rather
than an end card, ended on the product, and were honest in the same breath — declaring the cost of a
short hold, a withheld mark, an accepted register risk — where the weaker boards left the
inconsistencies for a checker to find: a flag that never appears, two fixes claiming one problem, a
close card too short to read its own ask.
