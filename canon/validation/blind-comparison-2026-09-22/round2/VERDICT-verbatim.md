# Blind comparison — judge's verdict

Fifteen boards, five per job, judged on: hero frame; beat craft (feeling / framing / first_frame / impact);
requirements landing on numbered beats; whether the board's decisions would have prevented that job's
recorded failures; brand and ask; honesty. Ranked 1 (best) to 5 within each job.

---

## Job M — backpack castaway, 30 s vertical

### 1. M-910d9803

- The best hero frame in the set, and it is a frame rather than a claim: the open bag with the arc of nine
  salvaged items laid round it and "a lashed driftwood-and-cord fishing spear already longer than the bag is
  tall", with the man crouched behind "so his body gives the bag its scale". It states its own rule — "nothing
  in the frame is a claim: the whole argument is the visible arithmetic between one thirty-litre bag and
  everything that came out of it."
- Kills D1 at source. The gag beat opens on a still that is already past belief — the first_frame is the hero
  frame with "the spear already risen a bag's height out of the main compartment" — so the motion has somewhere
  to go ("longer than the bag, then longer than the man"). The delivered film failed because its still showed
  ordinary rummaging; this one cannot.
- The only board of the five that answers D5 by discipline rather than by deletion. Beat 5 says exactly where
  the long prop lives between reveal and departure: "the spear is lashed along one side under a compression
  strap", with the cut motivated by "the click of the side-strap buckle". It then declares the continuity it
  does give up — "we never saw the raft being built. That is a sacrifice taken from the bottom of the list."
- D3 is prevented by scope: the departure is 5.5 s for one action, a walk "away from camera into the shallows
  towards frame right", not a wade-plus-board-plus-strokes.
- Best answer to the customer's "text font style could be better": every super carries colour, size, weight,
  case and a measured ratio at both extremes of its ground ("Deep ink (#101820) at 72 px... 14.1:1 against the
  brightest sky and 5.8:1 where a cloud darkens it"), and the wordmark is "the supplied SVG, placed and never
  redrawn, restyled or regenerated".
- Cost, and it is a real one: the customer's own joke — "he could keep his arms in the bag" — never lands on a
  numbered beat. The spear replaces the arms, and unlike its five other deviations this substitution is not
  declared. The wordmark itself also waits until 27.5 s, though the composed name arrives at 4.2 s.

### 2. M-137dd837

- The cleanest complete board. Every customer clause lands on a numbered beat, including the one M-910d9803
  drops: beat 4, "The arm", whose first_frame already shows "right arm in the open bag to the elbow" before any
  motion — the same D1 fix, applied to the gag the customer actually asked for.
- D5 cannot recur because the oar is gone: the kit is "three green coconuts, a leaf-wrapped bundle of dried
  fish, a coil of coir rope, a bamboo water tube, a flint blade, a folded palm-leaf mat", and the raft carries
  "a sail made of a torn grey shirt". That is a direct answer to the customer's complaint rather than an evasion,
  though it solves the problem by removing it.
- D3 prevented: beat 6 is 4.5 s of a single lateral track as he "walks from frame-left across pale sand into
  the shallows toward a small driftwood raft... moored at frame-right".
- Strongest brand timeline of the five. The wordmark lands at 5 s "at the moment of recognition", 30L at 13.5 s
  "beside the spin", the product name at 22 s "as it is seen in use on his back", and the close holds the hero
  still — which is the plainest answer to "we could use mokobara logo/name properly".
- Its hero frame is the quieter choice — the packed bag with "the empty space where a heap of coconuts, rope,
  fish and water tube lay a moment ago". It works, but it asks the viewer to remember the heap, where the top
  two boards show the arithmetic in the frame itself.

### 3. M-a8c82d8e

- Good hero frame, honestly argued: top-down on the open bag with the salvage in a half circle, "every single
  item plainly bigger than the opening it is about to go into. No hands, no face, no words in the frame." It
  then declares the deviation this costs — a top-down "shows fewer surfaces than a three-quarter, because
  capacity is what the brief asks the frame to prove and only the mouth-up view can prove it."
- Beat craft is disciplined and producible: "No camera move at all — the only movement is his hand and then his
  breathing"; ambient "falling as if he has stopped hearing it". first_frames are genuinely pre-motion states.
- D1 only half-prevented. The still loads the promise (items bigger than the opening) but the beat still starts
  from a static laid-out arc with "nothing moving", so the impossible thing has to be manufactured entirely in
  the motion — the same dependency that broke the delivered film, if less severely.
- D5 is avoided rather than solved: the spear shaft goes in and never comes back out, so nothing can appear
  twice, but the board never states where a long prop lives. D3 is prevented — beat 6 is one walk, and "The cut
  comes on that step, not on the raft."
- Fine brand thinking: the wordmark bug at 7.5 s, and a close that grades the held final frame of beat 6 so
  "the story's last image and the brand's first solo frame are literally the same picture and attribution
  cannot slide off." But the arms gag is missing here too, undeclared.

### 4. M-ac072eae

- Serviceable hero frame that does sell capacity without copy: the open bag ringed by the haul with "a carved
  wooden paddle and a hand-made fishing spear lying across the foreground, both plainly longer than the bag...
  The bag is small; the pile is not."
- Beats are specific in the picture and thinner in the reasoning: beat 1's "one full wave cycle of sound (hiss,
  draw, silence) before the hand enters" is excellent; elsewhere the impact fields mostly name a camera and a
  sound rather than a decision.
- D1 is the weak point. Beat 5 starts on hands "reach[ing] in from frame right for the first coconut" —
  ordinary action — and the impossible moment ("the spear vanishing is the joke") is deferred to the motion.
- It is the only board that keeps a paddle, and it keeps it safely by never letting it back out; but the
  departure beat asks for a wade plus "pushing a small lashed-driftwood raft ahead of him" inside 4 s, which is
  the shape of D3 again, and beat 7 then jumps to him already seated on open water.
- Brand and ask are the thinnest bar one: name, capacity, wordmark and URL all arrive on a single 2-second card
  at 28 s. For a customer who asked for the logo and name to be used properly, two seconds at the end is a
  small answer. Two deviations declared, both fair.

### 5. M-8a8d89da

- The hero frame is genuinely good — arm in to the shoulder, "his cheek pressed against the top grab handle",
  the island laid in a ring, "The joke and the product claim are the same picture." It is also the only board
  that turns the customer's "bag was slightly torn" note into a story value: "the bag is unharmed and that is
  almost unbelievable", and at the door "the man is a wreck, the bag is not."
- But it would not have prevented D1, which is the failure this job actually recorded. Both gag beats open on
  ordinary action: beat 5's first_frame is "the castaway's two hands just entering it", beat 6's is "the
  castaway's hand holding the first coconut just above the opening". The arms-to-the-shoulder moment is the
  last half-second of each clip — exactly the structure that could not be pushed past.
- Beat craft is the least specific of the five: "handheld feel", "A short hard zoom in on the laugh", "he
  presses it to his chest and laughs silently up at the sky" describe performance rather than what is in frame
  and what moves.
- The wordmark appears once, at 27 s, on the close card only; beat 8 also opens a whole second location, a
  Mumbai corridor and door, for a 3 s generated clip — cost and identity risk for a beat the brief does not ask for.
- "deviations": [] — nothing declared, on a board that overrides the no-humour default, has no voice at all and
  invents a second location. That is the one flat failure of honesty in job M.

## Job V — rental app, retro video-game, second version

### 1. V-222df3c5

- The level is the owner's own rent register: "platforms are the ruled rows of a paper rent register... seen
  edge-on as solid ledges two pixels thick". The hero frame pays that off in one picture — a real hand holding a
  real phone over the real page, "its cyan light lying flat across the ruled page", and at the light's edge "the
  paper spike and the falling bells are already coming apart into loose cyan grain. One frame, two worlds, the
  product in a working hand between them."
- Every beat has a feeling that could only belong to this buyer, which is precisely what the first film lacked:
  "He is quick and he is competent, and it still is not enough — the frustration belongs to the system, never to
  him"; "the flatter thing underneath it: the month has beaten him again and he can see exactly how."
- Impact fields make directorial decisions, not descriptions: "The duck is silent; the cash slide gets the
  beat's only sound accent... so the loss is heard rather than shown twice"; "the bells land off the beat, so the
  last 1.2 s read as a stall rather than a climax."
- Requirements are fully placed and paired: all fifteen strings verbatim, each clear landing "in the exact screen
  positions s03–s07 held, so the swap is legible without anyone explaining it", with real measured ratios
  (15:1, 18:1, 16:1) and named Reels/Shorts safe zones.
- Honesty is the deepest here: seven deviations, including the all-caps penalty "accepted at label length only",
  one declared restless beat, the describe-aloud licence taken only inside the action passage, and the firearm
  replaced by light with the ASCI check flagged as still owed.
- Minor: `native_audio: false` with the cue laid by code sits slightly against the brief's audio flag, though the
  reasoning — one unbroken track "instead of restarting per clip" — is a production insight, not an oversight.

### 2. V-0d068fa1

- The clearest product demonstration of any board in either rental job. Through the phone's screen the corridor
  is "clean, lit, each cot row calm with a cyan status pill beside it", around the rectangle it is "still dust,
  hanging paper and a man down on one knee", and "The owner's sprite straddles the screen's edge, so the same man
  is visible in both states in one frame."
- Beat craft is excellent and physical: a first_frame of "the OWNER caught mid-stride, right foot off the floor,
  the register flapping open with four loose slips already leaving it"; an impact where "the run-cycle sound stops
  dead on the same frame" and "the card's landing is a concrete thud, not a game 'boing'".
- It refuses the parade the first version fell into — "one continuous side-scrolling platformer level — one
  location, one rightward run, not a parade of vignettes" — and withholds music until 10.5 s so the score
  channels "a relief the picture has already made rather than supplying one".
- Brand and ask are properly sequenced: cyan planted small at 0 s, the name composed at 8.0 s, the supplied
  wordmark at 9.6 s, the product shown in use 10.5–14.0 s, and the film "ends on the pack". Five deviations,
  each naming the uncovered case rather than claiming it is solved.
- Held off first place only on rhythm: all five problem words are read between 0.9 s and 7.5 s, some for barely
  a second, where the top board gives each hazard its own clear window.

### 3. V-081c5a26

- Solid, confident, conventional. The hero frame reads in one glance with no copy: the powered owner on the
  flagpole above "a three-storey pixel PG building with every window lit warm yellow and a small cyan check-mark
  floating over each window" and "five small grey heaps where the obstacles used to be."
- Feelings are vivid and short, which is the right register here: "Exasperation: the money has legs"; "The sting
  of being ghosted"; "Crushed: too many problems arriving at once."
- Good structural idea, cleanly stated: each problem label is "answered by its check-marked feature, in the same
  slot, so the swap itself is the message." Brand is on screen from frame one as the HUD score plate and returns
  on the phone, the flag and the card.
- Where it falls behind the top two: the impacts are mostly sound-plus-camera notes rather than decisions, the
  world has no colour discipline so the cyan never becomes the product's own signal, and the contrast claim is
  asserted — "contrast well above 4.5:1 over the busy game frame" — rather than measured against a named ground.
- Two deviations only, and neither covers the no-voice case or the supplied all-caps.

### 4. V-7cb5eaba

- Fourteen beats, ten of them 1.7–2.0 s, which is the parade this job was told to avoid. The board sees it and
  argues the case — "14 beats are forced; beats 1–5 and 8–12 are declared action passages" — but the feelings it
  can afford at that length are one-word states: "Satisfaction of a clean hit", "Ease", "Control", "Order", "Calm".
- The hero frame is genuinely strong and earns its place: the phone held up, "a ring of cyan light bursts outward
  from it", the key ring igniting, bubbles popping inside the ring — "The product in his hand is visibly the
  source of the light that is clearing the obstacles."
- Honesty is good: seven deviations, including the forced beat count, the all-caps, the withheld mark, and the
  brief's literal "gun" replaced by the KEY BLASTER, with the reasoning given rather than buried.
- Craft is undercut by an unresolved style: "chunky low-poly voxel 3D with a clean pixel-art grid feel" asks for
  two different renderers at once, and the board never decides which one governs.
- Requirements all land and the labels sit in one fixed zone throughout, which is the right call for a muted feed.

### 5. V-f1784420

- The weakest on the exact axis this job was diagnosed on. Feelings are asserted rather than built — "Instant
  recognition: 'oh, it is a platformer' and a smile", "Pure release" — and impacts are largely SFX lists: "Two
  bumps timed to two 'thud' SFX and a descending sting... Camera locked."
- Brand arrives at 18.0 s, the latest of any board in either rental job, and the board does not flag it as a
  deviation; it only observes that "Before 18 s the blue brick ground quietly carries brand blue without naming
  the brand."
- One deviation is declared in total, and it is about the duration band — nothing on the absent voice, the
  supplied capitals, or the late mark.
- The placement reasoning is a single line each ("Lands on the first swallowed coin; same super style, top
  third") with no ground named and no ratio measured, on a moving pixel field where legibility is the whole risk.
- Beat 8 then asks one 5.5 s clip to rewind the level, re-line five obstacles, burst all five and stack five
  supers, and the close card holds only 1.5 s — the two places where the film has to be read are the two places
  it is given least time.

## Job A — same rental brief, first version, no prior direction

### 1. A-be820af8

- Real dramatic structure rather than a run of gags: all five problems land as blocks inside 8.6 s and lock
  into "a solid slate wall that fills the upper two-thirds of the tall frame", with the owner "very small at the
  very bottom of the long axis". Beat 2 scrolls him downward on screen so that "the upward reversal in beat 6 is
  legible" — that is film thinking, not game decoration.
- The hero frame makes the product the visible cause: the phone is "the one photoreal object in the whole pixel
  world... held out ahead of him like a lamp rather than carried like a prop", and the blocks "are already coming
  apart into falling coins at the exact edges the light touches, while the blocks outside the light are still
  solid."
- Feelings are written for the real buyer, which is the cure for the recorded flatness: "The small, familiar
  humiliation of being stopped by a thing you already knew was coming... a PG owner should see his own Tuesday,
  not a mascot"; "Momentum — not triumph. A man getting through his own building at walking-into-work speed for
  the first time."
- It fights the parade deliberately: "6.2 s in one take — the longest hold in the film, set deliberately against
  the fast-cut norm so the run reads as one action and not as a parade of scenes."
- Brand carried four ways and ended on the product — mark on the phone at 9.2 s, on the flag, as "the live app on
  the real desk at 25.6 s", then the card — with the match cut specified: the phone "sits in exactly the screen
  position and at exactly the screen size it occupied in the last pixel frame".
- The most honest board in the whole set: nine deviations, including conceding a rule it breaks outright — "The
  ten-second bound is kept; the never-lapsing bound is not, and this is the deviation."

### 2. A-c76ed0a9

- The best single piece of direction in job A is in beat 1: "He runs the full 3.2 s and never gains a pixel on
  the frame edge — the level scrolls at exactly his speed, which is the whole joke and the whole problem."
- Outstanding hero frame, and it sells with the product in use: the beam "leaving that screen and running the
  full height of the frame", the gatekeeper "breaking apart into a falling column of coins that curve back down
  into the phone", and the four remaining hazards "still whole but lit cyan for the first time".
- Beat 7 is the most intelligent solution beat in either rental job: the paper storm "is not shot down — it is
  collected", because "The one hazard that could never be beaten by force is beaten by filing, which is the
  honest mechanic of the product and the reason this beat is not cut for pace."
- The product is on screen from frame one and the board makes its absence the story: the phone is "dark and
  useless through beats 1-3, which is the story", with the mark arriving at 9.6 s inside the window.
- Five deviations, well argued, including the comic register braked so that "the joke carries the selling point
  in every beat", and the borrowed platform-game code "re-aimed so that the level is the owner's month".
- Behind the winner only on brand variety — the mark exists in two places, the phone screen and the card, and
  the flag is left "a plain cyan pennant... no emblem" — and on the crowding of three problem words into a 2.6 s
  beat, which is declared but still the riskiest read in the film.

### 3. A-a4753377

- The most distinctive treatment in job A, and it commits: "Handmade stop-motion diorama... felt, clay and
  painted card", the level a cutaway four-storey PG building, and the lives counter is the man's own keys.
- It opens on emotion instead of an establishing shot, and says why: a tight close-up where "the rent_stack
  monster's torn-paper mouth enters from frame right and slaps his cheek", held "the full 2.5 s so the face
  reads before the world is shown", with "no music yet" so the loop can enter "after the emotion of the hit has
  been made in silence."
- Strong hero frame: phone held out like a blaster, "the grey water spray from the tap at the top of the stair
  hits that outline and turns to cyan dust in mid-air", five monsters leaning back, "The only copy in frame is
  the small HUD wordmark chip at top left."
- Brand from frame one on the HUD chip, on the phone screen at 19.5 s, on the card; contrast measured (14.3:1,
  7.0:1); six deviations declared, including the re-angle argument that "the form cannot be retold without RentOK."
- Held to third by rhythm: eleven beats at 2–3 s each still reads as a floor-by-floor list, and stop-motion
  across ten generated clips is the heaviest consistency bet any board in this job makes.

### 4. A-8d76de10

- A competent, complete pixel board with nothing broken in it. Feelings are properly human — "The gut-drop of the
  empty room. Too late again."; "Game over is one hit away. The viewer wants someone to give him a way out." —
  and cuts are motivated: "The cut out is motivated by his eyes flicking up to something above frame."
- The hero frame works without copy: the powered owner at the top of a jump with "every obstacle he met...
  recoiling" at the frame edges and the hearts refilled — "the man who was losing has the phone, and everything
  that beat him is backing off."
- Brand is handled well and variously: the wordmark in the HUD "from frame one to the flag", the ground tiles in
  brand blue, the mark composed onto the unfurling flag so that "the win condition is literally the brand", and a
  close that ends on the phone.
- What keeps it mid-table is that the board mostly describes a competent game rather than deciding anything a
  director would have to decide; the placement reasons are slots ("Same nameplate slot, obstacle 2") and the
  contrast claim is waved through ("white on #0038FF clears 4.5:1 anyway").
- Three deviations only, and the duration-band one is the least interesting of the three available.

### 5. A-b2cf7ecc

- Twelve beats and the direction thins out across them. Feelings are labels rather than states — "Immunity. The
  tables have turned and it feels great"; "Total control; even the paperwork and the swarm are toys now" — which
  is close to the flatness this job was diagnosed with in the first place.
- The hero frame asserts its effect instead of showing it: "Everything says immune and winning: power in the
  hand, obstacles dissolving, brand blue under his feet and brand cyan in his fist, with no word on screen."
  Saying a frame says something is not the same as building a frame that does.
- Brand arrives at 15.4 s and the board does not treat that as a deviation; the two deviations it lists are the
  duration band and the use of the supplied wordmark instead of a generated product shot.
- Placement reasoning is a line apiece with no ground and no measurement — "Names obstacle two on the first
  missed coin" — on the same busy pixel field every other board here measures.
- The close card holds 1.5 s for a wordmark, two ask lines, a URL and a sprite; the film's only instruction gets
  the shortest read in the film.

## Across the three jobs

What separated the top boards from the bottom ones was not length, vocabulary or how much the board knew — the
longest board in job M finished first and the second-longest in job V finished fourth. Three things did the
separating. First, the **first_frame carried the argument**: the boards that won made the still already contain
the impossible or the emotional fact — "the spear already risen a bag's height out of the main compartment",
"right arm in the open bag to the elbow", the phone's light with "the blocks outside the light... still solid" —
while the losing boards opened on ordinary action and hoped the motion would find the moment, which is exactly
how the delivered backpack film lost its gag. Second, the winners **made decisions and named their cost**:
where the long prop lives between two beats, which beat is the declared action passage, which rule is being
broken and which higher one is being kept ("The ten-second bound is kept; the never-lapsing bound is not, and
this is the deviation"). The bottom boards declare nothing — M-8a8d89da files an empty deviations list while
overriding three defaults, V-f1784420 files one — and an undeclared decision is one nobody can check. Third,
the winners wrote **feeling as a specific person's state rather than a mood word**: "a PG owner should see his
own Tuesday, not a mascot" and "the frustration belongs to the system, never to him" are directions a shot can
be built from; "Pure release" and "Total control" are not, and that difference is the whole of the diagnosis
both rental jobs were given. The weakest boards in all three jobs share one more tell: they spend their
shortest holds on the frames that have to be read — a 1.5 s close card, five supers inside one 5.5 s clip,
three problem words in 2.6 s — and their longest on frames that are already understood.
