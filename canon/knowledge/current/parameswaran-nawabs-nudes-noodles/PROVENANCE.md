## Author's position and interests — relevant to evidence origin

Parameswaran is a career advertising practitioner (FCB Ulka) writing about the value and history of
his own industry. Three consequences are recorded on the objects rather than left to a reader to
notice:

1. Several campaigns discussed are his own or his agency's, and he says so.
2. Proprietary qualitative studies by **Cogito Consulting, his own agency's consulting arm**, are
   reported as findings (the ManMood and Youth Mood studies, the 2002 celebrity study). The
   publisher of these findings has a commercial interest in them, and none is reported with a
   method, sample or effect size.
3. The book opens by defending the profession to a stranger, which is a fair statement of its
   posture.

## Overlap with live Canon

**No work overlap.** This is a new work by an author not otherwise in the corpus, on a subject —
Indian advertising history and Indian cultural context — that no accepted source covers.

Two relations are recorded in the lineage matrix and are **not** independence-defeating:

- The book **cites** Rama Bijapurkar's *We Are Like That Only* and *A Never-Before World* in its
  endnotes. If a Bijapurkar source is ever admitted, that pair carries a `cites_source` relation,
  which the Audit Gate states is deliberately not disqualifying.
- The book **cites** Rachel Dwyer's *Picture Abhi Baaki Hai*, which is a different work from
  *Cinema India*. A citation of an author's other book does not create a relation to the uncited one.
- Piyush Pandey is **named in passing** as one of several writers who continued Hindi-originated
  copywriting. That is incidental mention, not `shared_primary_informant`: no load-bearing claim in
  this book rests on Pandey's own account.
