# Provenance record — Building a StoryBrand, chapters 1–3 (CANON-003 book 18, Lane D)

**Book:** Donald Miller, *Building a StoryBrand: Clarify Your Message So Customers Will Listen*,
HarperCollins Leadership, 2017.
**Section processed:** Section 1 in full — chapter 1 "The Key to Being Seen, Heard, and
Understood", chapter 2 "The Secret Weapon That Will Grow Your Business", chapter 3 "The Simple SB7
Framework". About 8,900 words.

## Why this section
Section 1 is the book's own division and holds the complete reasoning system: the causal argument
for why marketing fails, the case for story as the remedy, and all seven framework elements stated
as numbered principles with the output artefact. Section 2 then gives each element its own chapter.
Processing one of those later chapters would have given one element in depth without the argument
that produced it.

## Visual evidence — verified at figure level, and it mattered here
`visual_completeness: verified_figure_level`. Twelve figure references in the section; six figures
opened and read. Nothing committed.

**Every figure in this book carries `alt="image"`.** A text-only extraction recovers nothing from
them — not a caption, not a label, not a hint that anything was there. Three things were recovered
only by looking:

1. **The framework diagram forks.** The prose lists seven elements in order. The diagram draws the
   last two as branches from one junction — success rising, failure falling — so they are
   alternative outcomes of a decision, not consecutive steps. A text-only reconstruction encodes a
   straight list of seven and is wrong, with nothing in the text to reveal it.
2. **The BrandScript template carries a field schema the prose never states.** Villain, internal,
   external and philosophical under the problem; empathy and authority under the guide; process and
   agreement under the plan; direct and transitional under the call to action — and an eighth box,
   Character Transformation, with a from and a to, in a framework named for seven elements.
3. **The three-level problem taxonomy is demonstrated only in figures.** Chapter 3 states that
   customers face external, internal and philosophical problems and gives no example. The worked
   instances sit in two chapter 2 diagrams, several pages earlier, which the text never points to.

## What this book contributed
**The first source in Lane D where plain text loses a substantial part of the argument, and the
first that binds readily to the product schema.** Books 16 and 17 produced zero Creative IR
bindings between them; this one produced four, plus an evaluation binding, a benchmark binding and
a governance binding.

The reason is not that this source is stronger. Its evidence is the weakest in the lane: the
foundational mechanism is reported conversation with a friend, the results are uncontrolled revenue
figures, and the closing position is unfalsifiable as stated. It binds well because it is about the
same object our product is about — what a commercial message should contain. **Bindability and
evidential quality moved in opposite directions here**, which is worth recording precisely because
a system that ranked Canon knowledge by how well it binds would rank this book first.
