# Provenance record — The Vignelli Canon, Part One (CANON-003 book 5)

**Book:** Massimo Vignelli, *The Vignelli Canon*
**Section processed:** Part One, "The Intangibles" — PDF spreads 5–17, twelve named sections.

## Why this section
Part One is the book's own division and is self-contained: twelve named values stated before any
technique appears. Part Two, "The Tangibles", begins at PDF spread 18 and covers paper sizes, grids
and typefaces — different material requiring a different treatment.

## Visual evidence — VERIFIED
`visual_completeness: verified_page_level`. All 13 spreads rendered ephemerally at 90 dpi and
inspected; nothing committed.

**Colour control.** Saturation was measured across all 13 spreads: colour present on 9 of them, up to
2,166 coloured pixels per 4,800 sampled. The identical measurement on the Albers digitisation
returned zero on every page. The check distinguishes a colour-bearing source from a stripped one, and
it is worth keeping as a routine step.

## The finding this book contributed
**A full-page graphic buried an entire named section in the text layer.** Part One has twelve named
sections; eleven extract cleanly. *Syntactics* — one of the three concepts the book opens by
announcing — shares its spread with the New York City Subway Diagram, whose several hundred words of
route legend are emitted around and through the prose. In the extracted text the section reads as a
wall of subway routes.

It was caught because the contents page lists a section the body text did not appear to contain, and
recovered in full by rendering the spread.

This is **not corruption** — every word is present and correct. It is an ordering failure, the
mildest member of the family that blocked Lupton outright. The difference in consequence is large:
Lupton spliced sentences into text the author never wrote, which is unrecoverable; this merely
misplaces a block, which a visual pass repairs completely. A contents page turns out to be a cheap
independent check on whether extraction has lost a section.
