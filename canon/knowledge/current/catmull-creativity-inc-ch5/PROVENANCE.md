**Book:** Ed Catmull with Amy Wallace, *Creativity, Inc.: Overcoming the Unseen Forces That Stand
in the Way of True Inspiration*, Random House, 2014.
**Section processed:** chapter 5, "Honesty and Candor", complete.

## Why this section
It is where the book states its central operating mechanism — the Braintrust — and does the most
reasoning in one place: it poses the problem (why capable people withhold criticism), defines the
mechanism, gives its preconditions and its failure modes, defines its unit of feedback ("a good
note"), shows a session running, and closes by generalising the mechanism for a reader who does not
work there. It is self-contained; nothing in it depends on an earlier chapter beyond a one-sentence
recap of how storyboards become reels.

## What this book contributed
**A chapter that describes a repeating process — a loop with a cadence, an artefact, an ordering
and stated failure conditions — entirely in prose, with no diagram, table or figure anywhere.**
The visual-design books in this batch lose evidence when pictures are stripped. This source class
has the opposite problem: there was never any structure outside the sentences, so whatever
structure the process has must be reconstructed by the reader, and two readers could reasonably
reconstruct it differently. That is why both SourceConceptSystems here carry
`extractor_synthesis` on their whole-system claims.
