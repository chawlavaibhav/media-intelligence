# Provenance record — Master Shots, frame material and chapter 8 (CANON-003 book 11, rebalance lane)

**Book:** Christopher Kenworthy, *Master Shots: 100 Advanced Camera Techniques to Get an Expensive
Look on Your Low Budget Movie*, volume 1, second edition. Michael Wiese Productions, ISBN
9781615930876. The introduction is signed Perth, June 2011.
**Section processed:** the book's frame — Introduction, "How to Use this Book", "About the Images"
and the Conclusion — together with chapter 8, "Directing Attention", complete: techniques 8.1
Object Guide, 8.2 Handing Off Motion, 8.3 Change of Direction, 8.4 Reflections, 8.5 Rest Point,
8.6 Color Guides, 8.7 Reverse Angles. About 4,200 words.

## Why this section
This book is a hundred numbered recipes. A chapter alone would expose the recipe *form* and not the
reasoning that governs it: what the recipes are for, how the author intends them to be read, what
evidential status the pictures carry, and what he claims knowing a hundred of them does to a
director. All of that is in the frame material — and the frame material alone would expose the
reasoning with no instance of it. The two together are one coherent section, and the front matter
turns out to be load-bearing for the visual pass as well: the meaning of the diagrams' arrows and
the deliberate difference between the renderings and the film frames are both stated there and
nowhere else.

## Visual evidence — verified at figure level, with one loss the extraction could not repair
`visual_completeness: verified_figure_level`. Nothing committed; all inspection ephemeral.

**This source states its own visual dependence**, which makes the pass unusually well specified. Its
front matter tells the reader to reconstruct each technique from the frame grabs and the overhead
diagrams *first*, and says practising that reconstruction matters more than watching the films the
examples come from. It then defines the notation: white arrows for camera movement, black arrows for
actor movement.

**The notation works, and it works because it is tonal rather than chromatic.** The overhead diagram
for technique 8.2 shows two black arrows crossing — the two actors — and one white arrow leading
away from the camera icon, which is the backward dolly the text describes. White against black
survives greyscale reproduction, low resolution, and a reader with no colour vision.

**Which matters, because every image in this book is greyscale.** Colour saturation was measured
across all 124 images at roughly 2,000 samples each: **zero coloured pixels in any of them.**

**And technique 8.6 is called Color Guides.** Its argument is that a character in a strikingly
coloured orange jacket can be identified while blurred and distant, because the rest of the frame is
graded almost entirely to blue. Its illustration is greyscale, and in it the background figure is a
small grey smudge on a grey beach — which is the outcome the technique says colour prevents. **The
demonstration does not merely fail to support the claim; it shows the opposite.**

INFERRED, not verified: this is the printed book's own condition rather than a digitisation
artefact. Three things point that way — the arrow notation distinguishes camera from actor by white
against black, a choice only a monochrome book needs to make; "About the Images" describes the frame
grabs as enhanced "to greatly improve their clarity when printed" and never mentions colour; and the
greyscale is uniform across all 124 images rather than patchy. Settling it would need a physical
copy, which this task does not permit acquiring.

The corresponding object carries `extraction_uncertainty: figure_not_inspected` in the sense SPEC-03
intends — the figure that would support the claim does not exist in an inspectable form — and its
`inspected.figures` entry records what was found instead.

## What this book contributed
**A source whose figures are load-bearing by the author's own instruction, and which contains one
technique its own production cannot illustrate.** Nothing in the text layer, the file, or the image
is anomalous: the image is a legitimate greyscale photograph of a real frame. The loss is visible
only in the relation between what the section claims and what its illustration can show, and only to
someone reading both. No mechanical check would find it.
