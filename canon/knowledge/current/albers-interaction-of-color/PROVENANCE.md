**Book:** Josef Albers, *Interaction of Color* · **Publisher:** Yale University Press
**Section processed:** Chapters I–V (PDF pages 29–43). Chapter VI begins at PDF page 44.

## Why this section
Chapters I–V are one continuous derivation, not five topics. Colour cannot be remembered or named
(I), is never seen alone and takes its meaning from context (II), therefore the study works in paper
rather than paint (III), therefore the founding exercise is to make one colour look like two (IV),
and the hardest judgement in that work is relative lightness across hues (V). The span exposes
Albers's whole reasoning system from premise to classroom method.

## Visual evidence — BLOCKED, for a new reason
`visual_completeness: blocked_visual_validation`

Nothing here was unreachable. Every page rendered, every figure is present, legible and correctly
positioned. **The digitisation is entirely greyscale, and the book's subject is colour.**

Measured: saturation across all fifteen rendered pages of the span, plus a six-page sample from later
in the book, found **zero colour pixels out of 4,800 sampled per page, on every page tested.** Not
muted — absent.

The PDF title promises "selected plates", and plates are indeed present. They are reproduced without
colour, so the promise is technically met and practically void.

**Consequence for this extraction.** Chapter IV's central exercise — two identical pieces on two
different grounds, made to look unlike each other — cannot be inspected at all. Those claims are
extracted from text alone and carry that caveat explicitly. One demonstration survived intact:
chapter II's four typographic settings of the book's own title, which is about letterform rather than
colour.
