# Provenance record — Art & Fear, printed pp.1–21 (CANON-003 book 17, Lane D)

**Book:** David Bayles & Ted Orland, *Art & Fear: Observations on the Perils (and Rewards) of
Artmaking*, 1993. Image Continuum Press printing (the copyright page lists twelve Capra Press
printings 1994–2000 and printings through 2010).
**Section processed:** Part I, chapter I "The Nature of the Problem" (printed pp.1–8, including the
book's own section "A Few Assumptions") and chapter II "Art and Fear" (printed pp.9–21, including
its subsections Vision & Execution, Imagination, Materials and Uncertainty).

## Why this section
These two chapters are where the book states its premises and builds its argument; every later
chapter applies them to one named fear. The span is coherent by the book's own division, and it
contains the only procedure in the section — the two-step "Operating Manual for Not Quitting" —
which makes the reasoning system visible rather than only the conclusions.

## The finding this book contributed
**A source that states its own foundations as chosen assumptions rather than as findings, and says
why it chose them.** The book rejects the talent view of art with the sentence that it is
fatalistic *even if it is true*, and then adopts four premises under the heading "A Few
Assumptions" because those premises place responsibility in the maker's hands.

That is a kind of claim the Canon has not met before. A finding can be contradicted by evidence; a
premise adopted for its effect on the person holding it cannot be, and treating the two alike would
misread the source in both directions. The SPEC-03 fields record it — `caveats` with
`origin: source_stated`, and a system whose whole-system claim is `source_explicit` — but nothing
in the schema marks the category itself. Recorded in the Lane D issue file as D-07.
