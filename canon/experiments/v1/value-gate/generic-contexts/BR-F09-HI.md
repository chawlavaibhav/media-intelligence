# Craft context

## Filming a physical exchange
An object passing from one person to another is a small action that has to read clearly. The
hands and the object should be framed so the moment of transfer is visible, and the object should
remain the same object throughout — same size, same condition, held plausibly. Contact between
hands and object is what makes the action believable; ambiguity about who is holding what breaks
it immediately.

## Surfaces and their appearance
Different materials behave differently under light. Fabric scatters light softly and shows its
weave in shallow relief. Printed or laminated surfaces produce more directional highlights, and a
reflection falling across printed matter will obscure it. Where a uniform or a package carries
branding, that branding has to stay legible through the action, which constrains both the angle
and where the light comes from.

## Consistency across the sequence
Across a short sequence the same elements must hold: the same people, the same clothing, the same
object, the same location and consistent light. Sound should also carry across cuts so the
sequence feels like one continuous moment rather than assembled fragments.

## Copy that must appear exactly
Where text is supplied as required copy, it is fixed content. It must be reproduced exactly as
given, and any mark or accent within it is part of the word rather than decoration. Text should
be checked character by character against the source rather than by eye, because a single altered
character can change a word's meaning entirely while still looking plausible.

## Meaning before styling
Establish what a piece is actually saying before deciding how it should look. Where a line has a
specific sense, the design has to preserve that sense; treating supplied words as a shape to be
arranged is how meaning gets lost.

## Register
A brief asking for something simple and real is asking for ordinary settings, natural light and
unforced performance. Everyday credibility does more for a service business than polish does.

## Timing a small action
A brief physical action needs a moment before and after it to read clearly — the approach, the
transfer, the acknowledgement. Cutting into the middle of the action and out again leaves the viewer
assembling what happened rather than watching it.

## Framing at a doorway
Doorways constrain where a camera can be placed and often produce a strong contrast between interior
and exterior light. Deciding which side of that contrast the important detail sits on prevents faces
or branding from being lost in shadow or blown out.

## Before finishing
Confirm the transfer reads clearly, the branding stays legible, and all required text is exactly
as supplied.
