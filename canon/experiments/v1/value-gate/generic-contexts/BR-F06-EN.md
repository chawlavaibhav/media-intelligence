# Craft context

## Keeping a sequence coherent
A sequence of shots reads as one continuous event only if the viewer can place each new shot in
relation to the last. The main things that must hold are the direction a subject faces or moves,
the position of things within the space, the appearance of the subject, and the continuity of
sound. When one of these breaks, viewers may not identify what went wrong, but they feel that
something did.

## Screen direction
If a subject moves left to right in one shot and right to left in the next, they read as having
turned around. Keeping the camera consistently on one side of the line of action preserves this.
Where direction genuinely must reverse, a deliberate transitional shot can carry the change
without confusion.

## Changing shot size and angle
Cutting between two shots that are too similar produces a visible jump. Cutting between shots
that differ clearly in angle and in size reads as a deliberate change of viewpoint. As a
practical rule, change both at once rather than only one.

## Consistency of the subject
Across a sequence the same person must remain recognisably the same person: the same face, the
same clothing, the same details. This sounds obvious and is one of the most common failures in
assembled sequences, because each shot is judged on its own before the sequence is watched as a
whole. Check it by watching the shots in order rather than by reviewing them individually.

## Telling a story without dialogue
Without speech, the sequence carries meaning through action, order and consequence. Each shot
should advance a simple chain — a situation, a change, a result — and the viewer should be able
to state what happened. Ambient sound and music set the emotional register but cannot supply
information that the images do not contain.

## Register
A brief asking for something that feels real rather than styled is asking for restraint:
available light, ordinary settings, unposed action, imperfection left in. Polished lighting and
posed framing will read as advertising however natural the performance.

## Light as a continuity element
Light direction, quality and colour are part of what makes a sequence read as one continuous moment.
Shots recorded under obviously different conditions will not cut together even when everything else
matches. Where a sequence is meant to occur over a short period, the light should be consistent with
that period.

## Rhythm without dialogue
With no speech to structure the piece, shot duration carries the rhythm. Holding a shot slightly
longer than feels necessary often allows a moment to register, where cutting on the beat of the music
throughout produces something that feels like a montage rather than an observed event.

## Product visibility in a wearable category
Where the product is worn rather than held, it needs at least one shot that shows it clearly enough
to be recognised, and that shot has to be motivated by the action rather than inserted. A moment
where the subject naturally interacts with the product is more effective than a detached insert.

## Before finishing
Watch the sequence in order and confirm the subject stays consistent, the geography holds, and
the intended action is understandable without any words.
