# Craft context

## A person speaking to camera
When someone addresses the viewer directly, credibility comes from how they speak rather than
from production polish. Natural delivery, ordinary surroundings and unforced framing support
belief. Over-lit, over-composed treatments push the same words toward reading as a performance.

## Testimony and evidence
A personal account carries weight because it is specific and because it is someone's own. Claims
that are large and general invite doubt in a way that specific, bounded claims do not — a stated
circumstance, a particular result, a named condition. Where a claim concerns a real outcome, it
should be one the business can actually support, and the account should be genuine.

## The salesperson test
A useful check for any commercial script is whether a person selling face to face would actually
say those words to this customer. Language that would sound absurd in person usually reads as
advertising noise on screen. This test tends to remove superlatives and slogans and leaves
concrete reasons.

## Framing and background
A medium shot that includes the hands allows a speaker to hold and show a product naturally. The
background should establish context without competing — a real working environment usually does
more for credibility than a neutral backdrop, provided it is not busy behind the speaker's head.

## Showing the product with the speaker
If the product must be identified, it needs a moment where it is held still and clearly visible.
A product waved while someone talks will not be recognised. Its packaging should be the current
one, and legible at viewing size.

## Sound
Speech intelligibility matters more than any other audio consideration. Location sound with
background noise is acceptable and often desirable for authenticity, so long as every word is
clear. Music, if present, should sit well under the voice.

## Length and structure
A twenty-second piece supports roughly one claim and one closing line. Trying to fit several
claims produces a rushed delivery and a viewer who retains none of them.

## Location and its meaning
Where a speaker stands is itself an argument. A person filmed in the environment where they actually
use the product carries more credibility than the same person against a neutral background, because
the setting corroborates what they say. Any location should be checked for distracting elements
behind the speaker before recording.

## Pacing of speech
A single speaker delivering one claim needs time for the words to land. Rushing delivery to fit more
in reduces both comprehension and believability. If the script does not fit comfortably in the
available time, the script should be shortened rather than the delivery accelerated.

## Text supporting speech
On-screen text that repeats or reinforces a spoken point helps retention, particularly where a
viewer may be watching without sound. It should agree exactly with what is said; a mismatch between
spoken and written versions of the same claim is noticed and undermines both.

## Before finishing
Confirm the words are understandable, the product is identifiable, and the speaker reads as a
real person rather than a presenter.
