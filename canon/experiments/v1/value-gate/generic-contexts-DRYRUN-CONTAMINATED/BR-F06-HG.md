# Craft context

## Telling a complete story in very little time
A short piece that must show a situation, an action and a result has roughly one beat per few
seconds. That means each beat has to be immediately legible: a single clear action, framed so the
important thing is obvious without searching. Anything requiring interpretation will be missed.

## Reducing what the viewer must process
The less work a viewer has to do, the more of the message survives. Familiar situations, familiar
objects and conventional framing all reduce that work. Novelty is expensive in a short piece,
because a viewer spends their first moments working out what they are looking at rather than
following what is happening.

## Structure
A reliable structure for a short commercial piece is a problem the audience recognises, an action
that addresses it, and a visible result. Naming what the audience actually wants — and what is
getting in the way — usually matters more than describing the product's features. The result
should be shown rather than claimed.

## Choosing what to leave out
When a brief contains more than the duration can hold, the answer is subtraction rather than
compression. Speeding everything up to fit produces a piece where nothing registers. Decide what
the single necessary message is, keep it, and remove the rest, saying explicitly what was removed
so the decision can be reviewed.

## Making the beats read
Each transition should be motivated by the action, so the viewer follows the chain rather than
noticing the edits. Where a device or interface appears, it needs enough time to be recognised at
viewing size, which in practice means longer than it feels while editing.

## Ending on the action
Where the purpose is to prompt a specific behaviour, the closing frames should carry that clearly
and hold long enough to be read. A service promise expressed as a specific number is easier to
remember than a general claim of speed.

## Legibility of screens and interfaces
Where a device screen carries part of the story, it must be readable at viewing size, which usually
means showing a simplified version of what is on it rather than the full interface. A screen the
viewer cannot read contributes nothing except the general idea that a phone is being used.

## Continuity through a compressed sequence
Even a very short sequence has to hold together: the same person, the same setting, the same objects
in the same state. Compressed timelines make this harder rather than easier, because each shot has
less time to re-establish context for the viewer.

## Sound in a sound-off environment
Much short-form video is watched without sound. Anything essential to understanding should be
carried visually or by on-screen text, with audio treated as reinforcement rather than as the primary
channel.

## Before finishing
Count the beats against the duration and confirm each one can be understood on a single viewing
with the sound off.
