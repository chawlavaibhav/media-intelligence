# Craft context

## Working from an existing campaign
When a client asks for something consistent with work they already have, consistency is the
requirement and not a preference. Before designing anything, establish what actually makes the
existing work recognisable. It is rarely everything: usually two or three specific properties do
the work — a palette, a type treatment, a compositional habit, a particular quality of light —
while the rest varies freely. Identify those load-bearing properties explicitly, because copying
everything produces a repeat rather than a companion piece.

## Asking rather than assuming
If the reference is named but not supplied or described, that is a gap in the brief and should be
raised. Inventing a house style and describing it as consistent is the common failure here, and
it will not survive contact with the client's actual materials. It is legitimate and useful for a
plan to state exactly what it needs to see.

## What should change and what should not
A seasonal or campaign variation changes subject, mood and content while holding identity
constant. Being clear about which axis is moving prevents a variation from reading as a rebrand.
A useful test: if the new piece appeared beside the old one, would a customer recognise them as
the same voice?

## Recognition takes time to build
Visual identity accumulates value through repetition. Changing it for the sake of freshness
discards that accumulated recognition, which is expensive and slow to rebuild. When something is
already working and recognised, the burden of proof sits with any proposal to change it.

## Using supplied photography
Client-supplied images constrain what is possible, and those constraints should be established
early rather than discovered late. Consider what the existing images actually show, whether they
are consistent with each other, and whether the required treatment can be applied to them.

## Minimal copy
Where a single line carries the whole message, it needs enough space and contrast to be read
without effort, and it should not compete with a busy area of the image.

## Before finishing
Confirm the piece would be recognised as belonging to the existing campaign, and that any
assumption made about the reference has been stated rather than buried.
