# Canon context

## regularity is the value and the danger
_Source: samara-making-breaking-grid-ch1 · scs_sam_c003_004_

The set of claims in which the property that makes a grid useful — regularity — is also identified as the thing that must be worked against: articulation as the real work, leaving the structure partly unrevealed, the flat-versus-deep consequence of how far alignments are marked, the statement that succumbing to regularity is the greatest danger, the variation tactics, the licensing of violation, pacing across a sequence, and the tension named directly in the campaign section.

The source builds an argument for structure across roughly forty pages and then states that structure alone produces failure. The resolution offered is that the grid is a guide rather than a rule, that a system exists to encourage flexibility, and that responsibility sits with the designer — "Grids don't make dull layouts, designers do." The test for a legitimate deviation is never given beyond "a good reason".

## the three aspects as a sequential test
_Source: vignelli-canon-intangibles · scs_vig_c003_001_

Semantics, syntactics and pragmatics are presented as three aspects to be examined one at a time, and the source treats them as successive conditions: find the meaning, make the elements consistent with one another, and check that the result is understood. Failing the third voids the first two.

Work can be semantically correct and syntactically consistent and still be wasted effort, if at the point of fruition no one understands it. The three aspects are therefore cumulative conditions rather than parallel concerns, and comprehension is the one that can void the others.

## specificity as a credibility mechanism
_Source: hopkins-scientific-advertising-ch1-7 · scs_hop_sa_005_

Chapter 7's chain: readers grant selling talk a licence, so generalities and superlatives carry no weight and also discredit everything beside them; a specific claim removes the licence because it is either true or a lie and the advertiser is assumed not to be able to lie; a definite figure additionally makes the reader infer that tests were made; therefore stating an ordinary shared attribute in specific detail claims it for whoever states it first; and none of this costs additional space.

Belief in an advertising claim is produced by its checkability rather than by its content, so the same argument gains or loses most of its force according to how definitely it is stated.

## the framework is used as a filter that removes material
_Source: miller-storybrand-sb7 · sk_sb_c003_0005_

The framework's primary use, as the source describes it, is subtractive: it is a filter that decides what to cut. A tagline sounding good or an image catching the eye is not a reason to keep it if it does not serve the customer's story. The source borrows the test from film editing, where whole characters are cut when they do not serve the plot.
