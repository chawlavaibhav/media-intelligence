# Canon context

## the sb7 framework
_Source: miller-storybrand-sb7 · scs_sb_c003_002_

The seven elements in the source's own order — a character who wants something, a problem, a guide, a plan, a call to action, a failure avoided, a success — each stated as a numbered principle with its own heading, its own icon, and its own field on the output artefact.

The source states the sequence as one sentence and treats it as the necessary structure of a compelling story, transferred whole to a brand message. Two qualifications are the source's own and matter for anything applying it: the elements are not to be used evenly, with the failure element explicitly dosed like salt; and the diagram places success and failure as a fork from a single junction rather than as consecutive steps, so the last two elements are alternative outcomes of the same decision.

## three independent justifications for cutting
_Source: murch-blink-p1-25 · scs_murch_c003_003_

Three separate arguments for why films are cut: a practical one (assembling everything simultaneously is logistically very difficult, and long takes compound the risk of failure), a selective one (discontinuity lets the best angle be chosen for each emotion and story point), and an intrinsic one (cutting is a positive influence in and for itself, by the force of its paradoxical suddenness).

The three arguments are cumulative and deliberately ordered from weakest to strongest, and the source signals the escalation — "beyond even these considerations" — before stating the third. The point of the sequence is that the intrinsic argument survives the removal of the other two: "We would want to cut even if discontinuity were not of such great practical value." That sentence is the load-bearing one, and it converts cutting from a workaround into a medium.

## the success checklist
_Source: heath-made-to-stick-introduction · scs_hea_mts_001_

Six principles — Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Story — each stated as the answer to one question about the audience, presented together as a checklist to run against a specific idea, and explicitly qualified as a common set of traits rather than a formula.

Sticky ideas draw from a common set of six traits which make them more likely to succeed. There is no formula: you do not need all of them to succeed and having all of them does not guarantee success. Used as a checklist against an idea you already have, they show you which of the six it fails.

## processing cost causes the audience to disengage
_Source: miller-storybrand-sb7 · sk_sb_c003_0003_

The second stated cause. Understanding a message costs effort, and effort works against the same survival function that governs attention, so when a message demands too much processing the audience tunes out the source of it to conserve. The source's image is that a customer has to run on a treadmill for as long as you are talking.
