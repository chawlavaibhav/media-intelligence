# Canon context

## the salesmanship test as a decision procedure
_Source: hopkins-scientific-advertising-ch1-7 · scs_hop_sa_002_

A single question — would this help a salesman sell the goods, or help me sell them face to face with the buyer — offered as the way to settle any advertising question, together with the answers the source derives from it: advertising exists only to sell, its errors are multiplied, literary style and visible selling effort create resistance, imposed brevity is wrong, enlarged type is waste, write to one typical buyer, learn the buyer's reactions in person, never write to please the seller, offer service rather than ask for the order, and take the order by removing the buyer's risk. Chapter 5 then names the one place the analogy does not hold — the salesman cannot be ignored and the advertisement can — and treats the headline as the repair.

Almost every question about how an advertisement should be written can be answered by asking what a good salesman would do in front of one buyer; the answers the book gives are that derivation, not separate findings.

## specificity as a credibility mechanism
_Source: hopkins-scientific-advertising-ch1-7 · scs_hop_sa_005_

Chapter 7's chain: readers grant selling talk a licence, so generalities and superlatives carry no weight and also discredit everything beside them; a specific claim removes the licence because it is either true or a lie and the advertiser is assumed not to be able to lie; a definite figure additionally makes the reader infer that tests were made; therefore stating an ordinary shared attribute in specific detail claims it for whoever states it first; and none of this costs additional space.

Belief in an advertising claim is produced by its checkability rather than by its content, so the same argument gains or loses most of its force according to how definitely it is stated.

## the measurement argument
_Source: ogilvy-ch2-advertising-that-sells · scs_ogl_c003_002_

One argument recurs through the chapter in five places: nobody in the chain is paid to find out whether advertising works, so it is not measured; because it is not measured, the field does not accumulate knowledge; because it does not accumulate knowledge, agencies repeat known mistakes; and the remedy is to imitate the one branch of the trade that does close the loop.

The chapter's criticisms of the industry are one causal chain, not separate complaints. Absent measurement is the root; uncodified experience, repeated mistakes, committee process and the cult of creativity are its consequences; direct response is the proposed corrective because it alone attributes sales to advertisements.

## stickiness is a property of design not of subject matter
_Source: heath-made-to-stick-introduction · sk_hea_mts_0002_

Whether an idea sticks is a property of how it is built, not of how inherently interesting its subject is. The source poses the question directly — are ideas born interesting or made interesting — and answers that this is a nurture book. It concedes the basketball analogy breaks down at exactly this point: unlike players, ideas can be engineered.
