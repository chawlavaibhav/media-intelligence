# Canon context

## the rule of six
_Source: murch-blink-p1-25 · scs_murch_c003_001_

Six criteria for an ideal cut, numbered by the source and ranked: emotion, story, rhythm, eye-trace, planarity, three-dimensional continuity. The source attaches a percentage to each (51, 23, 10, 7, 5, 4), states a procedure for when they conflict — sacrifice upward from the bottom — and gives two claims about how the ranks relate to each other: that higher criteria obscure failures of lower ones but not the reverse, and that the top three are bound far more tightly to each other than the lower three are.

The six are a single ordered instrument, not six separate pieces of advice. The order is what makes it usable: it tells an editor what to give up first when a cut cannot satisfy everything, and the source states it in that form — "What I'm suggesting is a list of priorities." The ordering is also claimed to be perceptual and not merely preferential: satisfying a higher item tends to obscure problems with lower items, and not the other way round. And the source ranks three-dimensional spatial continuity LAST, deliberately reversing what it says film schools teach first and what early sound film treated as the rule.

## rules held defeasible by creative intent
_Source: grammar-of-the-shot-ch4 · scs_gos_c003_003_

The chapter states its rules and then states, repeatedly, that they are not absolutes. Every named guideline carries an explicit permission to depart from it for a creative reason, and the hardest rule in the chapter is given a worked exception.

The guidelines in this chapter are defaults, not constraints. The authors state directly that there are very few absolutes among them and that a creative reason is sufficient warrant to depart from any of them. The exception is not a footnote to the rules; it is stated as their governing condition.

## the sb7 framework
_Source: miller-storybrand-sb7 · scs_sb_c003_002_

The seven elements in the source's own order — a character who wants something, a problem, a guide, a plan, a call to action, a failure avoided, a success — each stated as a numbered principle with its own heading, its own icon, and its own field on the output artefact.

The source states the sequence as one sentence and treats it as the necessary structure of a compelling story, transferred whole to a brand message. Two qualifications are the source's own and matter for anything applying it: the elements are not to be used evenly, with the failure element explicitly dosed like salt; and the diagram places success and failure as a fork from a single junction rather than as consecutive steps, so the last two elements are alternative outcomes of the same decision.

## simplicity is finding the essential core by exclusion
_Source: heath-made-to-stick-introduction · sk_hea_mts_0009_

The first principle answers the question of how to find the essential core of an idea. It requires mastery of exclusion and relentless prioritisation, and the source is explicit that brevity is not the goal: saying something short is not the mission, sound bites are not the ideal, proverbs are. The target is an idea that is simple and profound at once.
