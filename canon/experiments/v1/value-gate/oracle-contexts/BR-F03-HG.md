## image as the thing actually bought
_Source: ogilvy-ch2-advertising-that-sells · scs_ogl_c003_003_

A connected account of why image rather than product is what advertising sells: products in a category are often materially alike, consumers experience the name's associations as properties of the thing, so image becomes the differentiator, is built by consistency over decades, and defaults to quality for products consumed in public.

Where products are alike, what the consumer buys is the image, and the image is the advertiser's actual product. It is built only by projecting the same personality year after year, it defaults to an impression of quality for anything consumed visibly, and for parity products it is the only remaining ground of difference.

## psycho logic as a second operating system
_Source: sutherland-alchemy-introduction · scs_sut_alc_002_

The source's model of how people actually decide. A parallel, largely unconscious system evolved to be useful rather than optimal; its outputs look irrational only because we judge the motive wrongly, which is what the nonsense / non-sense distinction separates; we cannot report its reasons because self-deception is what makes deceiving others convincing; feelings therefore arrive without explanations; context determines the reading; and logic's certainty is bought by narrowing its inputs, which is exactly what makes it fail outside its frame.

The world runs on two operating systems. One achieves an unambiguously right answer by limiting the data points it considers, and is hopelessly wrong outside that frame. The other considers far more and reaches a not-perfect-but-rarely-stupid conclusion. Neither is the correct one; which deserves credibility depends on the context.

## why marketing fails and what the remedy has to do
_Source: miller-storybrand-sb7 · scs_sb_c003_001_

The argument underneath the framework: attention is allocated by survival relevance, processing a message costs effort that works against survival, so a message that is either irrelevant to survival or expensive to process is discarded. Story is offered as the remedy because it arrives pre-ordered; the framework is the operational form of story; the framework is used subtractively as a filter; and two tests — three questions for a narrative, three answers in five seconds for a page — decide whether the result works.

The source treats message clarity as a competitive position rather than a matter of presentation, and the two named mistakes are the load-bearing premises: a better product loses to a clearer one because attention is allocated before quality is assessed. Everything the framework does follows from those two claims, so the framework's authority rests entirely on them.

## the curse of knowledge and the only available escape
_Source: heath-made-to-stick-introduction · scs_hea_mts_002_

The source's explanation of why a set of near-commonsense principles is not already in general use, and of why the remedy has to take the form it does. Existing communication advice leaves the message undesigned; the Curse of Knowledge makes an expert unable to see what a listener is missing; the Curse cannot be reversed; therefore the only remaining variable is the form of the idea, and the checklist is the instrument for changing it.

Knowing something makes it impossible to imagine not knowing it, and that tendency — not lack of skill or effort — is what consistently defeats good communication. Because it cannot be unlearned, the only reliable remedy is to transform the idea, and the six principles are the instrument for that transformation.
