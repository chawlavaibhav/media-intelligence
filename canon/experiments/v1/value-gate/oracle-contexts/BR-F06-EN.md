# Canon context

## continuity geometry of the action line
_Source: grammar-of-the-shot-ch4 · scs_gos_c003_001_

A single geometric construction generates most of this chapter's rules. Attention between subjects creates a sight line; tracing that sight line fixes the action line; the action line cuts the circle of possible camera positions into two halves; staying in one half preserves screen direction; crossing it reverses direction; and reciprocal coverage positions are computed by mirroring across the same line. The rules are consequences of one plan drawing, not a list.

Screen direction, the 180 degree rule, jumping the line, the 30 degree rule and reciprocating imagery are all derived from a single imaginary line established by the talent's own sight line. Once that line is fixed, the permitted camera positions, the mirrored positions for matched coverage and the failure mode of crossing it all follow geometrically.

## defects with no within frame signature
_Source: grammar-of-the-shot-ch4 · scs_gos_c003_002_

Several of this chapter's failures share an unusual property: each individual frame is correct, and the fault exists only in the relation between frames. A crossed line, a jump cut, a mismatched over-the-shoulder pair and a broken eye-line are all invisible in any single image.

A distinct class of defect in this chapter cannot be detected by inspecting one frame, because no frame contains it. The defect is a property of a pair or sequence of shots. Any process that examines frames independently will report these scenes as clean.

## the four continuities
_Source: grammar-of-the-edit-ch3-5 · scs_gote_c003_003_

Continuity is divided by the source into four kinds that must each be addressed separately: content, movement, position and sound. Each has its own failure and, for three of the four, its own remedy.

The four are treated as one responsibility with four independent failure surfaces. A transition can satisfy any three and fail the fourth, and each failure looks different to the viewer — a mismatched object, a reversed direction of travel, a subject that jumps across the screen, sound that vanishes. Two of the four, movement and position, share a single underlying cause in the geometry of the action line, and the source treats them separately in Chapter Three before supplying the shared cause in Chapter Five.

## seven ways to move attention without a cut
_Source: kenworthy-master-shots-ch8 · scs_ms_c003_002_

Chapter 8 read as a set of alternatives to cutting: motivate a move with an object in transit; hand the scene from one subject to another with a retreating camera; carry the move on an actor's differential speed across the lens; put subject and reaction in one frame with a reflection; drive the camera to a predetermined close-up regardless of the action; identify a background figure by an established colour; or move nobody but the actors in a static wide master.

The seven are alternative answers to one question — how to redirect attention without a cut — and they differ in what carries the redirection: an object, the camera's retreat, an actor's speed, a reflective surface, the camera's indifference, a colour, or blocking. Two of the seven use no camera movement at all, which is why the set is better described by what redirects attention than by what the camera does.
