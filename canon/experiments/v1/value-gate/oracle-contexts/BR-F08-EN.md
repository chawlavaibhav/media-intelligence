# Canon context

## the six element test for a transition
_Source: grammar-of-the-edit-ch3-5 · scs_gote_c003_001_

Six named factors — information, motivation, shot composition, camera angle, continuity and sound — which the source introduces together as the criteria for whether a transition is a good edit, applies one at a time to every transition and edit type in the following chapter, and finally restates in negative form as the six reasons two shots cannot be joined at all.

The six factors are a single test, not six separate pieces of advice. The source's own closing move makes this explicit: when two shots will not cut together, the reason is that one of exactly these six is wrong, and no change of transition type will repair it. The framework is therefore both the standard for a good edit and the diagnostic for a bad one, and the source states there is very little an editor can do once one of the six has failed.

## the four continuities
_Source: grammar-of-the-edit-ch3-5 · scs_gote_c003_003_

Continuity is divided by the source into four kinds that must each be addressed separately: content, movement, position and sound. Each has its own failure and, for three of the four, its own remedy.

The four are treated as one responsibility with four independent failure surfaces. A transition can satisfy any three and fail the fourth, and each failure looks different to the viewer — a mismatched object, a reversed direction of travel, a subject that jumps across the screen, sound that vanishes. Two of the four, movement and position, share a single underlying cause in the geometry of the action line, and the source treats them separately in Chapter Three before supplying the shared cause in Chapter Five.

## psycho logic as a second operating system
_Source: sutherland-alchemy-introduction · scs_sut_alc_002_

The source's model of how people actually decide. A parallel, largely unconscious system evolved to be useful rather than optimal; its outputs look irrational only because we judge the motive wrongly, which is what the nonsense / non-sense distinction separates; we cannot report its reasons because self-deception is what makes deceiving others convincing; feelings therefore arrive without explanations; context determines the reading; and logic's certainty is bought by narrowing its inputs, which is exactly what makes it fail outside its frame.

The world runs on two operating systems. One achieves an unambiguously right answer by limiting the data points it considers, and is hopelessly wrong outside that frame. The other considers far more and reaches a not-perfect-but-rarely-stupid conclusion. Neither is the correct one; which deserves credibility depends on the context.

## the sb7 framework
_Source: miller-storybrand-sb7 · scs_sb_c003_002_

The seven elements in the source's own order — a character who wants something, a problem, a guide, a plan, a call to action, a failure avoided, a success — each stated as a numbered principle with its own heading, its own icon, and its own field on the output artefact.

The source states the sequence as one sentence and treats it as the necessary structure of a compelling story, transferred whole to a brand message. Two qualifications are the source's own and matter for anything applying it: the elements are not to be used evenly, with the failure element explicitly dosed like salt; and the diagram places success and failure as a fork from a single junction rather than as consecutive steps, so the last two elements are alternative outcomes of the same decision.
