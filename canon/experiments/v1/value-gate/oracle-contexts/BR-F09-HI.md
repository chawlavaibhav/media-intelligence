# Canon context

## the three reflection types as a contrast set
_Source: light-science-magic-ch3 · scs_lsm_c003_001_

Diffuse, direct and polarized direct reflection are defined against one another on the same four questions: does viewing angle matter, does source distance matter, does source size matter, and how bright is the result. Every real surface produces a mixture, and the mixture's proportions are what make one surface look unlike another.

The three reflection types form one contrast set, not three separate topics. Each is defined by how it answers the same questions, they behave oppositely on several of them, and no real surface exhibits one alone. What a photographer sees is always a proportion.

## the four continuities
_Source: grammar-of-the-edit-ch3-5 · scs_gote_c003_003_

Continuity is divided by the source into four kinds that must each be addressed separately: content, movement, position and sound. Each has its own failure and, for three of the four, its own remedy.

The four are treated as one responsibility with four independent failure surfaces. A transition can satisfy any three and fail the fourth, and each failure looks different to the viewer — a mismatched object, a reversed direction of travel, a subject that jumps across the screen, sound that vanishes. Two of the four, movement and position, share a single underlying cause in the geometry of the action line, and the source treats them separately in Chapter Three before supplying the shared cause in Chapter Five.

## specificity as a credibility mechanism
_Source: hopkins-scientific-advertising-ch1-7 · scs_hop_sa_005_

Chapter 7's chain: readers grant selling talk a licence, so generalities and superlatives carry no weight and also discredit everything beside them; a specific claim removes the licence because it is either true or a lie and the advertiser is assumed not to be able to lie; a definite figure additionally makes the reader infer that tests were made; therefore stating an ordinary shared attribute in specific detail claims it for whoever states it first; and none of this costs additional space.

Belief in an advertising claim is produced by its checkability rather than by its content, so the same argument gains or loses most of its force according to how definitely it is stated.

## begin by searching for the meaning of the subject
_Source: vignelli-canon-intangibles · sk_vig_c003_0002_

The first act on any new assignment, in any form of design, is to search for the meaning of the thing being designed. That search may begin with the history of the subject, and may extend to the company, the product, its market position, the competition, its destination, the final user, or the semantic roots of the subject itself. It provides the basis for a correct inception of a project, and it indicates the most appropriate form for that particular subject.
