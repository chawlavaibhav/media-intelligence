## 2. What the evidence says about each, in one paragraph

**A — human CD + LLM + tools.** OBSERVED: this is what actually produced the accepted film. Its failure mode is also observed: the human's taste arrives after seeing a film ("competent, not extraordinary"), the human reverses himself, and the human's time is the scarce input (break-even ≤ 1.7 h/job; observed 1–8 h). INFERENCE: A is the ceiling on quality and the floor on human attention; it is the right *fallback* and the wrong *product*.

**B — LLM + short checklist + tools.** OBSERVED (councils 14, 15): the strong model supplies the coarse ad spine unaided *when the brief says "ad"*; it does not reliably supply brand-early (0/3 B01 packages), and under a tutorial-shaped brief plus "proceed on the brief" it produced a tutorial. So B works if and only if the checklist *forces* the twelve intake decisions and the structural questions before spend, and something other than the author answers them. B's counterfactual on Cumin avoids the three structural rejections (the ones the human attributed to Canon) and none of the engineering ones. B without gates is case 002: fast and defective.

**C — current system.** OBSERVED: mechanically sound where it measures (ledger, exact text, geometry gates when imported, micro-qualification), inert or harmful where it does not: policy-blocked knowledge, self-attested doctrine, approvals at the wrong altitude, prose learning, 12.9:1 governance-to-craft ratio, 16 % of runtime LOC ever imported by a job. Its debuggability is real and should survive into whatever replaces it.

**D — queryable Canon/Q&A.** OBSERVED: the only controlled evidence (EVAL-037) is an unjudged tie at 2.5× cost with a 53 % HOLD diet; the corpus that matters for advertising is ~30 claims that fit in context without retrieval (Part 8 B1); the literature says near-miss chunks hurt. INFERENCE: D solves a problem the programme does not have (finding the rule) and adds one it does (crowding).

**E — specialist agents.** OBSERVED analogue: Media Factory's receptionist forced structure into every brief (a *required field*, not optional knowledge) and MI dropped it. Literature: separation helps only when the critic has different information, a trained objective, or a programmatic gate; a prompted persona of the same model with the same 8,035-word skill is predicted to add a 22nd "pass" (council 15 §4 rank 4; Part 8 E1–E5). INFERENCE: E is worth exactly two roles — an intent completer that fills the twelve decisions, and a fresh-context checker given only the blueprint and the checklist — plus deterministic QA. Not a five-agent pipeline; writes stay single-threaded.

**F — learned policy.** OBSERVED: nothing in the repo supports it — no labels, no qualified judge, no split, model-generated Q&A only. Literature supports narrow checklist-formatted judges (2B model + instance checklist ≈ GPT-4o) and warns against fine-tuning facts and training on self-generated data. INFERENCE: F is a later-stage option for one or two recurring judgements (tutorial-vs-ad; voice naturalness) after E6 produces reliable labels; not an architecture.

## 3. Cross-cutting findings the comparison surfaces (INFERENCE)

1. **Every architecture pays the same media bill.** Provider cost is 1–7 % of price in every case; the dimensions that separate the architectures are human attention, recurrence and context cost, not CpAO.
2. **The one variable that changed outcomes in the observed record is *when* and *at what altitude* a challenge arrived** — not which knowledge store was attached. 001 converged when the human dictated structure; 003 failed when nobody challenged the blueprint; 002's defects were all single-frame gate classes with no gate on the path.
3. **B and E converge.** B with a non-author answering the checklist *is* the minimal E. The literature's positive results (CoVe factored verification, TICK, checklists at pause points) are all instances of that convergence.
4. **C's kept subset is an ingredient of every other architecture**: ledger, gates, exact text, micro-qualification, verbatim verdicts. The comparison is not "C vs B"; it is "C's process mass and knowledge policy vs a checklist-and-challenge layer over C's tools".
5. **D and F are not competitors today**; they are options gated on evidence that does not exist (E2/E3 for D; E6 + labels for F).

## 4. What would change the matrix (for Part 14)

- If E1/E3 show that oracle compiled Canon beats a rejection-derived checklist by unanimity on the 9 briefs, D and a checklist-form compiled pack move up on Quality and C's Canon investment is vindicated in form.
- If E4/E5 show that a fresh-context reviewer and gated re-assembly recover ≥ 50 % of what the producer self-passed, E's two-role form moves to ++ on Failure recurrence and Human attention.
- If E7 shows strong + oracle ≫ weak + oracle while strong + nothing ≈ strong + oracle, model strength dominates and the Lab's *reasoning-model* half reopens.
- If E6 shows the single judge is unreliable (κ < 0.4), no cell in this matrix that rests on production verdicts can be read as more than n = 1 taste.
