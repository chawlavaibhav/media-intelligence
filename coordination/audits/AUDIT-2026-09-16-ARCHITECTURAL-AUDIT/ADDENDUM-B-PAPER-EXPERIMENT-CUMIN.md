### Reading (INFERENCE)

1. **The nine lines on which Pathway 1 fails are exactly the dimensions the human rejected on** at V1/V2/V3 (no hero in first/last frame, no brand early, no ask, no product close, wide opening, timeline before VO, voice after plates, crops with cards over the subject, supers ≠ VO). A fresh-context session with a twelve-line checklist, given only the plan, flagged all of them at 13:27Z-equivalent, before USD 0 had been spent. The producing session, with 21 Canon check lines and the same facts, flagged none. This is the E4 result in miniature: the difference is not the knowledge, it is who answers.
2. **Line 7 is a defect in the checklist, and the checker exposed it.** Pathway 1's "the hero image needs no copy … sells at a glance" is the self-attested PA-D7 line; the checker accepted that *claim* as a described frame and answered YES. The frame it refers to does not exist in any beat. Rephrase line 7 to require a numbered beat or board frame ("Which beat/frame? Quote it."), not a description. The same lesson as PA-D7 itself: a question that can be satisfied by an assertion will be.
3. **Line 12 is a second soft spot.** Pathway 1 tested the grip clip first and called it the hardest requirement; the checker took that at face value. The shot that actually failed on the plates was the miss (beat 4) and the notch payoff (beat 5). Rephrase: "Which shot has the highest generative risk (hands, crossing objects, falling objects, faces under VO), and is that the one tested first?"
4. **Limits.** n = 1 brief; Pathway 2 written by the same model family with hindsight; one checker, one run (Sonnet); the checklist was written by the same auditor. The blind trial that removes those limits is §7. None of the limits touches finding 1: the checker had no hindsight and no transcript, and its NO answers quote the plan's own text.
