## A. Context engineering for agents

### A1. Position effects and long-context degradation (the "context rot" literature)

| # | Source | Direct claim |
|---|---|---|
| A1.1 | **Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts** — Liu, Lin, Hewitt, Paranjape, Bevilacqua, Petroni, Liang (Stanford/UW/Samaya). arXiv:2307.03172, submitted 6 Jul 2023, rev. 20 Nov 2023 (TACL 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172 | OBSERVED: "performance is often highest when relevant information occurs at the beginning or end of the input context, and significantly degrades when models must access relevant information in the middle of long contexts." |
| A1.2 | **Same Task, More Tokens: the Impact of Input Length on the Reasoning Performance of LLMs** — Levy, Jacoby, Goldberg (Bar-Ilan/AI2). ACL 2024; arXiv:2402.14848. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14848 | OBSERVED: reasoning performance drops significantly with longer inputs "well before reaching the models' maximum input-length capacity", using FLenQA which pads only task-irrelevant text so length is isolated as the variable. |
| A1.3 | **RULER: What's the Real Context Size of Your Long-Context Language Models?** — Hsieh et al. (NVIDIA). COLM 2024; arXiv:2404.06654. https://github.com/NVIDIA/RULER | OBSERVED: "While all models claim context size of 32k tokens or greater, only half of them can effectively handle sequence length of 32K by exceeding a qualitative threshold." |
| A1.4 | **NoLiMa: Long-Context Evaluation Beyond Literal Matching** — Modarressi et al. (Adobe Research / LMU). ICML 2025; arXiv:2502.05167 (Feb 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05167 | OBSERVED: with minimal lexical overlap between question and needle, "10 models dropping below 50% of their short-length baselines at 32K"; GPT-4o falls from 99.3% to 69.7%. |
| A1.5 | **Context Rot: How Increasing Input Tokens Impacts LLM Performance** — Hong, Troynikov, Huber (Chroma). Technical report, 14 Jul 2025. https://research.trychroma.com/context-rot | OBSERVED: across 18 models "as needle-question similarity decreases, model performance degrades more significantly with increasing input length"; "even a single distractor reduces performance relative to the baseline"; "models perform better on shuffled haystacks than on logically structured ones"; and on LongMemEval "significantly higher performance on focused prompts compared to full prompts". |
| A1.6 | **Context Length Alone Hurts LLM Performance Despite Perfect Retrieval** — Du et al. (Amazon / HUJI). arXiv:2510.05381, 6 Oct 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05381 | OBSERVED: even when models "can perfectly retrieve all relevant information, their performance still degrades substantially", and this persists "when they are all masked and the models are forced to attend only to the relevant tokens". |
| A1.7 | **How Many Instructions Can LLMs Follow at Once? (IFScale)** — Jaroslawicz et al. (Distyl AI). arXiv:2507.11538, 15 Jul 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11538 | OBSERVED: with 500 simultaneous instructions "even the best frontier models only achieve 68% accuracy"; models show "bias towards earlier instructions" and three decay shapes (threshold, linear, exponential). |
| A1.8 | **Classifier Context Rot: Monitor Performance Degrades with Context Length** — Martin, Roger. arXiv:2605.12366, 12 May 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12366 | OBSERVED: frontier models (Opus 4.6, GPT 5.4, Gemini 3.1) "miss these actions 2× to 30× more often when they occur after 800K tokens of benign activity than when they occur on their own"; partial mitigation "with prompting techniques such as periodic reminders throughout the transcript". |
| A1.9 | **LOCA-bench: Benchmarking Language Agents Under Controllable and Extreme Context Growth** — Zeng, Huang, He (HKUST). arXiv:2602.07962, 8 Feb 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07962 | OBSERVED: "as the amount of context grows, their reliability often deteriorates"; "agent performance generally degrades as the environment states grow more complex", but "advanced context management techniques can substantially improve the overall success rate." |
| A1.10 | **Found in the Middle: Calibrating Positional Attention Bias Improves Long Context Utilization** — Hsieh et al. (Google). Findings of ACL 2024; arXiv:2406.16008. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16008 | OBSERVED: LLMs "exhibit a U-shaped attention bias where the tokens at the beginning and at the end of its input receive higher attention, regardless of their relevance"; calibration improves RAG "by up to 15 percentage points". |

INFERENCE (A1): Since 2023 the finding has only hardened: degradation is monotone in length, worst when the needed item is lexically dissimilar to the query, worst in the middle, and it is caused by length itself, not only by distractors. A1.7 is the closest analogue to a doctrine "pack": many simultaneous imperative instructions produce a measurable adherence curve with a bias to the earliest ones. A1.8 is the closest analogue to a QA/judge step run over a long production transcript.

### A2. Instruction hierarchy, rule-following and prompt fragility

| # | Source | Direct claim |
|---|---|---|
| A2.1 | **The Instruction Hierarchy: Training LLMs to Prioritize Privileged Instructions** — Wallace, Xiao, Leike, Weng, Heidecke, Beutel (OpenAI). arXiv:2404.13208, Apr 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13208 | OBSERVED: LLMs "often consider system prompts from application developers to be the same priority as text from untrusted users and third parties"; hierarchy is *trained in* via data generation, not achieved by prompting. |
| A2.2 | **Can LLMs Follow Simple Rules? (RuLES)** — Mu et al. (UC Berkeley et al.). arXiv:2311.04235, Nov 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04235 | OBSERVED: "almost all current models struggle to follow scenario rules, even on straightforward test cases." |
| A2.3 | **Can Large Language Models Truly Understand Prompts? A Case Study with Negated Prompts** — Jang, Ye, Seo. TL4NLP workshop, PMLR 203, 2023; arXiv:2209.12711. https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.12711 | OBSERVED: on negated prompts the scaling law inverts — "all LM types perform worse on negated prompts as they scale", and models tend to "perform the task as if provided with the original prompt." |
| A2.4 | **Quantifying Language Models' Sensitivity to Spurious Features in Prompt Design** — Sclar, Choi, Tsvetkov, Suhr. arXiv:2310.11324, Oct 2023 (ICLR 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324 | OBSERVED: "performance differences of up to 76 accuracy points" from formatting-only changes in few-shot prompts (LLaMA-2-13B). |
| A2.5 | **Context Engineering for AI Agents: Lessons from Building Manus** — Yichao "Peak" Ji (Manus). 18 Jul 2025. https://manus.im/blog/Context-Engineering-for-AI-Agents-Lessons-from-Building-Manus | OBSERVED: "The more uniform your context, the more brittle your agent becomes" (few-shot pattern mimicry causes drift); recitation — "By constantly rewriting the todo list, Manus is reciting its objectives into the end of the context"; "Leaving the wrong turns in the context" lets the model adjust. |

INFERENCE (A2): Prohibitions phrased negatively ("don't make it a tutorial", "no text over faces") are exactly the instruction form the negation literature (A2.3) shows models handle worst, and rule-following studies (A2.2) show is fragile even when the rule is short and in-context. This is a mechanism by which "the knowledge is in the pack" and "the ad still becomes a tutorial" can both be true.

### A3. Vendor guidance on context assembly, progressive disclosure, caching

| # | Source | Direct claim |
|---|---|---|
| A3.1 | **Effective context engineering for AI agents** — Rajasekaran, Dixon, Ryan, Hadfield et al. (Anthropic). 29 Sep 2025. https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents | OBSERVED: "Context is a critical but finite resource for AI agents"; LLMs have an "attention budget"; system prompts should sit at "the right altitude … the Goldilocks zone between two common failure modes" (brittle hardcoded logic vs vague guidance); prefer "just in time" loading via "lightweight identifiers"; "Compaction is the practice of taking a conversation nearing the context window limit, summarizing its contents, and reinitiating"; "Structured note-taking, or agentic memory, is a technique where the agent regularly writes notes persisted to memory outside of the context window"; "specialized sub-agents can handle focused tasks with clean context windows". |
| A3.2 | **Equipping agents for the real world with Agent Skills** — Anthropic. 16 Oct 2025. https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills | OBSERVED: three-level progressive disclosure — "At startup, the agent pre-loads the `name` and `description` of every installed skill into its system prompt"; "If Claude thinks the skill is relevant to the current task, it will load the skill by reading its full `SKILL.md` into context"; linked files are "the third level (and beyond) of detail, which Claude can choose to navigate and discover only as needed"; "When the `SKILL.md` file becomes unwieldy, split its content into separate files and reference them." |
| A3.3 | **Prompt caching** — Claude Platform Docs (accessed 2026-09-16). https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching | OBSERVED: "Cache prefixes are created in the following order: `tools`, `system`, then `messages`"; "Changes at each level invalidate that level and all subsequent levels"; "Cache read tokens are 0.1 times the base input tokens price" (0.025× on the newest models); "By default, the cache has a 5-minute lifetime"; "You can define up to 4 cache breakpoints". |
| A3.4 | **Long context prompting tips** — Claude Docs. https://docs.claude.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/long-context-tips | OBSERVED: put long documents "near the top of your prompt, above your query, instructions, and examples" (queries at the end improved quality "by up to 30% in tests"); "ask Claude to quote relevant parts of the documents first before carrying out its task". |
| A3.5 | **Building effective agents** — Erik Schluntz, Barry Zhang (Anthropic). 19 Dec 2024. https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents | OBSERVED: "find the simplest solution possible, and only increasing complexity when needed"; routing "classifies an input and directs it to a specialized followup task … allows for separation of concerns"; evaluator-optimizer is "particularly effective when we have clear evaluation criteria, and when iterative refinement provides measurable value"; prompt chaining can "add programmatic checks (see 'gate' …) on any intermediate steps". |
| A3.6 | Manus (A2.5) | OBSERVED: "The KV-cache hit rate is the single most important metric for a production-stage AI agent"; "Treat the file system as the ultimate context"; mask tool logits rather than removing tools mid-task. |
| A3.7 | **Agentic Context Engineering: Evolving Contexts for Self-Improving Language Models (ACE)** — Zhang et al. (Stanford / SambaNova). arXiv:2510.04618, 6 Oct 2025; ICLR 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04618 | OBSERVED: prior context-optimisation "often suffer[s] from brevity bias, which drops domain insights for concise summaries, and from context collapse, where iterative rewriting erodes details over time"; ACE keeps a structured, itemised playbook updated incrementally by generator/reflector/curator roles and reports "+10.6% on agents and +8.6% on finance". |
| A3.8 | **Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs? A Comprehensive Study and Hybrid Approach** — Li, Li, Zhang, Mei, Bendersky (Google). EMNLP Industry 2024; arXiv:2407.16833. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16833 | OBSERVED: "LC surpass[es] RAG in long-context understanding, while RAG is significantly more cost-efficient"; Self-Route lets the model choose per query and "achieves comparable performance to LC at a much lower cost." |
| A3.9 | **LLMLingua** — Jiang et al. (Microsoft). EMNLP 2023; arXiv:2310.05736. https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05736 | OBSERVED: perplexity-based token pruning "allows for up to 20x compression with little performance loss." |

INFERENCE (A3): Vendor guidance converges on four mechanisms: (i) a stable, cacheable prefix; (ii) metadata-first, body-on-demand loading (skills); (iii) task-specific material placed late (recency) or recited; (iv) externalised notes rather than ever-growing in-context state. Two published tensions matter for the audit: ACE (A3.7) argues *against* aggressive summarisation of accumulated know-how (brevity bias), while the context-rot line (A1) argues *against* loading everything. The reconciliation in the literature is structure + selectivity, not volume.

### B2. When retrieval hurts

| # | Source | Direct claim |
|---|---|---|
| B2.1 | **The Power of Noise: Redefining Retrieval for RAG Systems** — Cuconasu et al. (Sapienza). SIGIR 2024; arXiv:2401.14887. https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14887 | OBSERVED: "the retriever's highest-scoring documents that are not directly relevant to the query (e.g., do not contain the answer) negatively impact the effectiveness"; yet "adding random documents in the prompt improves the LLM accuracy by up to 35%". |
| B2.2 | **When Not to Trust Language Models** — Mallen, Asai, Zhong, Das, Khashabi, Hajishirzi. ACL 2023; arXiv:2212.10511. https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10511 | OBSERVED: retrieval helps long-tail facts but not popular ones; "Adaptive Retrieval … leading to improved accuracy by 5.3% and halving API costs". |
| B2.3 | Chroma Context Rot (A1.5) | OBSERVED: similar-but-wrong distractors are the most harmful content; one distractor already hurts. |
| B2.4 | **Mem0: Building Production-Ready AI Agents with Scalable Long-Term Memory** — Chhikara, Khant, Aryan, Singh, Yadav (Mem0). arXiv:2504.19413, Apr 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413 (verified from PDF text, Table 2 and §4.3) | OBSERVED: on LOCOMO (~26K-token conversations) "a full-context method … still achieves the highest J score (approximately 73%)" (72.90 ± 0.19); Mem0 ≈ 67%, Mem0-graph ≈ 68%; "Even the strongest RAG approach peaks at around 61%"; full-context p95 latency 17.1 s vs 1.44 s for Mem0. |
| B2.5 | **Are Online Skill and Memory Modules Always Worth Their Tokens? A Budget-Constrained Study of Web Agents** — Hajimiri et al. arXiv:2606.15017, 12 Jun 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15017 | OBSERVED: with a token-matched vanilla baseline "the vanilla baseline matches or surpasses all three augmentation methods [AWM, ASI, ReasoningBank] in aggregate success rate while often using fewer total tokens"; "run-to-run variance materially affects outcomes". |

INFERENCE (B2): Three distinct ways retrieval over a doctrine corpus can *lower* output quality are documented: (a) near-miss chunks (semantically related principles that do not answer the job's question) are actively harmful (B2.1, B2.3); (b) chunked retrieval loses to whole-document context when the corpus fits (B1.1's 200K rule; B2.4); (c) retrieval/memory overhead can consume the budget that extra reasoning steps would have used better (B2.5).

### B3. Principles vs examples vs questions — what changes a decision

| # | Source | Direct claim |
|---|---|---|
| B3.1 | **Rethinking the Role of Demonstrations: What Makes In-Context Learning Work?** — Min et al. (UW/Meta). EMNLP 2022; arXiv:2202.12837. https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12837 | OBSERVED: "randomly replacing labels in the demonstrations barely hurts performance"; what matters is the label space, input distribution and *format* of demonstrations. |
| B3.2 | **Case-Based or Rule-Based: How Do Transformers Do the Math?** — Hu, Tang, Yang, Zhang (PKU). ICML 2024; arXiv:2402.17709. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17709 | OBSERVED: intervention experiments confirm "transformers are performing case-based reasoning"; Rule-Following Fine-Tuning ("providing explicit rules in the input and instructing transformers to recite and follow the rules step by step") generalises 1–5-digit training to 12-digit addition ">95% accuracy". |
| B3.3 | **In-Context Principle Learning from Mistakes (LEAP)** — Zhang et al. (CMU/Google). ICML 2024; arXiv:2402.05403. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05403 | OBSERVED: induce mistakes on a few examples, reflect, extract "explicit task-specific 'principles'", then prompt with examples + principles; "improves over the standard few-shot prompting using GPT-4 by 7.5% in DROP, and by 3.3% in HotpotQA" at no extra examples. |
| B3.4 | **Retrieved In-Context Principles from Previous Mistakes (RICP)** — Sun et al. EMNLP 2024; arXiv:2407.05682. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05682 | OBSERVED: mistakes are "clustered based on their underlying reasons for developing task-level principles"; at inference "the most relevant mistakes for each question are retrieved to create question-level principles"; consistent gains across seven reasoning benchmarks (e.g. +22.6% relative on AQuA vs zero-shot CoT). |
| B3.5 | **Chain-of-Verification Reduces Hallucination in LLMs** — Dhuliawala et al. (Meta AI). arXiv:2309.11495, Sep 2023; Findings of ACL 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11495 | OBSERVED: draft → "plans verification questions to fact-check its draft" → answers them "independently so the answers are not biased" → final; strongest is "fully factored execution" where each question is answered without seeing the draft. |
| B3.6 | **Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback** — Bai et al. (Anthropic). arXiv:2212.08073, Dec 2022. https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073 | OBSERVED: principles are operationalised as *critique prompts* applied one principle at a time; "Generating critiques improves harmlessness compared to simply generating revisions directly." |
| B3.7 | Manus (A2.5) | OBSERVED: uniform few-shot histories make the agent "brittle" — examples are imitated, including their mistakes. |
| B3.8 | **TICKing All the Boxes: Generated Checklists Improve LLM Evaluation and Generation** — Cook et al. (Cohere/Oxford). arXiv:2410.03608, 4 Oct 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03608 | OBSERVED: instruction-specific yes/no checklists raise exact judge–human agreement "46.4% → 52.2%"; using the same checklist for self-refinement gives "+7.8%" absolute on LiveBench reasoning and Best-of-N "+6.3%" on WildBench. |

INFERENCE (B3): The published mechanism by which a "principle" changes a decision is not "read it in a prefix" — it is (i) a *question* the model must answer about its own draft (B3.5, B3.6, B3.8), (ii) a principle *attached to a retrieved similar mistake* (B3.3, B3.4), or (iii) a rule the model is made to *recite and apply step by step* (B3.2). Plain demonstrations move behaviour by format and distribution, not by their stated lesson (B3.1), and can propagate their own defects (B3.7). No source I found shows that a static list of abstract principles injected as a prefix reliably changes downstream generation on tasks unlike the principle's origin; the mistake-anchored and question-form variants are where the measured effects are.

No source shows the same effect from the same content expressed as declarative doctrine in a prefix. Two caveats bound the claim: checklists decay with length (F2.8), and checklist rewards can be gamed (F2.10). Checklists therefore look like a *representation* change with measured effect, not a silver bullet.

## What this implies for the audit (≤600 words; maps mechanisms to hypotheses, no winner chosen)

**Hypothesis: retrieval weak.** Supported mechanisms: contextual chunk enrichment + hybrid BM25 + reranking cuts retrieval misses by two-thirds (B1.1); mistake-anchored retrieval of principles (RICP, B3.4) is the form shown to change reasoning decisions. Against: retrieval over a corpus that fits in ~200K tokens is dominated by full-document context (B1.1, B2.4), and near-miss doctrine chunks are actively harmful (B2.1, A1.5). The audit should therefore distinguish "retrieval fails to find the rule" from "retrieval finds adjacent doctrine that crowds out the rule". The eight-state chain matters: research only shows retrieval helps at states 2–3; it does not show state 5 (decision changed) for abstract principles.

**Hypothesis: context overloaded.** Strongly supported as a *possible* mechanism: degradation is monotone in length even with perfect retrieval (A1.6), adherence decays with instruction count and favours early instructions (A1.7), negatively-phrased rules are handled worst (A2.3), and a judge run late in a long transcript misses 2–30× more (A1.8). Vendor guidance is to keep a stable cacheable prefix, load bodies on demand, and recite the live checklist late (A3.1–A3.6). Whether the two packs cross any of these thresholds is an empirical question for the repo councils (token counts, position of the failing rule, instruction count).

**Hypothesis: role collapse.** The literature is unusually consistent: same-model self-critique without external signal does not correct and can degrade (E1, E3, E4), judges favour their own generations (D2.3, D2.4), and MAST names "task verification" as a top-level failure class (E10). Separation helps only when the critic has different information, a trained objective, or a programmatic gate (D3.1, D3.3, A3.5). This supports investigating whether QA is the same model with the same context as the author — and cautions against fixing it with another prompted persona.

**Hypothesis: representation wrong.** Supported: transformers reason case-based, not rule-based, unless made to recite rules (B3.2); demonstrations act through format not lesson (B3.1); the measured behaviour changes come from binary instance-level questions (F2.1–F2.7). If the doctrine is stored as prose principles and Q&A rather than as pause-point checklists and paired failure/success exemplars, the research says the form, not the volume, is the limiting factor. This hypothesis is compatible with all the others rather than exclusive of them.

**Hypothesis: learned policy needed.** Supported for procedure/format, not facts: ~10^3 curated examples shape behaviour (D1.6), trained verifiers are worth ~30× generator scale (D3.1), small checklist-driven judges match GPT-4o (D3.7), and 8 examples/class suffice for narrow classifiers (D3.9). Against: fine-tuning on new facts induces hallucination (D1.4), LoRA underfits (D1.5), self-generated data risks collapse (D1.9), and generator/judge kinship leaks preference (D2.4). A narrow, checklist-formatted, separately-trained detector ("is this ad a tutorial?") is well precedented; a fine-tuned general operator is not.

**Hypothesis: over-engineered.** Supported: budget-matched vanilla actors match skill/memory modules (B2.5); free-text lesson memories confabulate and repeat (C2.3); iterative memory rewriting collapses detail (A3.7); Anthropic's own guidance is "simplest solution possible" with programmatic gates (A3.5); Cognition's 2026 position keeps writes single-threaded (E12). Against: memory *does* help when it is procedural/executable and contrastively distilled (C1.5–C1.8, C1.11, C1.12), and clean-context subagents help parallel read tasks (E11).

Cross-cutting: the only intervention type with positive evidence from *both* human-factors and LLM studies is a short, instance-specific confirmation checklist executed at a defined pause point by something other than the author (F1, F2). That is not a verdict on the audit's hypotheses; it is the mechanism most of them would need to instantiate anyway.

## Findings ranked (by load-bearing weight for the audit)

1. Same-model, prompt-only self-critique does not reliably correct and can degrade; correction needs external feedback, a trained critic, or a programmatic verifier (E1, E3, E4, D3.1, D3.3). [OBSERVED across ≥5 independent sources]
2. Context length alone degrades performance even with perfect retrieval; instruction adherence decays with instruction count with early-instruction bias; judges/monitors lose recall in long transcripts (A1.6, A1.7, A1.8). [OBSERVED]
3. Checklists as binary instance-level questions measurably improve both judging and generation; the same content as declarative doctrine has no comparable measured effect (F2.1–F2.7, B3.5, B3.6). [OBSERVED for checklists; absence-of-evidence for doctrine — INFERENCE]
4. Transformers reason case-based; rules change behaviour when recited/applied stepwise or anchored to retrieved similar mistakes (B3.2, B3.3, B3.4). [OBSERVED]
5. Judges favour their own (or kin) generations; multimodal judges are weak at absolute scoring (D2.3, D2.4, D2.5). [OBSERVED]
6. Free-text reflective "lessons" confabulate and repeat; programmatic failure-signal extraction fixes it (C2.3). Memory/skill modules can be net-negative under a matched token budget (B2.5). [OBSERVED, 2026]
7. Full-context beats both memory extraction and chunked RAG when the corpus is ~26K tokens; Anthropic's stated threshold for skipping RAG is ~200K tokens (B2.4, B1.1). [OBSERVED]
8. Near-miss retrieved passages are worse than random ones (B2.1, A1.5). [OBSERVED]
9. Fine-tuning injects procedure/format well with ~10^3 curated examples but injects facts poorly and raises hallucination (D1.1, D1.4, D1.6). [OBSERVED]
10. Multi-agent gains are task-structure dependent; MAS failures are mostly system-design and verification failures (E10, E11, E12). [OBSERVED]
11. Human-factors: checklists work only when enforced at a pause point; mandated-but-unenforced checklists show null effects (F1.1–F1.5). [OBSERVED]
12. Cacheable-prefix discipline (tools → system → messages; any earlier change invalidates later cache) constrains where per-job material can go (A3.3, A3.6). [OBSERVED]
