## A. How agencies turn incomplete briefs into complete briefs

### A1. Canonical brief structures and their mandatory fields

**GET / WHO / TO / BY (one-line brief).** OBSERVED-PRIMARY. Julian Cole, "How to use the creative brief GET/WHO/TO/BY", Strategy Finishing School, May 2021, https://strategyfinishingschool.com/how-to-use-the-creative-brief-get-who-to-by/ — defines GET = target consumer; WHO = consumer problem (what they think/feel/do now); TO = desired response; BY = "one message/action". Cole's principle: "creative briefs have to be simple or wonderful, but nothing in-between". The Spotify worked example shows the format forces a *single* lever (BY) and a *behavioural* delta (WHO → TO). Origin credited to BBDO NY (Martyn Straw, ~2004-05) per a Medium history piece (SEARCH-ONLY: https://medium.com/@baiba.matisone/origin-of-the-get-who-to-by-model-0ed51eafd65d).

**"Sacred Six" (Planning Dirty).** SEARCH-ONLY / UNKNOWN. Cole states he collected "over 30+ creative brief templates" and found "six main components that kept coming up time and time again" (x.com/juliancole/status/1252904694466273282, Apr 2020). The six names could not be verified (tweet not fetchable; academy.planningdirty.com DNS fails). Do not cite the list contents.

**Textbook creative brief (aggregated).** OBSERVED-PRIMARY. Wikipedia "Creative brief" (citing Burtenshaw/Mahon/Barfoot *Fundamentals of Creative Advertising* 2011; O'Guinn et al. 2011; Butterfield *Excellence in Advertising* 1999) lists: Background; Target audience; User/consumer profile; Key insight; Objectives; Single message; Desired customer behaviour; Tone of voice; Mandatories; Deliverables; Timeline; Budget; Approvals. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_brief

**Ogilvy-style brief fields.** OBSERVED-SECONDARY (template reproductions on Scribd/monday.com; original Ogilvy template not fetched). Recurring mandatory fields: single-minded proposition (one sentence), reason to believe / support (specific evidence), tone of voice (with concrete examples), what the audience currently thinks vs. what we want them to think/do, mandatories, measurement, channels, deadline/budget. https://www.scribd.com/document/475407107/ogilvy-DO-Brief-template-doc

**JWT Planning Cycle (Stephen King, 1974).** OBSERVED-SECONDARY (Medium summary of the JWT guide). Five questions: Where are we? Why are we there? Where could we be? How could we get there? Are we getting there? — the questions a strategist asks the client before a brief exists. https://utkarshgargofficial.medium.com/whats-so-special-about-1974-s-jwt-planning-guide-highly-recommended-by-world-s-top-strategic-213ccaebf916

**Binet & Field (IPA).** OBSERVED-PRIMARY (IPA page). *The Long and the Short of It* (2013) — analysis of the IPA Databank (996 campaigns 1980–2010); brand-building vs activation on "two different clocks"; ~60:40 budget split; fame/emotional campaigns show the largest profit effects; activation gives sharp, short-lived lift. https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/effectiveness-research-analysis/les-binet-peter-field. Implication for briefs: the brief must declare *which clock* the ad is on (activation vs brand), because it changes proof/CTA weighting.

**Ehrenberg-Bass distinctive assets.** OBSERVED-SECONDARY + PRIMARY journal. Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (OUP 2018); Sharp, *How Brands Grow* (2010). Newest empirical benchmark: "Shape-based assets are strongest: benchmarking distinctive brand asset performance across industries", *International Journal of Advertising*, 2026 (DOI 10.1080/02650487.2026.2637295): 1,162 distinctive assets, 21 categories, 4 countries, 9 years; shape-based assets (logos, packaging) perform best (40% Fame / 71% Uniqueness); colour assets weakest. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02650487.2026.2637295. Brief implication: the brief must enumerate the brand's *existing* distinctive assets (pack shape, logo, sonic, character) so the creative uses them; an AI system that invents new "branding" each job is working against this evidence.

### A2. The questions a strategist asks a founder (synthesised from the above; INFERENCE)

The fields above converge on a fixed interrogation set: (1) business objective and success metric; (2) who exactly (segment + current behaviour/belief); (3) what they should think/do after; (4) single proposition; (5) reason to believe / proof; (6) product role (hero vs enabler vs incidental); (7) distinctive assets that must appear; (8) tone with examples; (9) mandatories (legal, logo, offer terms); (10) deliverables/formats/durations; (11) budget/timeline; (12) who approves. Each is a *decision*, not a description; an incomplete request is one where a decision is missing, not where a detail is missing.

### A3. Role separation and review rituals

**Tissue session.** OBSERVED-PRIMARY. Mercer Island Group, "How Marketers & Agencies can win the tissue session" (Steve Boehler, 19 Mar 2024): a midpoint session presenting "early thinking about the business challenge", "early strategies", "early solution ideas" — rough territories, not finished work — so the client shapes direction before substantial finishing. https://migroup.com/blog/how-marketers-agencies-can-win-the-tissue-session/. Seed ATL (SEARCH-ONLY): term from tracing-paper ("tissue") comps; sessions "begin most often between creative teams and their creative directors, with account planners and account directors brought in after the CD is feeling comfortable". https://seedatl.com/atlanta-ad-agency-tissue-session/

**Internal review precedes client review.** OBSERVED-SECONDARY (Ziflow/Dalim/Revue practice guides). Six-stage pattern: submit → internal review → consolidate feedback → revise/version → external review → final sign-off; "internal review happens before client presentation"; "sign-off is documented in writing"; a named approver per stage; reviews "in a defined sequence—concept, copy, design, compliance" (Dalim, Rebecca Freeman, updated 23 Jun 2026, https://www.dalim.com/dalimfusion_main_blog/why-creative-projects-get-stuck-in-endless-revision-rounds). "Creative review board"/"creative council" as a formal named body: UNKNOWN (no primary source found; treat as agency-specific).

**Role split (planner / CD / producer).** OBSERVED-SECONDARY (agency-hierarchy explainers): planner owns the brief (problem, insight, proposition, tone, deliverables); CD owns creative quality vs brief; producer owns feasibility, schedule, budget, vendor casting and delivery specs. https://reallygooddesigns.com/creative-agency-roles/

## B. Near-universal structural knowledge for short video ads

### B1. YouTube ABCD (Google) — with the published numbers

OBSERVED-PRIMARY. Google, "A playbook for building effective creative on YouTube" (ABCD playbook PDF, research partner Ipsos, reviewed by Nielsen Neuro and Kantar; sources dated 2018), https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/_qs/documents/8472/ABCD_Complete_V7b_HR_1.pdf. Verbatim rules:
- Attract: "Use tight framing on the subject"; "Aim for two or more shots in the first five seconds" (positive for Ad Recall and Consideration); "If people appear in your video, open with them on screen"; "Use stunning, delightful, unexpected, or otherwise memorable imagery".
- Brand: "Introduce your product or brand in the first five seconds"; "If people appear in your video, have them mention your brand" — "Audio mentions of your brand by people on screen are associated with better results for Ad Recall and Consideration than audio mentions by voiceover"; logo placement by objective — "For Ad Recall, integrate your logo into the story or feature it on the product itself. For Consideration, integrate it as a super, text overlay, or persistent watermark"; "Show visuals and colors unique to your brand".
- Connect: function + emotion together; "Humor improves ad receptivity more than any other creative element"; "Make people core to the story".
- Direct: "Show offers and calls-to-action through text cards, simple animation or voiceover"; urgency offers; "Use specific calls-to-action (for example, Visit site, Sign up, Buy now)".
- Story arc: "emerging story arc" (strong cue, multiple peaks, brand cues throughout) vs traditional arc (lead-in/build/climax/reveal/branding at end).
- Data footnotes: Nielsen Neuro database 2013–2016 (n=6,000 ads); Google/Ipsos Creative Drivers Analysis Oct–Dec 2018 (n=5,000 TrueView ads, 11 verticals); Kantar/MB Link database >15,000 ads; "95%" of YouTube video played with sound on (Google internal, Sept 2018); 70% of watch time on mobile.
- Brief-stage instruction in the playbook itself: "Plan for an emerging story arc… Plan for mobile-first and sound on… Consider your subject(s)".

Effectiveness claim: OBSERVED-PRIMARY. Think with Google "YouTube ABCDs" (April 2022) and Google Ads Help "About the ABCDs" — "30% lift in short-term sales likelihood and a 17% lift in long-term brand contribution" (Google/Kantar Link AI, Global, 2021). https://business.google.com/en-all/think/future-of-marketing/youtube-video-ad-creative/ ; https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14783551. Google Ads Help adds objective-specific variants (Awareness: audio-led attention, prominent brand, relatable people; Consideration: product functionality/demo; Action: product centred, "present CTAs after establishing context"). Note "sales likelihood" is a Kantar Link-AI predicted metric, not measured sales.

### B2. Meta mobile-first guidance and safe zones (as currently published)

**9:16 Stories/Reels safe zone.** OBSERVED-PRIMARY (Meta Ads Guide, Instagram Reels video, served in Hindi locale): aspect ratio 9:16; recommended resolution 1440 × 2560; keep text/logos/key elements out of "at least 14%" of the top, "35%" of the bottom, and "6% on both sides". https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels. On a 1080 × 1920 canvas this is ≈269 px top, ≈672 px bottom, ≈65 px each side (arithmetic; third-party guides use 250–270 px top). Multiple third-party guides report Meta unified Stories and Reels to a single 9:16 safe zone in March 2026 (SEARCH-ONLY; e.g. https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones/). Design rule that follows: design for the Reels 35% bottom band, not the older Stories ~20%.

**4:5 feed and 1:1.** OBSERVED-PRIMARY for feed spec (Meta Ads Guide, Instagram feed video lists 9:16 at 1080 × 1920 with min width 250 px and 1% ratio tolerance; feed placements accept 1:1/4:5 with cropping outside 1.91:1–4:5). **Meta publishes no percentage safe zone for 4:5 or 1:1** — third-party "100–250 px" margins are working figures, not Meta numbers (SEARCH-ONLY, e.g. https://blog.adnabu.com/meta-ads/meta-safe-zones/). The documented, defensible rule is: when a single 9:16 master is reused, critical content must survive a centre crop to 4:5 (middle 1080 × 1350 of a 1080 × 1920 frame) and to 1:1 (middle 1080 × 1080). Meta's Ads Manager exposes a safe-zone overlay ("Safe Zone Guardrail") per third-party reports (SEARCH-ONLY).

**Sound-off / first 3 seconds.** OBSERVED-SECONDARY. "85% of Facebook video is watched without sound" — Digiday, 2016, reporting publisher data (https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/); Meta's own "design for sound off / captions" advice is widely reproduced but the current first-party page was not fetched. Facebook-commissioned Nielsen study (MarTech, 2015): lift in ad recall/brand awareness/purchase intent observed from <1 s of view; "47% ad recall, 32% brand awareness, 44% purchase intent" for ≤3 s viewers (SEARCH-ONLY; MarTech page 403). Facebook/MetrixLab: "23% improvement in recall when brand presence was introduced within 3 seconds" (SEARCH-ONLY via brainsuite). Treat "brand in first 3 s" as Meta-platform-specific guidance consistent with, but not identical to, YouTube's "first 5 s".

**Advantage+ creative enhancements.** OBSERVED-PRIMARY (Meta Help Center, Hindi locale, https://www.facebook.com/help/360566182054775/): 31 enhancements listed; generative-AI ones include Animation, Music, Overlays, Video Effects, Background Generator, CTA Stickers, CTA Improvement, Media Text Enhancement, Photo Generation, Photo Touch-up, Text Generator, Text Enhancement, Translation, Video Subtitles, Video Touch-up, Visual Touch-up; non-AI ones include brightness/contrast, dynamic overlays, product tags, standard labels, timeline info, store locations. "Some enhancements may be enabled by default, but you can disable them anytime" (translated). Restricted verticals excluded from gen-AI features. Operational consequence: a delivered creative can be altered by the platform (text rewritten, backgrounds extended, image expanded to other ratios) unless enhancements are switched off — QA must specify the enhancement state.

### B3. TikTok Creative Codes

OBSERVED-PRIMARY (TikTok "Creative Codes one-pager", PDF dated 5.2023, https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_One_Pager_CA.pdf). Six codes: 01 TikTok First, 02 Structure, 03 Stimulation, 04 Sound, 05 Trends, 06 Production. Verbatim data:
- TikTok First: "Go lo-fi (avoid glossiness)… Feature people (creators, employees, customers)"; moving TV-style assets to TikTok-First: +10% recall, +37% intent, +38% favourability, +17% brand association, 2× engagement/watch time (Kantar creative-coding analysis of 3,500 ads, 2021).
- Structure: hook / body / close. "The first six seconds are vital"; "90% of ad recall impact is captured within 6 seconds", "80% of the awareness impact is captured within 6 seconds" (TikTok "Value of View" analysis, 2020). Hook types: suspense (+16% watch time), surprise (1.7× VTR), emotion (1.7× awareness lift) (Lumen study, 2021). "Branding in the first few seconds should be subtle and not compromise the hook"; "Body: Additive branding"; "Close: Use strong CTA".
- Stimulation: music, transitions, movement, text pop-ups, emojis.
- Sound: 88% of users say sound is essential; TikTok "the only platform where ads with sound drive significant lifts in purchase intent and brand favorability" (TikTok/Kantar 2021; OBSERVED-SECONDARY via Social Media Today, 9 Jun 2021, https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-shares-new-insights-into-the-importance-of-sound-for-marketing-promo/601569/).
- Attention: MediaScience/TikTok lab study (343 US participants, eye-tracking/HR/GSR): "An ad on screen 6 seconds or less still delivered 38% of the recall compared to ads viewed 20 seconds or more" (OBSERVED-PRIMARY, https://www.mediascience.com/case-studies/understanding-attention-on-tiktok-ads/).

**TikTok safe zone.** OBSERVED-PRIMARY (TikTok Ads Help, "Video ads specifications", https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/video-ads-specifications): "The safe zone size is determined by the dimension (vertical, horizontal, or square), ad caption length, and any additional formats used"; TikTok provides downloadable safe-zone templates (LTR/RTL, with/without anchors) rather than a fixed percentage. Third-party figures (top ~150 px, bottom ~440 px, sides ~60 px on 1080 × 1920; or "top/bottom 120 px") are estimates and vary between guides (SEARCH-ONLY). Rule: use the platform's template, not a hard-coded number.

**YouTube Shorts safe zone.** OBSERVED-PRIMARY (Google Ads Help "About video ad specs", https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13547298): "Overlays, call-to-actions, and buttons may appear in different places depending on the format, campaign type, and screen"; Google supplies transparent PNG safe-area templates for horizontal (1920×1080), vertical (1080×1920) and square (1080×1080) — no percentages in text. Shorts specs page (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16041697): vertical preferred; horizontal served "with blurred top and bottoms"; "only the first 60 seconds will play on the Shorts feed"; CTA button appears at 3 s (PMax/App/Demand Gen) or 10 s (Video View/Reach); 40-char headline / 90-char description for CTA overlay.

### B4. Kantar / System1 evidence on early branding and product visibility

**System1 × TikTok, "The Long and the Short (form) of it"** (Andrew Tindall, 23 Jun 2025; 887 ads, 92,000 TikTok users, 8 markets; matched to 350+ brand/conversion lift studies). OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://system1group.com/blog/how-to-make-entertaining-short-form-ads-for-tiktok-social — "Ads which deliver the highest levels of early brand recognition drive 88% higher Memory lift, 92% higher Brand Awareness lift and 85% higher Brand Image lift"; creator ads got "39% more attention" but the brand was correctly identified at "roughly half the rate"; "logos in context, which are integrated into the scene" outperform standalone logo placement in opening seconds. Chief Marketer's report of the same study (3 Aug 2025, OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://www.chiefmarketer.com/system1-tiktok-study-branding-elements-and-creator-led-content-improve-short-form-video-ads/): sonic assets in first two seconds +191% brand-awareness lift; logos in context +182%; fluent characters +57%; **logo overlays reduced brand awareness**; exceptional early branding cut negative sentiment 48%, raised happiness 57%; creator + early branding → 2.3× awareness lift, 3× brand-image lift.

**Kantar attention research** (21 Nov 2023, OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/agile-market-research/the-role-of-attention-in-creative-effectiveness): passive attention "diminishes more quickly in short formats (Instagram & Facebook stories and TikTok) and feed formats… emphasising the need for clear brand presentation within the initial seconds"; Apple Pay case: viewers "often stopped watching by the time the brand was introduced". Kantar on Meta (OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://www.kantar.com/industries/technology-and-telecoms/optimizing-brand-building-on-meta): video beats static for brand-building; shorter beats longer; upper-funnel ads 1.6× more efficient for long-term brand metrics.

**UGC/creator vs produced.** Balanced reading of the evidence: creator-style ads win *attention* and platform fit (System1 +39%; TikTok "lo-fi… feature people"), but *lose branding* unless early branding is engineered (System1: brand ID at half the rate; Mi3 headline "4X brand memory lift but 'still like going to the casino'", 1 Jul 2026, page 403 — SEARCH-ONLY). Vendor statistics ("4× CTR", "50% lower CPC") are from UGC marketplaces and are not independent (SEARCH-ONLY; e.g. influee.co, billo.app). Conclusion: "UGC" is a *style register* decision to be briefed, with an explicit early-branding requirement, not a free performance win.

**Which of B is universal vs platform-specific.** Universal across Google, Meta, TikTok, System1, Kantar: (i) hook in the first 2–6 s; (ii) brand/product recognisable early, preferably *in context* (product in hand/in scene, spoken by on-screen person) rather than as an overlay; (iii) legible when muted (captions/supers) — except YouTube where sound-on is ~95%; (iv) explicit CTA/offer at the close via text card/VO; (v) keep text/CTAs out of UI-occluded bands. Platform-specific: exact safe-zone geometry; "brand in 3 s" (Meta) vs "5 s" (YouTube) vs "subtle in hook, additive in body" (TikTok); lo-fi register (TikTok) vs polished (YouTube TV-style); sound-on assumptions.

## C. Pre-production review practice

**Storyboards → animatics → previs.** OBSERVED-PRIMARY. Wikipedia "Storyboard/Animatic" (citing Brine, *The Art of Cinematic Storytelling*, OUP 2020): "an animatic is a sequence of still images (usually taken from a storyboard) displayed in sync with rough dialogue (i.e., scratch vocals) or rough soundtrack"; used to "work out any screenplay, camera positioning, shot list, and timing issues"; "Editing at the animatic stage can help a production avoid wasting time and resources on the animation of scenes that would otherwise be edited out". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animatic

**VO before animation ("timing to the track").** OBSERVED-PRIMARY. Alexander Williams, Animation Apprentice blog, 19 May 2020: "Record your dialogue first, then do the animation"; animating first then post-syncing is "almost always a mistake"; "the voice performance should be finalised before animation gets hold of it" even when scratch tracks are used. https://animationapprentice.blogspot.com/2020/05/audio-first-then-animation.html. Corroborated (SEARCH-ONLY) by Voices.com and studio workflow explainers: VO is imported into the animatic and "that's what ensures the timing of the animation to be consistent". Consequence for an AI pipeline: script lock → VO record → *measured VO durations* → shot durations; never fix shot durations before the VO exists.

**Picture lock as the audio gate.** OBSERVED-PRIMARY. Frame.io Workflow Guide, "Editing stages": assembly → rough cut (client feedback possible; "No professional sound people have touched the film yet") → fine cut → picture lock ("no more editorial changes can be made… so that the color, sound, and finishing teams can begin work"). https://workflow.frame.io/guide/editing-stages

**Loudness and dialogue/music norms.**
- EBU R 128 (v5, Nov 2023) OBSERVED-PRIMARY (PDF): Programme Loudness target "−23.0 LUFS", ±1.0 LU only where unattainable (live), ±0.2 LU QC tolerance; "True Peak Level… shall not exceed −1 dBTP"; short-form content "should be made in compliance with EBU R 128 s1". https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r128.pdf
- EBU R 128 s1 (v3, Aug 2020, adverts/promos) OBSERVED-PRIMARY (PDF): Programme Loudness −23.0 LUFS (±0.2 LU QC); "Short-term Loudness Level… shall not exceed −18.0 LUFS (+5.0 LU on the relative scale)"; Max True Peak −1 dBTP; "Loudness Range… not useful for short-form content"; measure "in its entirety, without emphasis on specific foreground elements such as speech". https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r128s1.pdf
- Netflix (OBSERVED-SECONDARY; Production Expert 13 May 2022 quoting Netflix spec): "−27 LKFS within a 2 LU window", dialogue-gated per ITU-R BS.1770-1 with dialogue intelligence; true peak "not exceed −2 dBFS true peak"; full mix 4–18 LRA, dialogue "within a window of 10 LRA or less". https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/how-to-optimise-an-audio-mix-for-delivery-to-netflix (Netflix page itself not reachable).
- Dialogue-to-background ratio: WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.7 (OBSERVED-PRIMARY): "The background sounds are at least 20 decibels lower than the foreground speech content, with the exception of occasional sounds that last for only one or two seconds." https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/low-or-no-background-audio.html. Broadcast mixing folklore of "music 18–20 dB under speech" (SEARCH-ONLY) is consistent but not a standard. Platform ads (Meta/TikTok/YouTube) publish no loudness target; social platforms normalise on ingest (SEARCH-ONLY, youlean comparison table). A defensible default for social ads: −14 to −16 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP, dialogue ≥ 15–20 dB over bed — the first number is convention, not standard (INFERENCE).

**Voice casting: character brief before audition.** OBSERVED-PRIMARY. Toby Ricketts, "How to properly brief voice auditions…", 2 Apr 2024: brief should specify accent/dialect, tone (warm, authoritative, energetic, conversational, dramatic), emotional quality, age range and gender, usage/budget, deadlines, formats, and request a *custom read of the actual script excerpt* rather than a reel. https://www.tobyrickettsvoiceover.com/blog/how-to-properly-brief-voice-auditions-to-maximise-casting-call-quality. Corroborated by casting-platform guides (SEARCH-ONLY: Voice123, Backstage). This maps directly onto TTS controls (see E3): the casting brief is the input to voice-design prompts and audio tags.

**Lock gates before money moves (agency producer workflow).**
- AICP Standard Commercial Production Agreement (OBSERVED-PRIMARY PDF, https://aicp.com/assets/editor/Standard_Commercial_Production_Agreement_1.pdf): payment schedule fields "Due upon signing of contract %", "Due upon approval of photography %", "Due upon completion and delivery of all elements and materials %"; §4 Changes in Specifications: "If at any time, Agency/Client desires to make any changes or variations from the script(s) or storyboard(s)… and such changes result in additional costs to Producer, Producer agrees to notify the Contracting Client of the amount before any such additional costs are incurred and Producer shall proceed only after receiving approval (written or oral) from Authorized Representative"; §3A: "Agency/Client shall supply scripts, storyboards, product props, production notes, music…"; cancellation within 1–10 working days costs ≥50% director's fee and ≥50% production fee. I.e. script + storyboard are *contractual inputs* locked before the bid is binding.
- PPM (pre-production meeting). OBSERVED-PRIMARY (Yamdu, https://yamdu.com/en/learn/preproduction/preproduction-meeting-ppm/): checks "Storyboard, AV script, shot list: Is it clear what footage needs to be collected?", cast/styling "full agreement", deliverables "Any cut-downs? Additional material for social media", milestones "shoot, rough cut, delivery". Agency glossaries (SEARCH-ONLY) add: pre-PPM shows director's boards/treatment; at PPM "you sign off all of them".
- Pre-testing at gates. Kantar (Věra Šídlová, 5 Apr 2022, OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://www.kantar.com/north-america/inspiration/advertising-media/how-can-you-get-the-best-return-from-your-tv-advertising): "Testing an ad at two stages of development (for example animatic and finished film) can increase advertising impact by 38%"; three stages → client ad in "top 2% by breakthrough potential". Kantar Marketplace (OBSERVED-PRIMARY): "When you test your ads early in the creative development process, you can increase effectiveness by up to 12%"; LINK+ tests animatics; database "260,000 ads". Kantar Media Reactions (12 Jul 2023): "40% of marketers test little or none of their creative… cost (55%) and project time (46%)". Note the 38% and 12% are Kantar marketing claims without published method.

## D. Ad QA and creative operations

**Agency/creative-ops QA checklists.** OBSERVED-PRIMARY. MagicBrief "Ad Creative QA Checklist" (Maddie King, 27 Mar 2025, https://magicbrief.com/post/ad-creative-qa-checklist-catch-mistakes-before-your-ads-go-live): technical (watch start-to-finish for glitches; audio/video balance; duration matches placement; aspect ratio; "key visuals and text avoid edges; check mobile legibility"; preview on the actual platform), copy (typos; claims substantiated), brand (fonts/colours/logo/tone), legal (UGC permissions, credits, cultural sensitivity), performance (hook strength, message flow, pacing, CTA clarity, offer clarity). Generic ad-ops pre-flight (SEARCH-ONLY): specs/size/character limits, legal disclaimers per category, tracking/URLs. Variant packs (SEARCH-ONLY, consult.tv): the same campaign "can require dozens—or hundreds—of creative variants: sizes, aspect ratios, CTAs, legal lines, offer dates, landing pages, language". No independent, standardised industry QA checklist exists; the recurring categories are stable (technical/legibility/brand/legal/claims/CTA).

**AI creative platforms — what is documented (and only that).**
- *Pencil* (trypencil.com/the-platform, OBSERVED-PRIMARY, marketing page): "Goals, audience & brand in one place"; "Brand guidelines enforced automatically"; "Multi-stakeholder review with in-context feedback and versioning"; "Brand and legal sign-off built into the workflow"; "Predict creative performance before media spend". No public documentation of brief schema or QA rules found.
- *Omneky* (creative-brief page returns no content; blog 29 Sep 2023 OBSERVED-PRIMARY): automated brief draws on "historical information, industry trends, and consumer preferences" and "your brand, target audience, and messaging goals"; outputs drafts and mood boards. Marketing page (SEARCH-ONLY) says the brief tool "define[s] KPIs, audiences, and messaging". No schema published.
- *AdCreative.ai* (help centre, OBSERVED-PRIMARY): brand setup via URL import or manual; brand fields not enumerated in the help article; marketing pages (SEARCH-ONLY) mention logo/colours/description, a "compliance checker" and a scoring model. No QA rule documentation.
- *Meta Advantage+ creative*: see B2 — the only documented QA control is per-enhancement opt-out and preview.
- *Google Ads asset generation* (OBSERVED-PRIMARY, https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14150602 and /14150986): inputs = final URL, editable product/service summary, optional logos/business name, optional product images; outputs = headlines, descriptions, curated/stock/generated images, logo suggestions, auto-generated video if none supplied. QA obligations: "Advertisers should review any generated or suggested assets to ensure that they're accurate, not misleading, and not in violation of any Google advertising policies… before publishing"; "Assets created by generative AI aren't guaranteed to be approved by Ads Policy"; the generator "automatically limit[s]" "Children and prominent likeness", "Branded items and logos not provided as input"; "Style reference images and image outputs shouldn't contain logos, watermarks, or product images"; user-supplied product images yield "lifestyle imagery that is more faithful to your specific product".
- Adobe GenStudio for Performance Marketing (SEARCH-ONLY): "brand compliance" scoring against uploaded guidelines before the approval queue.
Pattern (INFERENCE): documented AI creative platforms put humans at (a) brand-kit definition and (b) final asset approval, and treat "brand compliance" as a score rather than a hard gate; none documents proposition/hook/proof checks.

## E. Multimodal AI production pipelines

### E1. Published pipelines and their ordering
- **VideoDirectorGPT** (Lin et al., arXiv 2309.15091, Sep 2023/Jul 2024, OBSERVED-SECONDARY abstract): GPT-4 expands one prompt into a "video plan" — scene descriptions, entities with layouts, backgrounds, and "consistency groupings of the entities" — then Layout2Vid renders with explicit spatial control and cross-scene entity consistency.
- **Anim-Director** (Li et al., arXiv 2408.09787, Aug 2024, SIGGRAPH Asia 2024, OBSERVED-PRIMARY): narrative → "director's script" with character profiles and interior/exterior descriptions → scene images → video prompts from scene images; consistency via "visual-language prompting… combines scene descriptions and images of the appearing character and setting"; the agent "evaluate[s] visual quality, and select[s] the best one" (self-QA loop, no human).
- **DreamFactory** (arXiv 2408.11788, Aug 2024, SEARCH-ONLY): simulated animation company (CEO/Director/Creator roles); "Key Frames Iteration Design Method" for cross-scene consistency.
- **StoryAgent** (Hu et al., arXiv 2411.04925, Nov 2024, OBSERVED-PRIMARY): agents for "story design, storyboard generation, video creation, agent coordination, and result evaluation"; LoRA-BE customised I2V for intra-shot temporal consistency; a storyboard pipeline for cross-shot subject consistency.
- **FilmAgent** (Xu et al., arXiv 2501.12909, Jan 2025, OBSERVED-PRIMARY): director/screenwriter/actor/cinematographer agents; three stages — idea development → scriptwriting → cinematography; "Critique-Correct-Verify" (scriptwriting) and "Debate-Judge" (cinematography); 15 film ideas, human score 3.98/5; multi-agent GPT-4o beat single-agent o1.
- **MovieAgent** (Wu, Zhu, Shou, arXiv 2503.07314, Mar 2025, OBSERVED-PRIMARY): screenwriter → director → storyboard artist → location manager in hierarchical CoT; inputs are script + *character bank*; outputs multi-scene, multi-shot video with "character consistency, synchronized subtitles, and stable audio"; evaluated on script faithfulness, character consistency, narrative coherence.
- **MM-StoryAgent** (Xu et al., arXiv 2503.05242, Mar 2025, OBSERVED-PRIMARY): text (multi-stage writing) → role-consistent images → audio (speech, music, SFX) → assembly; evaluated on text quality and cross-modal alignment.
- **AniMaker** (arXiv 2506.10540, Jun 2025, SEARCH-ONLY): MCTS-driven clip generation with a critic agent selecting among candidates.
- **ViMax** (Huang et al., arXiv 2606.07649, Jun/Jul 2026, OBSERVED-PRIMARY): hierarchical narrative engine (RAG), "dependency-aware visual consistency system tracking character and environmental states", VLM agents that "continuously monitor and refine both narrative coherence and visual fidelity".
Common order across all: story/script → per-shot plan with entity registry → *still* keyframes/storyboard → I2V per shot → audio (VO/music) → assembly; audio is last in the academic pipelines (contrary to animation practice in C), which is why they report subtitle sync rather than VO-driven timing. Only MovieAgent takes a character bank as *input*; none takes a product bank.

### E2. Commercial tool controls for identity consistency
- **Runway Gen-4 / References** (OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://runway.com/research/introducing-runway-gen-4): "consistent characters, locations and objects across scenes"; "with a single reference image"; no fine-tuning; page states no limitations. Third-party (SEARCH-ONLY): up to 3 reference images, label them "image_1…" in prompts; neutral pose, even lighting; "95%+" consistency figures are vendor/blog claims.
- **Google Veo 3.1** (OBSERVED-PRIMARY, Gemini API docs https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo): "Up to three reference images" with `reference_type: "asset"` to "Preserve subject appearance (person, character, or product)"; first+last frame interpolation; extension by 7 s up to 20× (720p only); durations 4/6/8 s ("8 seconds only if 1080p or 4k or using reference images"); 16:9 and 9:16; "English (EN) is fully supported, but other languages have not been evaluated"; videos deleted after 2 days. Ingredients-to-Video blog (13 Jan 2026): character/object/background consistency across scenes; available in Flow, Gemini API, Vertex, Vids, YouTube Create.
- **Kling Element Library** (OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://kling.ai/quickstart/klingai-element-library-3-user-guide): an element = "at least 2 reference images (1 main reference image + 1 additional reference image)… up to 4"; types Characters, Animals, Props, Costumes & Accessories, Scenes; referenced with "@" in prompts; Video 3.0 binds "up to 3 additional elements"; Image 3.0 Omni "up to 10 images/elements".
- **fal.ai Workflows** (OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/model-apis/workflows): chain models into one endpoint, "passing outputs from one model as inputs to the next"; streaming events `submit`/`completion`/`output`/`error` expose intermediate results (a hook for per-step QA); documented example is image → background removal → sticker only.

### E3. Known failure modes and documented mitigations
- **Identity/face drift, product drift**: Higgsfield (9 Aug 2026, OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://higgsfield.ai/blog/why-ai-video-generations-fail): "Upload reference images that show the face clearly from a few different angles" or train a reusable identity; root cause "the model had to guess at something… the person generating already knew and simply didn't specify". Morphic (OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/ai-video-troubleshooting): pass "a set of images that define the character" into every generation; reuse the same character *and location* references across shots; pin aspect ratio/model/style; save as workflows.
- **Hands**: Morphic — "reduce how much the hands are asked to do", frame them out, use reference for a specific action, keep clips short. Search evidence (SEARCH-ONLY): of 7 clips requiring hand interaction, 2 usable and neither showed a full hand clearly. Direct relevance to "couple using chopsticks".
- **Text**: models "hallucinate gibberish for background text" and fail specific strings even in Veo 3.1/Wan 2.6 (SEARCH-ONLY, uselamina.ai); Google's own image generator excludes logos not supplied as input (D). Mitigation in practice: never generate on-screen copy; composite typography post-hoc.
- **Physics/motion jitter**: describe one movement, "gentle/steady" pace words, anchor camera (Morphic).
- **Selection draws**: academic pipelines (Anim-Director, AniMaker) generate N candidates and have an LMM critic pick; ViMax adds continuous VLM monitoring. Language-support caveat for Hindi audio in Veo: "other languages have not been evaluated".

### E4. TTS naturalness and casting controls (2025–2026)
- **Artificial Analysis Speech Arena** (OBSERVED-PRIMARY, Aug 2026 snapshot https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/provider-voice; Elo from blind pairwise "which sounds more natural" votes): 1 Cartesia Sonic 3.6 (1277), 2 Inworld Realtime TTS-2 (1243), 3 Speechify Simba 3.2 (1238), 4 Alibaba Qwen-Audio-3.0-TTS-Plus (1234), 5 VUI Labs Luna, 6 Inworld TTS-2 Flash, 7 StepFun StepAudio 2.5, 8 Google Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS (1204), 9 BreezeBlue, 10 ElevenLabs v3 Conversational (1197). Earlier 2026 snapshots had Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS at #1–2 and Eleven v3 at #3 (SEARCH-ONLY, X/MarkTechPost). The ranking churns monthly; no Indian-language arena exists there. Hugging Face TTS Arena V2 exists (blind A/B, Elo) but its current table was not fetched.
- **Indian languages**: Sarvam Bulbul V3 (5 Feb 2026, OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/bulbul-v3): Josh Talks blind A/B, "50 to 70 annotators per language… over 20,000 total votes from more than 500 annotators", 11 languages, vs ElevenLabs v3 alpha, v2.5 flash, Cartesia Sonic-3: full-band — "ElevenLabs v3 alpha leads on audio quality; Bulbul V3 outperforms Cartesia Sonic-3 and all other competitors"; 8 kHz telephony — "Bulbul V3 is the clear top performer". Sarvam-commissioned. PSP benchmark (Menta, arXiv 2604.25476, Apr 2026, OBSERVED-PRIMARY): ElevenLabs v3, Cartesia Sonic-3, Sarvam Bulbul, Indic Parler-TTS on Hindi/Telugu/Tamil; "no single system is Pareto-optimal across all six dimensions"; retroflex collapse grows Hindi < Telugu < Tamil (~1%, ~40%, ~68%). Net: for Hindi ad VO, ElevenLabs v3 and Bulbul V3 are the two defensible candidates; choose by *audition*, not leaderboard.
- **Casting controls exposed**:
  - ElevenLabs v3 (OBSERVED-PRIMARY docs): audio tags in brackets — emotions `[curious] [crying]`, delivery `[whispers] [shouts]`, reactions `[laughs] [sighs]`; "The most important parameter for Eleven v3 is the voice you choose. It needs to be similar enough to the desired delivery"; stability modes Creative / Natural / Robust ("Creative… prone to hallucinations"); no SSML breaks in v3. Voice Design API: `voice_description` (required), `guidance_scale` (default 5; "Higher numbers force it to stick more to the prompt"), `loudness` (0 ≈ −24 LUFS), `seed` ("Same seed with same inputs produces same voice"), `reference_audio_base64` + `prompt_strength` (ttv_v3 only), `quality`, `should_enhance`. https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/text-to-voice/design
  - Google Gemini TTS (OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation and Cloud docs): natural-language control of "style, accent, pace, and tone"; 30 named voices; multi-speaker up to 2; Hindi (`hi`/`hi-IN`) supported at GA; recommended prompt structure "Audio Profile, Scene, Director's Notes, Sample context, Transcript, Audio tags"; inline tags `[whispers] [laughs] [excited]` (3.1 Flash TTS preview). This is the closest one-to-one mapping to a VO casting brief.
  - Sarvam Bulbul (OBSERVED-PRIMARY https://docs.sarvam.ai/api-reference-docs/models/bulbul): v3 current; 11 languages; 30+ named speakers; `pace` 0.5–2.0 (v3), `pitch` and `loudness` v2 only; "Bulbul does not support SSML tags"; no emotion/style tags — prosody inferred from text. So casting on Bulbul = speaker choice + pace; emotional direction must be written into the script.

## F. Human-in-the-loop leverage

- **Where review pays**: the animation and commercial-production literature is unanimous that review value is front-loaded — animatic edits avoid animating scenes "that would otherwise be edited out" (Wikipedia/Brine); picture lock exists so that sound/colour do not rework (Frame.io); AICP makes script/storyboard the contractual baseline and prices changes after approval; Kantar reports the largest measured gains from testing at *two* stages (animatic + film, "38%") rather than once at the end. Dalim (2026): "Involve key stakeholders at the brief stage, not the review stage"; reviews "in a defined sequence—concept, copy, design, compliance". No peer-reviewed cost-of-late-rejection study specific to advertising creative was found (UNKNOWN); the quantified claims are vendor (Kantar) numbers.
- **Uncertainty-triggered review**: Learning-to-defer / selective prediction literature (Madras et al. 2017 "Predict Responsibly"; Mozannar et al. AISTATS 2023 "Who Should Predict?"; "Learning to Defer: A Survey", 2025, SEARCH-ONLY) — the model abstains below a confidence threshold and routes to a human; risk–coverage trade-off. LLM-specific: Bachar et al., "LLM Performance Predictors: Learning When to Escalate in Hybrid Human-AI Moderation Systems", arXiv 2601.07006, Jan 2026, AAMAS 2026 (OBSERVED-PRIMARY): meta-model on "log-probabilities, entropy, and novel uncertainty attribution indicators" gives "significant improvements over existing uncertainty estimators in accuracy-cost trade-offs" and distinguishes ambiguous-content from ambiguous-policy failures. HITL surveys (Emergent Mind topic page; PMC systematic review 2026; Budd et al. 2019; Natarajan et al. 2024): "Human review prioritizes the annotation of informative or uncertain examples via mechanisms such as uncertainty sampling"; review engages "when model confidence drops or ambiguity arises".
- **Reading for creative pipelines (INFERENCE)**: the highest-value minute is the one spent confirming *decisions the model had to guess* (Higgsfield's root cause) — proposition, product role, hook, casting — because those are cheap to state and expensive to discover after render. Final QA should be threshold-gated (automated checks for safe-zone, legibility, loudness, duration; human only on flagged or low-confidence items). Concept sign-off should be unconditional (always human) because no automated confidence estimate exists for "is this the right idea".

## Implications for the requirement model and review points (≤600 words)

**Three review points are supported by the evidence, in this order:** (1) *Brief-complete gate* — a human confirms the twelve decisions in A2 (objective/clock, who, to-what, proposition, proof, product role, distinctive assets, tone, mandatories, deliverables/formats, budget, approver). This is the tissue-session/PPM analogue and the AICP "scripts and storyboards supplied by Agency/Client" baseline. (2) *Animatic gate* — script locked, VO recorded and timed, stills/storyboard approved, shot durations derived from the VO track, safe-zone overlay applied to the boards. This is where Kantar's two-stage testing gain and the animation "audio first" rule live. (3) *Picture-lock/QA gate* — automated checks (safe zones per platform template, caption legibility, loudness per target, duration, enhancement flags) with human review only on flagged items, plus a mandatory human look at any on-screen text and product/face identity across shots.

**Universal requirements (all video classes):** hook in first 2–6 s; brand/product recognisable early *in context*; CTA/offer at close via text card and/or VO; text legible muted; on-screen copy composited, never generated; identity references (character bank + product bank) supplied to every shot; VO before timing; loudness target + true-peak ceiling declared; enhancement state declared at delivery.

**Per job class — universal vs specific:**
- *Static performance ad / poster / banner*: universal = single proposition, proof line, CTA, brand asset, legibility at target size; specific = platform pixel specs, text-to-image ratio policies, print bleed. No hook/VO/loudness fields.
- *Product hero / in-scene*: universal = product identity from approved still (i2v from approved frame; ≤3–4 reference images per Veo/Kling), logos-in-context beats overlay; specific = 4:5 centre-crop survivability for Meta feed.
- *Offer ad*: universal = offer terms as mandatories, urgency (ABCD "Inspire"), specific CTA verb; specific = legal disclaimer conventions by market/category.
- *UGC / spokesperson*: universal = on-screen person says the brand (ABCD "Mention"), early branding engineered explicitly (System1: creators halve brand ID), casting brief (age/tone/accent) before voice; specific = lo-fi register (TikTok) vs polished (YouTube).
- *Tutorial / demo*: universal = hands and product interaction are the highest-risk generative elements — require reference stills of the action or real footage, "reduce how much the hands are asked to do"; consideration-objective ABCD (show function). Specific: none beyond platform.
- *Explainer*: universal = script lock → VO → timing to track; supers as composited text; specific = duration norms by placement (Shorts ≤60 s plays; CTA overlay at 3/10 s).
- *Product story / multi-shot commercial*: universal = entity registry with consistency groupings (VideoDirectorGPT/MovieAgent), same character+location references reused per shot, emerging story arc with brand cues throughout; specific = emotional/functional mix per objective.
- *Social Reel*: universal = 9:16 master with critical content in centre band; specific = Meta 14/35/6%, TikTok template-defined, YouTube PNG template — do not hard-code one platform's numbers into another.
- *Hindi / Indian-language ad*: universal = same structure; specific = TTS choice by audition (ElevenLabs v3 vs Bulbul V3), no style tags on Bulbul (emotion must be scripted), Veo audio "not evaluated" for non-English, retroflex/number pronunciation checks, script transliteration review by a native reader (human gate, not automatable today).
- *Campaign variant pack*: universal = one approved master, variants differ only in ratio/duration/CTA/legal/language; QA = variant-diff, not re-review; specific = per-placement specs and enhancement opt-outs.

**Do not hard-code:** "brand in 3 s" (Meta-specific; YouTube says 5 s; TikTok says subtle in hook), any safe-zone pixel value (platform-template dependent and changed in Mar 2026), social loudness targets (no standard), "UGC outperforms" (attention yes, branding no), a single TTS "best" (leaderboards churn monthly).

## Findings ranked (by load-bearing weight for the requirement model)

1. The brief fields agencies treat as mandatory are stable across sources (Wikipedia textbook synthesis; Ogilvy template; GWTB; Binet & Field clock; Ehrenberg-Bass assets) — the failing items in the task context (proposition, product role, proof, CTA, casting) are *all* standard brief fields, so the gap is intake, not generation. (A1–A2)
2. Google ABCD playbook gives verbatim, sourced structural rules (2+ shots in 5 s; brand in 5 s; on-screen person mentions brand > VO; logo on product for recall, super for consideration; specific CTA verbs; text card/VO for CTA) with n=5,000/6,000/15,000 ad datasets. (B1)
3. System1×TikTok (887 ads, 92k viewers): early branding in context ≈ +85–92% lift on memory/awareness/image; sonic asset in 2 s +191%; **logo overlays reduce brand awareness**; creators +39% attention but half the brand ID. (B4)
4. Meta safe zone is officially 14% / 35% / 6% for 9:16; no official 4:5 or 1:1 margins; TikTok and YouTube publish templates, not numbers. (B2–B3)
5. Audio-first is a hard animation rule; picture lock is the gate before sound; AICP contract makes script/storyboard the pre-money baseline; Kantar claims 38% from two-stage testing. (C, F)
6. EBU R128 s1 gives the only formal short-form ad loudness spec (−23 LUFS, max short-term −18 LUFS, −1 dBTP); Netflix −27 LKFS dialogue-gated ±2 LU, −2 dBTP; WCAG 20 dB speech-over-background. (C)
7. Every published agentic pipeline orders script → shot plan/entity registry → stills → I2V → audio → assembly and only MovieAgent takes a character bank as input; none takes a product bank; audio is last (the opposite of animation practice). (E1)
8. Vendor identity controls are convergent: ≤3 asset references (Veo), 2–4 images per element (Kling), 1–3 references (Runway); documented failure modes (faces, hands, text) all have the same mitigation — reference images and i2v from approved stills; text should not be generated. (E2–E3)
9. TTS: leaderboard leaders churn (Cartesia/Inworld/Gemini/ElevenLabs within ~80 Elo); for Hindi the evidence supports ElevenLabs v3 and Sarvam Bulbul V3 with no Pareto winner; casting controls differ sharply (Gemini prompt-directed, ElevenLabs tags + voice design, Bulbul speaker+pace only). (E4)
10. Meta and Google both document that platforms may alter or generate creative (31 Advantage+ enhancements; PMax asset generation) and place the review obligation on the advertiser — QA must record enhancement state. (B2, D)
11. HITL literature supports uncertainty-triggered review for *final QA* but offers no confidence estimator for *concept* correctness — concept sign-off must be unconditional human. (F)
