# Checker input — five customer briefs, two production plans each

You are the independent checker. You did not write any of these plans. For each brief there are two plans, **Plan X** and **Plan Y**. Evaluate each plan on its own against the brief and the permitted facts. Do not compare the two plans to each other, do not say which is better, do not guess who wrote them, and do not rewrite them. Use only what is written in this file.

For every plan, produce:

**Part A — four questions, each scored 0 (no), 1 (partly), 2 (yes) with one sentence of evidence quoted or closely paraphrased from the plan:**
A1. Did the plan correctly establish what the customer asked for, without silently adding requirements the customer did not state or dropping ones they did?
A2. Did the plan identify and resolve the factual, structural, creative and production questions that this job needs before money is spent?
A3. Are the plan's decisions concrete enough to execute (shots, timings, text, voice, formats, order of work) rather than described in general terms?
A4. Did the plan introduce unsupported assumptions, facts not in the permitted sources, unnecessary questions, or complexity the brief did not ask for? (2 = none found; 1 = one found; 0 = more than one) — name what you found.

**Part B — twelve checklist lines, YES or NO, one sentence of evidence each. If the plan does not say, answer NO.**
1. Is the product (or, for the Upwork intro, the finished ad work) named as the hero, and is it in the first frame and the last frame?
2. Is there a brand mark or spoken brand name within the first 3–5 seconds, and is the brand present throughout?
3. Does the film ask the viewer to do something (a URL, a verb, an offer, "link in bio"), on screen and/or spoken?
4. Does the film end on the product or brand (a pack shot, app screen, or end card)?
5. Is the opening a close-up or mid-action moment rather than a wide establishing shot or a logo?
6. Is the key event the brief asked for specified as a concrete frame or shot? (Cumin: the boy fails. Nivaas: same people in exterior and interior. Upwork intro: the QA moment where a wrong character is caught. RentOK: the turn from chaos to the clean system. Skincare: the demonstration.)
7. Which numbered shot, beat or frame would sell the product on its own without reading any copy? Answer YES only if the plan names a specific numbered shot or frame and describes what is in it; a claim such as "the hero image sells at a glance" without a numbered frame is NO.
8. Are voice-over or spoken-line durations measured (or planned to be measured) before the shot timings are fixed?
9. Is the voice cast by listening to candidates on a full line, before the visuals are made?
10. Is on-screen text kept off hands, faces and the product in every delivered format, with each format composed separately rather than cropped?
11. Do the on-screen text lines match the spoken lines where both exist?
12. Which shot carries the highest generative risk (hands, objects in contact, falling objects, faces speaking, legible screens in motion)? Answer YES only if the plan names that shot and tests it first.

**Part C — two lines:**
C1. List every consequential decision this plan leaves unresolved or vague (things a producer would have to invent on the day). Give the list; "none" if none.
C2. List every claim, fact, number or platform rule the plan states that is not supported by the brief or the permitted facts given below. "none" if none.

Output for each brief: `## B<n> — Plan X` then A1–A4, B1–B12, C1, C2; then the same for Plan Y; then one line `B<n>: Plan X A=<sum>/8 B=<k>/12 · Plan Y A=<sum>/8 B=<m>/12`. Nothing else.
