Product facts: Cumin Co. sells a 15 cm Ceramic Ramen Bowl in mint (Kairi) and Rosé glazes with a moulded rim notch that holds chopsticks or a spoon, plus acacia chopsticks. The brand's blog says "the bottom stick stays still, the top stick does all the work". The brand supplied 16 photographs (packshots and lifestyle). Deliverables: a short vertical video for Instagram Reels plus 4:5 and 1:1 versions. Spend cap USD 8.

## The checklist (answer per plan)

1. Is the product (the bowl) named as the hero, and is it in the first frame and the last frame?
2. Is there a brand mark or spoken brand name within the first 3 seconds, and is the brand present throughout?
3. Does the film ask the viewer to do something (a URL, a verb, an offer), on screen and/or spoken?
4. Does the film end on the product (a pack shot or end card showing the product)?
5. Is the opening a close-up or mid-action moment rather than a wide establishing shot?
6. Is the story event the customer asked for ("the boy fails") specified as a concrete frame or shot?
7. Is there a single frame described that would sell the bowl on its own, without reading any copy?
8. Are voice-over durations measured (or planned to be measured) before the shot timings are fixed?
9. Is the voice cast by listening to candidates on a full line before the visuals are made?
10. Is on-screen text kept off hands, faces and the bowl in every delivered format, with 4:5 and 1:1 composed separately rather than cropped?
11. Do the on-screen text lines match the spoken lines where both exist?
12. Is the hardest shot to generate identified and tested first?

## Five worked negative examples (from the knowledge library; use them as calibration, not as rules)

- **qa_abcd_0010**: a 20-s ad opens on a silent slow-motion product beauty shot, brings the logo up only at the end, shows no people, closes on a "Buy now" card. Judged by Google's ABCD source: branding arrives last (fails "early and throughout"), no people (fails "make people core"), silent open (fails audio attention). The end card itself is not wrong.
- **qa_abcd_0019**: an ad with no brand presence until a five-second logo lockup at the end fails all three: show up early and throughout; reinforce with audio (no spoken brand); draw on all branding assets (only one asset type used).
- **qa_abcd_0011**: putting the call to action in the first two seconds is the opposite of the source's only placement instruction: "present the ask after the context is set".
- **qa_ogx_0073**: a 30-s script that opens on eight seconds of wordless atmosphere, names the brand at 22 s, and carries a super that paraphrases the voice-over fails Ogilvy on three counts: open with the fire; use the name within ten seconds; the super must carry exactly the words of the soundtrack.
- **qa_lsmx_0039**: a white product on a white ground reads as "product on grey" if the subject carries the tonal extreme; relight so the background carries the extreme and the subject sits just inside it.

## PLAN X

**Completed brief.** Objective: consideration (show the product fitting into the viewer's life); spec film read first by the founders. Audience: urban Indian young adults planning a noodle night at home. Proposition (flagged for the customer's veto): "The bowl built for noodle night — it even holds your chopsticks when you give up." Proof: the rim notch shown doing its job; bowl size and glaze from the packshot; no claims beyond the product page. Product role: hero; the bowl is in frame 0 and the last frame; the chopstick lesson is the device that makes the notch matter. Distinctive assets: ask whether the embossed mark may appear; mint/rosé glazes, rim notch, acacia sticks preserved from the photographs. Tone: calm, warm, unhurried Indian-English female voice-over; not bright or high-energy. Mandatory text: the five blog fragments plus product name "15 cm Ceramic Ramen Bowl" and "cuminco.com" (asked for now). Deliverables: 9:16 master; 4:5 and 1:1 composed from their own plates, each with a reserved copy zone. Questions to the customer: objective default OK; embossed mark allowed; proposition veto; add product name + URL to the deck; spoon ending.

**Decisions.** Opening: a macro of the mint bowl with steam, his crossed chopsticks and a noodle sliding back; wordmark small top-left from frame 0; no wide two-shot. Brand: persistent wordmark from frame 0; the bowl in every frame; packshot end card; voice says "Cumin Co." at the close. Ask: "cuminco.com" on the end card and spoken, placed at the end after the story. Close: end card built by code from the brand's own packshot photograph (same finish as the film's bowls) with product name, brand, URL. Hero frame: macro of his chopsticks parked in the rim notch while a spoon lifts broth, held at least 3 seconds. Supers: the three step lines are spoken verbatim as the voice-over for those beats. Copy placement: each plate prompt reserves an empty wall zone for its geometry; 4:5 and 1:1 plates are drawn, not cropped; an obstruction check with a subject box runs before delivery. Timing: voice-over recorded and measured first; beat durations equal voice duration plus a hold; film length derived (about 20–23 s), not fixed in advance. Voice: three candidates auditioned on the longest line at final pace before any plate is made; customer's ear decides; direction "calm, warm". Cuts: new information per cut; holds set by content; beat 2 must show the settle as motion within 2 seconds. Faces: "mouths closed, nobody speaks" on any clip with a face under a voice line; prefer hands-only frames under voice. End card: packshot composited on a slightly darker warm ground than the glaze. Hardest shot: the miss in beat 4 (crossing sticks, falling noodle) is tested first with two draws budgeted; on-screen text and the end card are composited by code.

**Six-frame board (durations from measured voice).** F1 macro: mint bowl, steam, crossed chopsticks, noodle sliding back, wordmark at frame 0; voice "First noodle night with chopsticks." F2 macro: her hand, bottom stick settles (motion); super and voice "The bottom stick stays still." F3 macro: pencil pinch lifts a bundle; super and voice "The top stick does all the work." F4 macro: his hand, tips cross, the noodle falls; super and voice "Pinch and release." F5 macro on the rim: his chopsticks parked in the notch, spoon lifting; her laugh only in a separate two-shot with no voice under it; voice "It takes a few tries. The bowl doesn't mind." F6 end card: product name / Cumin Co. / cuminco.com; voice "Ramen night at home. Cumin Co. — cuminco.com."

**Order.** Brief and five questions to the customer → voice cast by ear → plates for every geometry including the notch shot → board with measured voice shown to the customer → test the miss shot first → remaining clips → assembly through the shared checks (schedule, obstruction, contrast, bounds, safe zones) → release.
