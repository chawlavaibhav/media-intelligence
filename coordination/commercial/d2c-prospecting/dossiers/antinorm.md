## 1. Brand overview

New Delhi-based D2C beauty and personal-care brand, founded **2024** by **Aparna Saxena** — a single,
publicly identifiable founder with a background spanning fintech and venture capital, who describes
founding the brand to solve her own problem as a time-starved modern consumer.

The proposition is **multifunctional, high-performance products that replace multi-step routines**, with
a stated focus on **climate-suited** formulations for Indian conditions — aimed at working Indian women.

Commercial shape (INFERRED, 2026-09-14):
- **₹28 Cr seed, January 2026**, led by **Fireside Ventures**, with **V3 Ventures** and **Rukam
  Capital** both increasing earlier commitments.
- **Three products today:** a **dry shampoo**, an **all-in-one hair cream**, and a **lip treatment** —
  each positioned as an alternative to a multi-step regime.
- **Up to seven new products planned over the coming year**, across beauty, personal care and hybrid
  categories.
- Claims early traction with **double-digit month-on-month repeat purchase rates** (brand's own claim,
  unverified).
- Funds to go to digital and offline distribution, R&D, scaling D2C, and **hiring across product
  development, growth, R&D and operations**.

**Why this brand despite the small base.** The honest case is odds, not ceiling. A 2024-founded brand
with three SKUs, a solo founder, no established creative partner, ₹28 Cr of credible consumer-VC money
and seven launches ahead is about as likely as a target gets to actually *read and answer* excellent
unsolicited work — and to still be forming the visual language we would be proposing to build.

## 2. Selected product

**The all-in-one hair cream.**

**Why this product.** Of the three SKUs it is the one whose entire proposition is *replacement* — one
product instead of several — which is a visual idea rather than a claim, and can be shown without
stating any efficacy benefit. A dry shampoo's benefit is hard to depict honestly without a
before/after performance claim; a lip treatment pushes toward skin and lip-colour fidelity, which is
where generation is weakest. The hair cream is a **hard-surface tube or jar** that can carry a
product-led hero frame with no face in shot.

## 3. Current positioning

Multitasking, climate-suited, performance-led beauty and personal care that compresses a routine — for
working Indian women who do not have time for multi-step regimes. Positioned against the *norm* of
elaborate routines, as the name states.

## 4. Target consumer

Working Indian women in metros, time-constrained, willing to pay for efficiency rather than indulgence.
Buys online; values doing less rather than more. Sceptical of routine-maximalism.

## 9. Claims we can safely make

Only published brand copy, once actually read. Safe on present evidence: the product name, the **exact
published price**, the category, and the **multitasking/all-in-one positioning the brand states about
itself**. Price and offer are the deterministic-overlay case — exact by construction.

## 10. Claims we must NOT infer

- **No efficacy or performance claims** — hold time, repair, hydration, hair health, damage reduction,
  "replaces N products" as a measurable claim — unless published verbatim for that exact SKU.
- **No "climate-suited" claim as a technical assertion.** It is the brand's positioning language; do not
  convert it into a performance property.
- No dermatological, clinical or ingredient-safety claims. No "clean", "non-toxic", "cruelty-free" or
  sustainability claims unless published.
- No repeat-rate, traction, growth or revenue claims — the double-digit repeat figure is the brand's own
  claim in a funding announcement, not a verified fact, and must never appear in creative.
- Nothing about their ad performance.

## 11. Creative whitespace

Argued from structure rather than observation (see §5): a brand with three SKUs, seven launches ahead
and money to hire a growth team has to establish a **repeatable production language now** — and is at
precisely the moment when it is cheapest for it to adopt one from outside rather than build one
internally. Arriving with a working system before the seven launches is better timing than arriving
after.

The proposition itself is unusually visual for personal care: *one product replacing several* is a
picture, not a claim, and it can be made without stating a single efficacy benefit.

## 12. Creative territories (high level — the production process chooses)

1. **Subtraction as the image.** The clutter of a multi-step routine resolving into one object. States
   the brand's whole positioning without asserting any performance claim.
2. **The object, elevated.** A single tube treated with real material seriousness — light, surface,
   shadow — with the exact price composed by code. A premium standard the category rarely gets at this
   stage of a brand's life.
3. **One grammar, seven launches.** A single deterministic visual system demonstrated on the hair cream
   and explicitly extensible to the seven products coming. The territory that makes the recurring case
   rather than describing it.

## 13. AI production risks

- **Evidence is thinnest of the Top 3.** No observed creative, no confirmed domain, no confirmed social
  handle, and no confirmation that the brand runs paid media. This dossier is the least ready to
  produce from, and that is a deliberate statement rather than an omission.
- **Brand-exact pack identity.** As with the other two: the supplied-photo route that would guarantee
  fidelity is **not available** — `IMG-EDIT`/`IMG-REF` evidence is directional only and routes to a
  person, and a static ad with a supplied product photo needs `image_generation` +
  `reference_fidelity` from one call, which **no route currently answers** (`runtime/ALPHA-1.md`,
  DEF-8).
- **Claims discipline is the live risk in this category.** Hair and lip care pull hard toward efficacy
  claims; the selected territories are chosen specifically to avoid needing any. Hold that line.
- **Exact copy must be code-composed** onto a textless plate, never generated in-scene (C-7) — the
  mechanism with 4/4 clean evidence (RR-1).
- **Motion:** one short silent clip only, derived only from the accepted still.
- **No faces**, which avoids the identifiable-person consent gate entirely.
- **SKU count may have changed.** Three products is a January-2026 figure; some of the seven may have
  shipped. Verify before building.
