## 1. Brand overview

Mokobara is an Indian premium travel-goods brand — hard-shell luggage, backpacks, work bags, totes,
travel accessories and wallets — founded in 2020 by **Sangeet Agrawal** and **Navin Parwal**, both
formerly of Urban Ladder. It is a design-led brand in the literal sense: its public argument is about
industrial design decisions, not about travel aspiration.

**Commercial position (INFERRED — press, fetched 2026-09-17):**

| | |
|---|---|
| FY25 revenue from operations | **₹230.15 Cr**, up **96%** from ₹117.44 Cr in FY24 |
| FY25 net loss | ₹10.18 Cr (from ₹4.24 Cr) |
| Latest round | **₹170 Cr (~$18 Mn) Series C**, September 2026, **led by Sauce.vc** (₹109 Cr of it), with Peak XV Partners, AYRA Ventures, Niveshaay |
| Reported terms | ~**2.8× valuation premium** (Entrackr) |
| Prior round | $12 Mn Series B led by Peak XV, Feb 2024, at roughly $80 Mn |

**Catalogue (OBSERVED 2026-09-17):** **107 products**. Collections are sliced three ways
simultaneously — by **gifting occasion** (`/birthday`, `/anniversary`, `/new-beginnings`), by
**price band** (`/under-5k`, `/under-10k`, `/above-10k`), and by **campaign** (six `wa-*` collections,
i.e. WhatsApp campaign landing sets). A `secret-clearance-sale-2-0` collection is live.

- Current featured launch (Transit Z): *"Every street, every airport, every work desk. The Transit Z,

The positioning is **design rigour**, not travel romance. The brand asks to be judged on decisions —
the detail, the intelligence of the design, the object doing its job. It does not sell escapism.

This matters for production: creative that leans on destination/wanderlust imagery would be *off*
this brand, however attractive. Creative that makes the object's detail legible is *on* it.

## 4. Target consumer

**ESTIMATE, drawn from price points and catalogue structure (OBSERVED), not from brand research:**

Urban Indian professionals and frequent travellers, roughly 25–40, who buy design as a signal. Price
bands of ₹4,299–₹12,999 place it above mass luggage and below international premium. The presence of
`/under-5k`, `/under-10k` and `/above-10k` as explicit navigation implies deliberate segmentation by
willingness to pay. Backpacks and work bags described against *"every street, every airport, every
work desk"* indicate a daily-carry commuter buyer alongside the travel buyer.

The dedicated **gifting** collections (`/birthday`, `/anniversary`, `/new-beginnings`) indicate a
substantial **gift** purchase occasion — the buyer is often not the user. That is a different creative
job from self-purchase and is currently served by collection structure rather than, as far as we can
see, by distinct creative.

## 11. Creative whitespace

**The observed absence.** A brand whose entire public argument is *"the joy is in the details"* has
built a large **still** library and has **nothing** on the one public channel where motion would live.
The details they are proudest of — a zip pull, a hinge, a wheel housing, the exact colour of
*Money Moves Sunray* — are things a photograph **states** and a short motion piece can
**demonstrate**. That is a format gap, not a quality gap, and it can be pitched without criticising a
single thing they have made.

**The structural absence.** Their catalogue is sliced by gifting occasion, by price band, and by
WhatsApp campaign — at least three parallel audience taxonomies over 107 products with up to 11
colourways each. That implies far more creative contexts than any conventional production pipeline
economically fills. **The gap is volume**, and volume is precisely what we sell.

**The occasion absence.** Gifting is served by *collection structure*. Whether it is served by
*distinct creative* is unknown — but the gifting buyer is a different person from the user, wanting a
different message, during a festive window that is open right now.

**What is emphatically not whitespace:** product photography. They are good at it and they invest in
it. Any pitch premised on "your photography needs work" would be wrong and would land badly with a
former creative director.

## 12. Creative territories (high-level — production chooses)

Three directions, deliberately left short. **Production selects and develops; this dossier does not
decide.** All three are inside Alpha 1: a static plate with code-composed exact copy, optionally
followed by one short silent motion version derived from the accepted still.

**A. The detail, made undeniable.**
Take the brand's own headline literally. The plate is built around one engineered detail at a scale
photography does not usually permit — the object treated as industrial design, not luggage. Price and
offer composed by code. *Strength:* it is the brand's own argument, so it cannot be off-brand.
*Risk:* stitching and weave fidelity is exactly where generation fails; this direction puts the
hardest thing in the centre of the frame.

**B. One composition, five colourways.**
A single strong product composition, re-rendered across all five named colourways as a set. The
deliverable is not an image but a **system** — visible proof that the next colourway, and the next
SKU, costs a fraction of the first. *Strength:* it demonstrates the commercial proposition rather than
describing it, and directly answers "could this become 50 pieces of work?". *Risk:* colour accuracy
must be exact on proprietary named colours, or the set reads as sloppy rather than systematic.

**C. The object in an impossible context.**
A product-led plate that places the backpack in a constructed, stylised environment no photoshoot
could stage, while keeping the product itself literal and exact. *Strength:* the clearest demonstration
of what AI production adds over a photographer, and the most obviously reusable per-campaign template.
*Risk:* furthest from their current restrained visual language — could read as *not-Mokobara* to a
design-led team. Territory B is the safer opening; C is the higher-ceiling follow-up.

**Recommended sequence, not instruction:** open with **B**, because it argues the commercial case
while carrying the lowest fidelity risk.

## 13. AI production risks

Ordered by how likely each is to sink the work.

1. **Fabric and weave fidelity — highest risk.** A premium backpack is texture: ripstop grain, webbing,
   stitch pitch, zip teeth. This is where generation fails first, and Mokobara's buyer is a designer
   who will see it instantly. *Mitigation:* favour compositions where fabric reads at a controlled
   scale; consider the supplied-photo edit route.
2. **Logo and wordmark fidelity.** The "mokobara" wordmark must be exact. **Never generate it.**
   Composite it, as Alpha 1 does with exact copy. Source file not yet located (§8).
3. **Colourway accuracy.** *Money Moves Sunray* is a specific proprietary colour. A wrong colour makes
   the ad unusable to them regardless of how good it looks. *Mitigation:* sample hex values from the
   35 reference images; verify against the PDP swatches.
4. **Hardware geometry.** Buckles, sliders, wheel housings and hinges are rigid, repeated forms that
   generation tends to soften or mis-count.
5. **Supplied-photo routing.** If the reliable path is editing their own packshot, that is
   `IMG-EDIT` / `IMG-REF` — **directional evidence only**, and today routed to a person, not
   automatically. Plan for human involvement on that route.
6. **The C-7 boundary is not at risk here, and that is the point.** No talking head, no lip-sync, no
   native speech, no multi-shot story, and no generated in-scene exact text. This product sits
   comfortably inside the frozen family — unusually comfortably.
7. **The hard dependency, restated.** Per `coordination/CONTROL-STATE.md` §9 and the workstream README:
   **the runtime has never called a provider.** `spend_authority.status: none`. Producing anything at
   all requires a decision on where the plan comes from, live transport behind `ExecutionBridge.run()`,
   and a signed runtime spend record. **This workstream selects a brand; it cannot unblock production.**

## 15. Recurring commercial opportunity

**Why one ad could plausibly become fifty pieces of work.**

The unit of work is **the colourway**, and it is defined by their catalogue, not by our pitch:

| Multiplier | Observed value |
|---|---|
| Products live | **107** |
| Colourways on a single SKU | up to **11** (Cabin Luggage) |
| Parallel collection taxonomies | **3** (gifting occasion · price band · WhatsApp campaign) |
| Drop cadence | Transit Z live now; `Studio Special` limited 500-piece releases |
| Seasonal peaks | Festive gifting (open now); travel season |

**A plausible shape, to be tested not assumed:** *2 hero plates + 10–15 colourway variations per
month*, plus a launch pack per drop, plus festive/gifting adaptations across the three gifting
collections. Multilingual and format adaptations sit on top of the same accepted composition at near
zero marginal creative cost.

**Why they can pay for it:** ₹230.15 Cr FY25 revenue growing 96%, a net loss held to ₹10.18 Cr, and
₹170 Cr raised about two weeks before this research. They have money, they have just been given more,
and the loss figure suggests they spend it with discipline — which is the profile of a buyer who
responds to a **cost-per-asset** argument rather than a cheapest-price one. That is exactly the
argument this project is built to make.

**Timing:** the Series C is roughly two weeks old. Budget allocation for the coming year is being
decided now, and the festive gifting window is already open.
