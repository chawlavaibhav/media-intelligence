## 1. Brand overview

Gurugram-based premium colour-cosmetics brand, founded **2022** by **Kairavi Bharat Ram** (Founder &
CEO), **Shashank Tibrewal** and **Shruti Gupta**. Positioned as international-grade makeup addressing a
quality gap in the Indian market, with a playful register — press quotes the brand on turning a daily
routine "dreamatic", and its signature format is **dual-sided, multitasking products**.

Commercial shape (INFERRED, 2026-09-14):
- **₹20 Cr (≈ $2.1M) raised, announced 6 Aug 2026**, led by **Saama Capital**, with **Sanjay Kapoor**
  (founder, Genesis Luxury) and the **SRF Family Office**; existing investors including Tanaaz Bhatia
  (Borderline Media) and the **Havells** and **Eicher Motors** family offices also participated.
- **27 SKUs** across lip, eye, face and fragrance. Premium price band **₹600–900**.
- Distribution: own D2C site · **Nykaa** · **Amazon** · quick commerce (**Blinkit, Zepto**).
- Stated use of funds, in three parts: **deepen quick commerce and offline retail**; **enter skincare**
  via first hybrid skincare–makeup launches; **scale Spritz by Typsy**, the fragrance sub-brand launched
  earlier in 2026.
- **More than 10 new products stated as coming in the next few months.**
- Product development involves travel to Italy and Korea to track global trends.

## 2. Selected product

**A single SKU from Spritz by Typsy**, the fragrance sub-brand — the exact SKU to be confirmed by hand
(see §13).

**Why this product, not a lipstick.** Fragrance is deliberately chosen over colour cosmetics for one
hard production reason: a **fragrance bottle is a hard-surface, non-skin object** with no shade
fidelity problem and no need for a face in frame. Colour cosmetics ads live or die on exact shade,
texture and swatch-on-skin accuracy, which is precisely where current generation is weakest and where a
spec ad would be judged as wrong rather than beautiful. Spritz is also a **named growth priority of the
fresh round**, so it is the SKU the founders are most invested in right now.

## 3. Current positioning

Premium-but-playful, quality-led, problem-solving makeup for Indian consumers — global standards at an
accessible premium price, with multitasking formats that compress a routine.

## 4. Target consumer

Urban Indian women, roughly 18–34, buying at ₹600–900 — above mass, below luxury. Discovers on
Instagram, buys on Nykaa, Amazon or quick commerce, and is increasingly served in ten minutes rather
than two days. Values efficiency (dual-sided products) and enjoys the brand's humour.

## 10. Claims we must NOT infer

- **No cosmetic performance claims** — wear time, transfer-proof, SPF, "8-hour", waterproof,
  dermatologist-tested — unless published verbatim for that exact SKU.
- **No skincare or efficacy claims of any kind.** The hybrid skincare launches are new and unread;
  skincare claims are a regulatory area, not a creative one.
- No fragrance-note or longevity claims unless published.
- No "clean", "non-toxic", "cruelty-free" or sustainability claims unless published.
- No bestseller, ranking or revenue claims. Nothing about their ad performance.

## 11. Creative whitespace

Three things arrive at once: **10+ launches in a few months**, a **fragrance sub-brand being scaled**,
and a **simultaneous quick-commerce and offline push**. That combination produces more demand for
SKU-level creative than a founder-led organic content engine can absorb — every launch needs a hero
asset plus price and offer variants for Nykaa, Amazon, Blinkit and Zepto.

The likely gap is a **premium product-led visual language that scales per launch** — something that
makes a ₹700 product look like it belongs at ₹2,000, repeatably, without a shoot per SKU. Quick-commerce
creative in particular is price-and-pack-led, which is exactly the deterministic-overlay use case.

## 12. Creative territories (high level — the production process chooses)

1. **The bottle as the whole world.** A Spritz bottle rendered with genuine material seriousness —
   glass, light, refraction — at a premium standard the ₹600–900 price point does not normally receive,
   with the exact price composed by code.
2. **Scent made visible.** Fragrance's standing creative problem is that the product cannot be shown
   doing anything. A generative treatment can make the invisible visible — the note as form, colour or
   impossible material — which is a real advantage over a shoot rather than a substitute for one.
3. **One grammar, every launch.** A single deterministic visual system shown across a fragrance, a lip
   and a face SKU — pitched as the production language for the 10+ launches already coming. The
   territory most likely to convert into recurring work.

## 13. AI production risks

- **Colour-cosmetics fidelity is the reason fragrance was chosen.** Exact shade, finish and
  swatch-on-skin accuracy is where generation is weakest. If the production process moves to a lip or
  face SKU, that risk returns in full.
- **Brand-exact bottle and pack identity.** A generated bottle that is *almost* theirs undermines the
  whole pitch. The supplied-photo route that would fix it is **not available**: `IMG-EDIT`/`IMG-REF`
  evidence is directional only and routes to a person, and a static ad with a supplied product photo
  needs `image_generation` + `reference_fidelity` from one call, which **no route currently answers**
  (`runtime/ALPHA-1.md`, DEF-8). Resolve before production.
- **Exact copy must be code-composed** onto a textless plate, never generated in-scene (C-7) — the
  mechanism with 4/4 clean evidence (RR-1).
- **Motion:** one short silent clip only, derived only from the accepted still.
- **No faces.** Avoids the identifiable-person consent gate entirely, which is a genuine advantage of
  the product-only approach here.
- **Which Spritz SKU is current is unknown.** The sub-brand launched "earlier this year" and 10+
  launches are in flight; the hero SKU must be confirmed by hand or the ad may be built on a
  discontinued or superseded product.
- **The official domain was not confirmed.** Resolve before anything is downloaded.

## 15. Recurring commercial opportunity

Plausible shape, to be tested not asserted: **creative around every SKU launch** — with 10+ launches
stated for the coming months, that alone is 10+ hero assets — plus per-SKU price and offer variants
across Nykaa, Amazon, Blinkit and Zepto, plus festive moments. A workable retainer framing is
**2–4 hero product films per month plus 10–15 platform variations**, scaling with the launch calendar.

The multilingual angle is worth naming in any pitch: because exact copy is **composed by code, not
drawn by the model**, it is exact in any script — Devanagari included — at no extra generation risk.
That is a concrete, defensible advantage for a brand pushing into Tier-2 quick commerce.
