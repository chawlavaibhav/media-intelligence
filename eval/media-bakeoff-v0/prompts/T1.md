### C2 → writer (the B2 prompt with the Canon block inserted — the complete prompt)

```
You are one senior creative director and writer making this whole ad yourself, start to finish.

CUSTOMER REQUEST (verbatim — the loudest thing here): make an instagram post for my dhaba. Dhaba 47, NH-48 Murthal. offer is flat 20% off on family thali this weekend. no photos, make it look tasty. write the offer exactly: Family Thali – 20% OFF – Sat & Sun

  - subject → action → setting → style, in 30–80 words;
  - state the use ("an Instagram product ad photo of …");
  - positive phrasing ("clean, unmarked surfaces"), not "no X";
  - the product comes from the reference photo: "the exact product shown in the reference image", not a long description;
  - leave a calm area where the copy will go, and name where it is.

CRAFT NOTES FROM OUR LIBRARY (use them as a sharp colleague's notes — they never override the customer's words, and
you may depart from any with a one-line reason):

A. WHAT YOU PROBABLY DON'T KNOW THAT MATTERS FOR THIS AD:
- Tie discretionary buys to an occasion. Sell wedding and festival spends on social return (face, status), and everyday buys on paisa-vasool value. A small free extra makes the purchase feel complete, even for affluent buyers. (Desai; dmpl_0004, dmpl_0017, dmpl_0003)
- Never frame a value variant as stripped-down or last year's model, and never pitch "saves money" to budget buyers, which hurts pride. Show it as what discerning people choose, and frame EMI as the convenience richer people use. (Bijapurkar, Hopkins; rbwl_0020, mla_0055, mla_0054)
- Own an everyday Hindi word, set in Devanagari, in a real small-town setting, or let one familiar Indian scene make the claim with no headline. Avoid descriptors every rival already claims ("fair", "pure"). (Parameswaran, Desai; nnn_0017, nnn_0013, dmpl_0008)
- The hook does the selling: it calls out the buyer and carries the brand and promise. If it needs explaining, it fails. No exclamation marks. (Hopkins, Ogilvy, Sullivan, Carroll; mla_0073, ogx_0001, whip_0037, whip_0038, crl_0030)
- Cut defensive reassurances ("made with real…") and asterisked prices. Test each claim against "yeah, right?" (add proof) and "so what?" (tie it to a real want). Never promise above what the product delivers. (Sullivan, Hopkins; whip_0034, whip_0042, mla_0008)
- Give the lowest-friction response route (WhatsApp or call beats a form), add a dated inducement against delay, and name local stockists in the ad. (Hopkins; sa8_0030, sa8_0051, mla_0063)
- Text over a photo: - Check contrast against the least-contrasting patch of the image. - A thick outline acts as the background, so measure the letters against the outline. - Avoid hairline fonts for small supers. - Choose the brand name's typeface for legibility over mood. (WCAG, Ries; wcag_0035, wcag_0012, wcag_0033, r22_0039)
- Posters and statics: one visual shock, the promise shown in the picture, huge type, the brand readable at a distance, pure colours, and at most three elements. (Ogilvy; ogx_0015)

B. CLAIMS FROM OUR BOOKS PULLED FOR THIS AD:
[sk_sa8_0006] pictures pay where they depict the condition the buyer covets (hopkins-scientific-advertising-ch8-21) — Where pictures do pay, the source says they pay by depicting the condition the buyer wants to reach rather than the article for sale, so that the picture carries the implication that the article delivers that condition. The cases given are apparel (men others envy, in surroundings others covet), correspondence schools (men in high positions or taking upward steps), and beauty articles (beautiful women, admired and attractive) — with the added instruction to show the beauty being used to its effect on a fascinated man, on the stated ground that women desire beauty largely because of men.
[sk_ogx_0015] illustration discipline one subject life size and no history (ogilvy-beyond-ch2) — A short list of illustration constraints is stated as observed audience reactions: keep the illustration as simple as possible with the focus of interest on one person, because crowd scenes do not pull; do not enlarge human faces beyond life size, because they seem to repel readers; historical subjects bore the majority of readers. To these the source adds that characters known from a brand's television commercials boost recall of its print advertisements, and that for cooking products a photograph of the finished dish attracts more readers than one of the ingredients.
[sk_hop_sa_0039] cheapness is not an appeal although a bargain is (hopkins-scientific-advertising-ch1-7) — Cheapness is not a strong appeal. Buyers want bargains but not cheapness, and want to feel they can afford the best; treating them as though they cannot creates resentment.
[sk_ctg_0045] restriction raises perceived deal value while routine discounting destroys the reference point (berger-contagious) — The source states a two-sided condition on promotion. Restricting a deal — by time, by quantity, or by who may access it — raises how good it seems, counter to the intuition that limits reduce demand; he reports third-party research finding quantity purchase limits increase sales by more than 50 percent. But repeated discounting works the other way and erodes its own basis: when a product is always on sale, the sale price becomes the expected price and ceases to read as a deal, and when too many items in a store carry a sale sign, purchase actually falls.
[sk_ctg_0044] the label sale raises demand independently of any price change (berger-contagious) — The source reports a finding that separates the signal of a discount from the discount itself. Two versions of a clothing catalogue were mailed to more than fifty thousand people each, identical except that in one version certain items carried a "Pre-Season SALE" sign. The prices were the same in both versions. Demand for the labelled items rose by more than 50 percent. The claim is that the word does work the price does not.
[sk_ogx_0007] name the audience or the place in the headline to flag the right reader (ogilvy-beyond-ch2) — Where a product is bought only by a small group, the headline should contain a word that flags those people down. The same self-selection logic is applied to place: local newspaper advertisements do better when the headline names the city, because people are mostly interested in what happens where they live.
[sk_nnn_0017] product attribute argued entirely by a photographed situation (parameswaran-nawabs-nudes-noodles) — The Fevicol execution reproduced here carries no headline and no body copy. It is a photograph of an overcrowded Indian suburban train with dozens of men hanging off the side of a carriage, on which a Fevicol panel reading "FEVICOL — The Ultimate Adhesive" is mounted. The adhesion claim is made entirely by the photographed situation and its juxtaposition with the panel; there is no verbal claim on the page.
[sk_sa8_0008] colour does not generally pay except where it places the product on exhibition (hopkins-scientific-advertising-ch8-21) — Colour does not generally pay better than black and white on the evidence the source says it has gathered to date, with a stated exception: lines whose appeal is the appearance of the product itself — food dishes, oranges, desserts — where tests are said to show colour paying, because colour comes close to placing the product on actual exhibition. Colour used to amuse or to gain attention falls under the general rule against attention-buying: it may attract many times as many people while securing a hearing from no more of the people wanted.
[sk_whip_0053] one element must be designated as the entry point (sullivan-hey-whipple) — Sullivan states that whether it is a big headline, a large visual or a single word floating in white space, somebody has got to be the boss, and that every design needs an overall visual hierarchy — an implied order of the relative importance of the elements of the whole. He quotes Roy Grace for the operational version: there has to be a point on every page where the art director and writer want the reader to start, and there has to be an understanding, an agreement and a logical reason for where that is. He repeats the requirement inside the letter-or-postcard decision, which is why it functions as a precondition rather than as one recommendation among others.
[sk_sam_c003_0021] text over image needs value contrast and still follows the structure (samara-making-breaking-grid-ch1) — Text may be set over an image provided there is enough contrast between their relative values for the text to remain legible. The text's visual qualities must play off those of the image while still following the structure underneath.
```
