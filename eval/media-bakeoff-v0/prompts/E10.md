[sk_sa8_0042] show the condition desired rather than the defect to be removed and never attack a rival (hopkins-scientific-advertising-ch8-21) — The source states a single positive-framing doctrine covering both competitors and the customer's own condition. Attacking a rival is never good advertising: it is not permitted in the best mediums, the selfish purpose is apparent, and it looks unfair. And the advertisement should depict the condition the buyer wants to arrive at rather than the defect being removed — pretty teeth not bad teeth, well-dressed people not the shabby, successful men not failures — on the stated ground that the customer already knows all about the wrinkles. The rule is extended to the instruction itself: tell people what to do rather than what to avoid, and assume compliance ("Send now for this sample") rather than imply neglect ("Why do you neglect this offer?"), which only suggests that people are neglecting. The source claims that on comparing a negative and a positive advertisement the positive outpulls the other four to one.
[sk_mla_0022] identify the real competitor which may not be a rival brand (hopkins-my-life-in-advertising) — The source reports investigating a category and finding that the competition every advertiser was fighting over was six per cent of the market, while ninety-four per cent of buyers were served by an alternative nobody was arguing against — the household making the product at home. It reports the result was an enormous demand and a price premium over rivals.
[sk_hop_sa_0051] a specific claim is believed because it could be caught being false (hopkins-scientific-advertising-ch1-7) — A specific claim is believed because it is either true or a lie, with no middle ground, and readers do not expect an advertiser to lie — they know he cannot lie in the best media. Definite statements are therefore usually accepted, actual figures are not generally discounted, and specific facts carry their full weight. The weight of an argument may often be multiplied by making it specific: a tungsten lamp said to give "more light" leaves doubt, while one said to give three and one-third times the light is believed; "Our prices have been reduced" makes no marked impression while "Our prices have been reduced 25 per cent" gets the full value of the announcement.
[sk_mla_0027] the visible trend of the crowd is the strongest argument available (hopkins-my-life-in-advertising) — The source names the observable direction of other buyers as the single most effective thing it found in advertising. Its stated reason is that buyers cannot judge value and know they cannot, so they substitute the verdict of the majority.
