CUSTOMER REQUEST (verbatim — the loudest thing here): new colour drop. Transit Backpack 30L in Crypto Sunray. make an instagram ad from our photo. text: New: Crypto Sunray and mokobara.com
ANSWERS: none
UNDERSTANDING: {produced at run time by B1}
PHOTOS: 2 attached as references — the product must look exactly like them; never describe it at length.
MEDIA MODEL: Nano Banana 2 (image, with reference photos).  HOW THIS MODEL WANTS TO BE PROMPTED:
- **Stills** (Nano Banana 2 / GPT Image 2):
  - subject → action → setting → style, in 30–80 words;
  - state the use ("an Instagram product ad photo of …");
  - positive phrasing ("clean, unmarked surfaces"), not "no X";
  - the product comes from the reference photo: "the exact product shown in the reference image", not a long description;
  - leave a calm area where the copy will go, and name where it is.

CRAFT NOTES FROM OUR LIBRARY (use them as a sharp colleague's notes — they never override the customer's words, and
you may depart from any with a one-line reason):

A. WHAT YOU PROBABLY DON'T KNOW THAT MATTERS FOR THIS AD:
- Text over a photo: - Check contrast against the least-contrasting patch of the image. - A thick outline acts as the background, so measure the letters against the outline. - Avoid hairline fonts for small supers. - Choose the brand name's typeface for legibility over mood. (WCAG, Ries; wcag_0035, wcag_0012, wcag_0033, r22_0039)
- Posters and statics: one visual shock, the promise shown in the picture, huge type, the brand readable at a distance, pure colours, and at most three elements. (Ogilvy; ogx_0015)
- A small, saturated subject can sit near a corner of a muted frame. Point the model's gaze at the product, freeze motion at the apex, and stack 9:16 frames up toward one peak. Pastel means brighter, not desaturated. (Freeman; pex_0041, pex_0015, pex_0045, pex_0006, pex_0018)
- Keep a contact shadow under cut-out products, or they float. Shoot flat products on white over a translucent, backlit base instead of trying to shade them. (Light Science & Magic; lsmx_0059, lsmx_0044)

B. CLAIMS FROM OUR BOOKS PULLED FOR THIS AD:
[sk_ogx_0006] news in the headline raises recall and must not be buried (ogilvy-beyond-ch2) — Advertisements containing news are recalled by twenty-two per cent more people on average than advertisements without. News is not restricted to a new product: it can be an improvement to an old one or a new way to use an old one. Because nine out of ten people will not read the body copy, news must be stated in the headline. The source adds that tried-and-true words — amazing, introducing, now, suddenly — should not be scorned.
[sk_ogx_0037] news is above average on television and can be manufactured for an old product (ogilvy-beyond-ch2) — Commercials containing news are above average at changing brand preference, and the source's stated reason is that products, like people, attract most attention when newly born. He complains that even when there is news to tell — which he says is rare — some copywriters underplay it or leave it out. For an old product, news can be created by advertising a new way to use it, his example being baking soda used to keep a refrigerator smelling sweet.
[sk_sa8_0008] colour does not generally pay except where it places the product on exhibition (hopkins-scientific-advertising-ch8-21) — Colour does not generally pay better than black and white on the evidence the source says it has gathered to date, with a stated exception: lines whose appeal is the appearance of the product itself — food dishes, oranges, desserts — where tests are said to show colour paying, because colour comes close to placing the product on actual exhibition. Colour used to amuse or to gain attention falls under the general rule against attention-buying: it may attract many times as many people while securing a hearing from no more of the people wanted.
[sk_alb_c003_0006] a colour is never seen alone (albers-interaction-of-color) — A single tone can be heard on its own, but a single colour is almost never seen unconnected and unrelated to other colours. Colours present themselves in continuous flux, constantly related to changing neighbours and changing conditions.
[sk_alb_c003_0013] colours differ in whether they influence or accept influence (albers-interaction-of-color) — Change in a colour's appearance is a result of influence, so the influencing colour must be distinguished from the influenced one. Some colours are hard to change and others are more susceptible, and the study seeks to identify which are inclined to exert influence and which will accept it.
[sk_alb_c003_0001] a colour name produces as many colours as listeners (albers-interaction-of-color) — Naming a colour to fifty listeners produces fifty different colours in their minds, and all of them very different. Even naming a colour everyone has seen innumerable times — the red of Coca-Cola signs, the same red across the country — still produces many different reds. Given hundreds of red samples to choose from, listeners select quite different colours, and none can be sure of having found the precise shade.
[sk_ogx_0015] illustration discipline one subject life size and no history (ogilvy-beyond-ch2) — A short list of illustration constraints is stated as observed audience reactions: keep the illustration as simple as possible with the focus of interest on one person, because crowd scenes do not pull; do not enlarge human faces beyond life size, because they seem to repel readers; historical subjects bore the majority of readers. To these the source adds that characters known from a brand's television commercials boost recall of its print advertisements, and that for cooking products a photograph of the finished dish attracts more readers than one of the ingredients.
[sk_ogx_0039] brand attribution fails by default and must be forced by name and package (ogilvy-beyond-ch2) — The default outcome, according to the source, is that a viewer remembers the commercial and forgets which product it was for, often attributing it to a competing brand. Registering the brand is therefore treated as work that must be done deliberately rather than as a by-product of a good commercial. His stated devices are to use the name within the first ten seconds and to play games with it, such as spelling it out; he reports a commercial that repeated the brand name twenty times without irritating anyone. Commercials that end by showing the package are reported more effective at changing brand preference than those that do not. He notes many copywriters think it crass to belabour the name.
[sk_sam_c003_0021] text over image needs value contrast and still follows the structure (samara-making-breaking-grid-ch1) — Text may be set over an image provided there is enough contrast between their relative values for the text to remain legible. The text's visual qualities must play off those of the image while still following the structure underneath.
[sk_whip_0049] a brand should own a visual territory recognisable before the content is read (sullivan-hey-whipple) — Sullivan reports Helmut Krone's requirement of a page style — that it should be possible to tell whose advertisement it is from twenty feet — and notes what is interesting about it: Krone was talking about print advertisements, not billboards. Sullivan endorses it and states a stronger version as his own view: the longer he has been in the business, the more convinced he is that art direction is where the major battle for brand building happens, because once a look is established and a design territory staked out, nobody else can use it without looking like your brand. He reports one instance of a look built by deliberate inversion of another: large pictures became small, small body type became large.

ADDITIONAL OUTPUT — fill after your treatment:
"decisions": {D1_idea, D2_conflict, D3_roles, D4_opening, D5_brand, D6_ask_and_end, D7_product_truth, D8_deviations} — each: D1_idea: The idea in one sentence — a specific moment in which this product matters. Do this in order:

   duration, which reference photos to attach, at most one spoken line in quotes (≤ 2.5 words per second of the scene,
   minus 0.4 s), and any ON-SCREEN COPY — copy is added by code afterwards, so NEVER ask the model to draw text, logos

4. FINISH NOTES. Where the copy/logo/end card go, and whether a music bed is wanted (one line of mood if yes).
