[sk_ogx_0040] the first frame decides whether the rest is seen and singularity is what sticks (ogilvy-beyond-ch2) — With thirty seconds available, the source treats the first frame as the gate: grabbing attention there with a visual surprise gives a better chance of holding the viewer, and commercials are screened out because they open with something dull. He states the asymmetry that makes this a real failure — the maker knows great things are coming and the viewer does not, and will have left before finding out. His instruction is literal: when advertising fire extinguishers, open with the fire. Alongside this he says visual banality fails, since a viewer exposed to thirty thousand commercials a year lets most slide off memory, so a commercial needs a touch of singularity, a visual burr that sticks; and he reports that commercials with a plethora of short scenes are on average below average at changing brand preference, conceding that one colleague handles many scenes without confusing people and that he himself cannot.
[sk_abcd_0007] open in the middle of the action or on a close up (google-abcd-video-ads) — The source names two concrete opening constructions for an ad — beginning in the middle of the action, and opening on a close-up — and offers bold imagery at the start as a third means. [sk_vig_c003_0009] visual power is achieved by contrast and is not impact (vignelli-canon-intangibles) — Design should be visually powerful, expressing content forcefully in every element. In graphic design, difference of scale within the same page gives strong impact, and bold type contrasting with light type creates dynamic impressions; in three dimensions, manipulating light through different textures and materials, and changing and contrasting scale, do the same. Visual strength can also be achieved with delicate layouts or materials. It is an expression of intellectual elegance and must never be confused with mere visual impact, which is usually visual vulgarity and obtrusiveness.
[sk_alt_c003_0025] mood is made by the character of the light not by exposure level (alton-painting-with-light-ch2) — A daylight feeling is not produced by overexposing; every negative is exposed normally, and the impression of time of day comes from the quality of the light — its direction, diffusion and the shadows it makes — rather than from its quantity.

Terrible mixes have been made from a hundred tracks and wonderful ones from three; what decides it is the initial choices, the quality of the sounds, and how capable the blend was of exciting emotions hidden in the hearts of the audience. The underlying principle is to produce the greatest effect in the viewer's mind by the least number of things on screen. The source applies it to all the crafts of filmmaking — acting, art direction, photography, music, costume — and to editing.
