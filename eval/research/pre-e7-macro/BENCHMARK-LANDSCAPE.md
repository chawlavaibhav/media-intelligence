## The five things this landscape changes in our thinking

### 1 · Requirements in a real request are **dependent**, not a flat list

This is the most structurally important finding in the program.

**Arena-T2I-Hard** takes 310 *real* hard user prompts and decomposes each into a **DAG of yes/no
questions**, checked in BFS order — *"If a parent isn't 'yes', its descendants are skipped and
counted as failed."*

Our compound bank items today declare a **flat `measurement_fanout`**: a list of capabilities, all
scored independently. On a real commercial brief that is wrong in a specific and dangerous
direction. If the model never rendered the product, then:

- `logo_wordmark_fidelity` — no logo to be wrong about
- `packaging_brand_colour_fidelity` — no packaging to sample
- `product_stability_in_clip` — nothing to stay stable

A flat fan-out can score all three as **pass**, and the asset that most completely failed the brief
comes back with the *highest* score. **The inflation is largest exactly where the output is worst.**

This does not require new capabilities. It requires the fan-out to carry **prerequisite edges**.

### 2 · The field has moved from "does it look right" to "is it intrinsically right"

**VBench-2.0** states the shift explicitly: models have progressed *"from achieving superficial
faithfulness in fundamental technical aspects such as pixel fidelity and basic prompt adherence, to
addressing more complex challenges associated with intrinsic faithfulness."*

Its 18 dimensions independently name four things our 36 either lacks or under-specifies —
**camera motion**, **motion order understanding**, **human identity/clothes as distinct from
anatomy**, and **multi-view consistency**. When an external benchmark family arrives at properties
our map does not have, that is a signal worth taking seriously, not a preference.

### 3 · Evaluator drift is now a **design problem**, not a maintenance chore

**GenEval2**'s associated paper is titled *"Addressing Benchmark Drift in Text-to-Image
Evaluation"*, and it markets Soft-TIFA as *"less likely to drift from human-alignment over time."*

**HPSv2** states two limits that are load-bearing for us:

> *"Comparison is only meaningful for images generated by the same prompt."*
> *"Scores can not be directly compared between v2.0 and v2.1."*

An evaluator **version bump breaks comparability**. Our Registry already pins `instrument_version`
and `instrument_config_hash` — this is external corroboration that the pin is not bureaucracy. It
also means benchmark-refresh triggers belong in the architecture from the start.

### 4 · Nobody uses one instrument for everything, and the good benchmarks say so

**T2I-CompBench** routes deliberately: BLIP-VQA for attribute binding, UniDet detection for spatial
and numeracy, CLIPScore for non-spatial relations, an MLLM as an alternative path. **GenEval** uses a
detector. **VBench** uses six different backbones across 16 dimensions.

This supports our six-family split. It also exposes a **specific weakness in our contract**:
T2I-CompBench separates **2D from 3D spatial relations**, while our single `spatial_relationship`
merges them — even though our own contract already notes that depth ordering *"is NOT decidable from
2D boxes"*. We wrote down the problem and then kept one capability.

### 5 · Two standard metrics we would plausibly have adopted are **contested or weak**

| Metric | Standard use | The problem |
|---|---|---|
| **LSE-C / LSE-D** (SyncNet) | The default lip-sync metric | Comparative work reports *"only LSE-O displays moderate effectiveness, casting doubt on their widespread application"* |
| **DINO / CLIP-I** | The default identity metric | A high DINO score *"may risk overfitting the identity-irrelevant information"* — it can reward shared background and call it identity |

Had we adopted either because it is standard, we would have gated production on an instrument whose
validity is disputed. **This is the strongest external argument in the program for our own rule:**
an instrument must be qualified for *our* judgement before it gates anything.

The identity finding also **independently corroborates ADD-01** (same-category decoys). An embedding
metric that rewards context similarity is precisely what a decoy catches.

## By benchmark: what it tests, how it judges, what transfers

| Source | Modality | Class | Judge | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GenEval | image | atomic compositional | detector (Mask2Former) | frame |
| GenEval2 | image | atomic compositional | VQA (Soft-TIFA) | frame |
| T2I-CompBench | image | atomic compositional | per-category: VQA / detection / CLIP / MLLM | frame |
| VBench | video | atomic dimensional | 6 backbones over 16 dimensions | sequence |
| VBench-2.0 | video | atomic dimensional | per-dimension pipelines | sequence |
| Arena-T2I-Hard | image | **real-user hard prompt** | dependency-aware DAG checklist | frame |
| HPSv2 | image | **preference model** | learned preference predictor | frame (pairwise) |
| DreamBench(++) | image | identity/reference | DINO/CLIP-I, or MLLM judge | asset set |
| SyncNet LSE-* | audio-video | metric family | pretrained SyncNet | sequence |
| TTSDS / IndicVoices-R | audio | benchmark + corpus | ASR-WER, ASV, prosody features | whole asset |

**Four classes, and they are not interchangeable.** Atomic-compositional isolates a property.
Real-user-hard tests co-occurrence under realistic load. Preference ranks relatively and cannot gate.
Corpus/metric families supply calibration material. A benchmark v2 needs the first three as
*separate axes*, which is what E7-F proposes.

## What does **not** transfer

- **No score from any of these is our score.** They did not test our conditions, our brand
  constraints, our scripts or our market. Nothing here licenses a capability claim.
- **A benchmark dimension is not market demand.** VBench measuring `aesthetic_quality` says nothing
  about whether customers ask for it. That axis is Canon's to establish, not ours.
- **Leaderboard positions are not comparable across benchmarks**, and none is quoted here.
- **`aesthetic_quality` via a learned aesthetic predictor is not commercial fitness.** It is a taste
  proxy trained on community preference and must never become a brief-satisfaction gate.
- **Open-domain object prompts are not commercial media.** GenEval's subjects are everyday objects;
  ours are brand-constrained products with exact copy.

## Gaps found in the landscape itself

Three things our product needs that I could **not** find a benchmark for on reachable surfaces:

1. **Brand-mark / wordmark fidelity under perspective and curvature.** Nothing located. Our own
   contract already records this as `required_but_no_calibrated_instrument`; the external world does
   not appear to have solved it either.
2. **Campaign / variant consistency** — N assets that must look like one campaign. No benchmark found.
3. **Exact spoken-script fidelity for code-mixed speech** (Hinglish brand names, prices). TTS
   benchmarks measure intelligibility via ASR-WER, which is a *different and weaker* question — and
   a robust ASR normalises a mispronunciation into the correct word, hiding the failure.

These are not evidence that the capabilities do not matter. They are evidence that **if we want
them, we will have to build the instrument ourselves**, and should budget accordingly.
