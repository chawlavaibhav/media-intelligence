FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
One (1) vertical performance video, 9:16, 1080×1920, ~24–26 seconds, MP4, burned-in captions, silent-autoplay-safe, optimized for Instagram Reels/Stories, YouTube Shorts and Meta Feed placements. Master delivered with captions-on (primary) and a caption-off version as a secondary cutdown asset.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
Produce a low-fidelity, high-credibility creator-style ad for a fictional Indian D2C brand's fragrance-free 2% salicylic-acid serum, targeted at adults with oily, blemish-prone skin. The video must function as a scroll-stopping performance asset (fast hook, visible demo, one clear purchase reason, soft CTA) while staying strictly within honest, non-clinical claim territory — no invented statistics, no "clears acne," no "dermatologist proven," no guarantees. Success = looks like an unpaid, unscripted phone-shot recommendation, not a studio TVC.

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
"The No-Sticky-Layer Test" — a woman filming herself at her bathroom mirror mid-routine reacts, in real time, to the one thing that made her stick with this serum: it's fragrance-free and it sinks in fast without the greasy/sticky film she's used to from other actives on oily skin. She ends by holding up her own visibly half-used bottle — a small authenticity cue that signals repeat purchase without stating any claim or number.

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Primary purchase reason (spoken + on-screen): fragrance-free, absorbs fast, no sticky/greasy layer — comfortable for daily use on oily/blemish-prone skin.
2. Product identity: brand name + "2% Salicylic Acid Serum" (factual formulation detail, not a clinical claim).
3. Trust cue: personal repurchase behavior ("this is what I keep repurchasing"), not a guarantee or statistic.
4. Soft CTA: availability cue only, no URL, no external website reference.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Framing: vertical handheld selfie-style, slight natural hand-shake, phone-camera field of view, occasional soft focus breathing (not gimbal-smooth).
- Lighting: single soft window light + practical bathroom light, warm-neutral, slightly overexposed highlights typical of phone front camera — no studio lighting rig look.
- Color: minimal grading, slightly desaturated warm skin tones, natural contrast — "iPhone memo" look, not a commercial LUT.
- Talent: Indian woman, late 20s, no heavy makeup, hair loosely tied, casual home tee/robe, subtle natural shine/texture on skin (relatable, not exaggerated or stigmatized).
- Set: home bathroom mirror or bedroom vanity with believable Indian-home clutter (towel, small plant, everyday bottles) — not a styled set.
- Typography: bold rounded sans-serif captions, white text/black soft outline, one accent-color keyword highlight box per line (matches bottle accent color), positioned lower-middle third to survive UI crop.
- Product prop: single consistent serum bottle — minimal dropper bottle, brand wordmark "Purenva," small label reading "2% Salicylic Acid Serum · Fragrance-Free" — same physical prop/reference used in every shot, shown visibly half-used in the final shot.

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
1. Lock two reference assets before any shot generation: (a) a single consistent face/identity reference of the actress, (b) a single consistent product bottle/label reference image. Feed both references into every subsequent shot generation to guarantee continuity of face and packaging across cuts.
2. Generate/shoot the six shots below in sequence at native 9:16, then edit as quick jump-cuts (typical UGC pacing) rather than smooth transitions — mismatched micro-jumps between shots reinforce authenticity.
3. Record/generate dialogue as native sync audio per shot (not a separate VO layer) to preserve lip-sync realism.
4. Add captions, keyword highlight boxes, and end-card text in post as deterministic overlays (not part of generation) for accuracy control.
5. Light color pass only: reduce saturation ~10%, keep exposure phone-realistic, no beauty smoothing.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- Identity drift risk: mitigated by locking one face reference across all six shots.
- Product inconsistency risk: mitigated by locking one bottle/label reference image across all six shots, including the "half-used" detail only appearing in Shot 6.
- Claim-creep risk: script and captions audited to ensure no percentages/results claims beyond the factual "2% salicylic acid, fragrance-free" formulation facts; no "clears," "cures," "guaranteed," "dermatologist-recommended" language used anywhere.
- Over-polish risk: explicit direction against studio lighting, gimbal smoothness, or beauty retouching in every prompt to preserve UGC credibility.
- Representation risk: skin shown with realistic, non-stigmatizing mild oiliness/texture — avoided exaggerated or prosthetic acne depiction.
- CTA risk: no website/link language used anywhere in script, captions, or end card, per constraint.
- Trademark risk: "Purenva" is a fictional name for this production; recommend a trademark clearance check before any real commercial deployment.
