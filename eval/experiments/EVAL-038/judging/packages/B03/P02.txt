## PRODUCTION_RECIPE

1. **Product Procurement:** Source a bottle (or modern can) representative of final packaging. If the bottle is unlabelled/prototype, light it so the form alone reads premium—clarity, edge definition, surface finish matter more than a visible label.

2. **Ingredient Preparation:** Acquire one fresh mosambi. Cut one segment (thin, showing interior juice and seeds) or use one whole small fruit. Mosambi must be visibly recognisable—its colour, texture, and form are the entire ingredient proof.

3. **Ground and Set:** Use a neutral, matte surface (pale grey, cool white, or very pale blue-grey). Ensure the surface is non-reflective and extends beyond the frame in all directions so no unplanned reflections or edge rolls appear. Avoid glossy or textured grounds—they compete.

4. **Lighting Setup:**  
   - **Key:** One large soft source (softbox, 4ft × 6ft or equivalent) positioned above-left, ~45° horizontal angle, ~30° above the camera axis. Power it sufficiently to produce a soft-edged shadow beneath the bottle and mosambi, consistent in direction.
   - **Fill:** Very gentle ambient or a large white bounce card on the right side of the bottle. Aim for a 3:1 to 4:1 key-to-fill ratio—not flat, but not theatrical shadow. The mood is professional and controlled, not dramatic.
   - **Background light:** Optional: a low-level cool rim or separation light behind the set (out of frame) if the ground reads too similar in tone to the bottle's base. Keep this subtle; the goal is tonal separation by contrast, not by a visible light.
   - **Camera:** Position the camera so the bottle sits in the left-centre of the 4:5 frame and the mosambi is in the lower-right. Shoot three-quarter bottle view (front face + side visible), showing maximum surface count without the back disappearing .

5. **Exposure & Rendering:** Expose for the highlight on the bottle (no blown speculars). The mosambi segment should read warm and well-saturated; the ground should be light but hold tone (not paper-white). Colour temperature: Cool overall (hint of blue in the ground), warm fruit (natural mosambi hue, no colour cast correction that flattens it).

6. **Focus & Depth:** Critical focus on the bottle's highlight and front label area. Allow the mosambi to sit slightly softer (minor focus falloff is naturalistic for product). Never use extreme shallow depth of field; maintain enough sharpness to show bottle form and fruit texture cleanly.

7. **Post-Processing:** 
   - Minimal retouching (remove dust, correct obvious flaws only).
   - Enhance the highlight consistency (one clean, not diffuse, catch on the bottle).
   - Boost mosambi colour warmth very slightly if it reads dull beside the cool bottle.
   - Ensure tonal separation: bottle and ground do not merge in grayscale.
   - No heavy filters, no vignetting, no obvious digital manipulation—premium aesthetic is craft-like, not designed.
