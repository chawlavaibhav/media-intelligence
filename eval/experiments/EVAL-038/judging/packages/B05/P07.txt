FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
One continuous narrative video sequence, exactly 6 shots, hard cuts only, target runtime ≈ 40 seconds, 16:9, dialogue-driven two-hander café confrontation scene with synced ambient sound and restrained line delivery.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
The customer wants a small, controlled dramatic scene — not a montage or a demo of camera technique. Success = the viewer instantly reads (a) who discovered what, (b) the emotional temperature (tense but contained, never shouted), and (c) the physical relationship between the two people at every moment, achieved through disciplined shot economy rather than coverage or movement.

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
Tension is carried by stillness, not motion. The camera never moves; the actors barely do. A fixed 180° axis and a single motivated light source turn a plain café table into a pressure chamber — the calm public setting amplifies the private rupture. One physical object (a folded letter, half-hidden under a hand) becomes the silent proof of concealment; the edit holds on it just long enough to register without over-explaining.

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Primary: Trust has just been broken — one person now knows the other hid something.
2. Secondary: Both are exercising visible self-control; the restraint itself is the drama.
3. Tertiary: No resolution is given — the scene ends on unresolved silence, leaving weight with the viewer.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Fixed 180° axis: Person A always screen-left, Person B always screen-right, established in Shot 1 and never crossed.
- Lens language: 35–50mm equivalent, shallow depth of field (~f/2–2.8), slight compression, no wide-angle distortion.
- Lighting: single soft key from a café window screen-left (motivated), warm-neutral color temperature, gentle falloff into shadow on the far side of each face — mirrors the emotional "one side lit, one side withheld" idea without being overt.
- Grade: warm neutral midtones, slightly desaturated shadows, low contrast crush — restrained, not stylized.
- Camera support: locked-off tripod throughout. No pans, zooms, dollies, or handheld movement in any shot.
- Cutting rhythm: slower-than-usual cuts (5–9s holds) — control, not coverage.
- Props (fixed, recurring): two ceramic coffee cups, one folded letter/paper partially tucked under Person B's hand, plain wood café table, muted background bokeh (blurred café patrons/window light).
- Wardrobe/appearance fixed for continuity (see PRODUCTION_RECIPE character sheet).

PRODUCTION_RECIPE

Character sheet (reused verbatim in every prompt for continuity):
- PERSON A: woman, early 30s, dark hair tied back loosely, wearing a plain charcoal sweater, seated screen-left, hands mostly still, minimal jewelry.
- PERSON B: man, late 30s, short brown hair, wearing a rumpled pale-blue button shirt, seated screen-right, one hand resting near a folded letter on the table.
- LOCATION: small independent café, wooden two-top table by a window, soft daylight, blurred background patrons, warm ambient tone.

Shot list (axis fixed: A left / B right in every frame):

SHOT 1 — Wide establishing two-shot (7s)
Purpose: Establish geography and mood before dialogue.
Static locked wide shot across the table, both characters fully visible, coffee cups between them, letter partially visible under B's hand. No dialogue — 2s of stillness, ambient café sound only, then A's eyes lift toward B.

SHOT 2 — Medium two-shot, slight side angle (6s)
Purpose: Reinforce spatial relationship, open dialogue.
Same axis, tighter medium framing showing both from a slight 3/4 side angle (perpendicular-ish to table). A speaks first, quiet and flat: "You knew."
Held beat of silence after the line.

SHOT 3 — Over-the-shoulder on B, foreground A's shoulder/hair out of focus (6s)
Purpose: Isolate B's response while keeping A physically present in frame (maintains axis).
B, controlled, not defensive: "I was going to tell you." Minimal gesture — fingers shift slightly closer to the letter.

SHOT 4 — Reverse over-the-shoulder on A, foreground B's shoulder out of focus (6s)
Purpose: Mirror composition of Shot 3, same axis, shows A's reaction and next line.
A, quieter, colder: "When?" Then silence — B does not answer on camera.

SHOT 5 — Static close insert on hands/letter on table (5s)
Purpose: Deterministic visual proof of the concealment; a breathing-room cutaway that still respects axis (letter positioned consistent with B's side of table, screen-right).
No dialogue. B's fingers rest near the folded letter; a single almost-motion (thumb pressing the edge of the paper) reads as guilt/restraint.

SHOT 6 — Return to wide two-shot, same framing as Shot 1 (9–10s)
Purpose: Close the scene on unresolved stillness, spatial clarity restored.
A, flat and final: "That's what I thought." Both remain seated, no shouting, no exit. Hold 3–4s of silence after the line before cut to black.

Approx. total: 7+6+6+6+5+9 = 39s (within ~40s target).

- Dialogue: four short lines only, delivered flat and quiet, no vocal escalation, generous silence between lines (per shot list above).
- Score: subtle low minor-key tone enters only at Shot 5 (the letter insert) and carries through to the end, reinforcing quiet dread without melodrama.
- Edit: hard cuts only between all six shots (no dissolves, no whip pans) — reinforces the "controlled/clinical" restraint rather than fluid emotional coverage.
- Final beat: 3–4 seconds of held silence (ambient only, score fading) before cut to black.
