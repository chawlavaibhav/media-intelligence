FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

---

**DELIVERABLE**

A 25-second vertical (9:16) UGC-style performance video featuring one Indian woman speaking directly to camera in a naturalistic bathroom or bedroom setting. The video demonstrates a fragrance-free 2% salicylic acid facial serum through authentic product handling and a clear benefit statement, styled as credible creator content with commercial visual control and usability.

---

**OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION**

The brief requires two opposing forces held in balance: documentary authenticity (natural light, conversational tone, minimal distance from audience) and commercial control (product legibility, skin clarity, one motivated reason to buy). The solution is a single-source soft window light, centered-then-slightly-offset framing, and a four-shot narrative arc that builds from problem to outcome without medical language. The phrase "one clear purchase reason" translates to a single benefit statement — oil control — stated as observed personal result, not clinical claim. Talent selection must read as a believable peer, not a model. Timing is set by the content's silent description: each shot holds only as long as its action requires.

---

**CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA**

A real-feeling Indian woman addresses the camera as though answering a friend's question: "Does this actually work?" She states her oil-and-blemish frustration, shows the serum's texture and how quickly it absorbs, and closes with a single anchor: "It actually controls oil." The product is introduced by her, not by a voiceover. No music bed intrudes; her voice and the space's ambient sound carry the narrative. The final frame holds her direct gaze and a calm expression — credibility through stillness, not performance.

---

**MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY**

**1st Read (Problem):** Talent's eyes and expression, direct address ("My skin gets so oily… wouldn't go away"). Carries tone and relatability.

**2nd Read (Solution):** Product enters hand, is briefly described, then applied. Texture visibility and quick absorption are demonstrated. Candidate angle shows bottle front (label) and her fingertip action simultaneously.

**3rd Read (Outcome):** Skin absorption and matte finish. Talent's closing statement: "It actually controls oil." The reason is benefit-focused (what it does for her) and belief-driven (not a clinical statement).

---

**VISUAL_SYSTEM**

**attention_order:** 
1st = Speaker's face, eyes fixed on camera, mouth delivering opening problem statement (fre_0020: eyes attract more strongly than any other subject).
2nd = Product held in her hand entering frame, bottle front label briefly legible, application motion on skin (action draws eye after statement establishes trust).
3rd = Skin texture post-application, matte finish visible, final direct gaze (outcome completes narrative arc).

**surface_finish_per_key_object:**
Serum bottle = glossy/direct (product hero, one highlight consistent with window light source).
Skin (post-application) = matte/diffuse (demonstrates product efficacy, non-reflective surface reads honest).
Applicator (fingertip) = matte/diffuse (natural skin, no distraction).

**implied_light_source:**
One soft window light source positioned camera-left or front-left (mimics natural morning light, typical to UGC bathroom/bedroom settings). Softness is large relative to talent's face, producing a soft shadow edge on the far side of her face and even skin tone without loss of detail. Highlight on glossy bottle reflects this single direction and large apparent source size. Direction remains constant across all four shots; no shadow contradicts it.

**placement_zone:**
Talent is centered in opening (Shot 1) because the scene points inward to her subjective problem statement (CA-D8 per fre_0019: centre when the scene points inward and the subject is the sole statement). During product demonstration (Shots 2–3), framing may hold center or allow slight offset to the right to show setting context (bathroom mirror, window) without breaking the 9:16 boundary. Reason: Creator content convention centers the speaker; brief's "directly to camera" reinforces center as the primary zone. Offset in demo is permissible to show setting without extreme placement violation.

**PRODUCTION_RECIPE**

**Shot 1: Problem & Direct Address | 8 seconds**
- **Framing:** Medium close-up (shoulders to top of head), centered in frame, slight deliberate gap between head and top frame edge (CA-D3: avoid magnetic edge effect).
- **Setting:** Bathroom or bright bedroom, neutral wall background (no busy pattern), natural window light from camera-left or front-left. Light is diffused (sheer curtain or overcast quality).
- **Action:** Talent speaks directly to camera in conversational tone, no product visible. She addresses her frustration with oily skin and blemishes matter-of-factly. Expression is relatable, slightly weary but not dramatic. Mouth and eyes carry the emotional content.
- **Sound:** Natural room ambience, talent's voice natural (not announcery), no music bed.
- **Purpose:** Establish peer credibility and the problem state. Anchor viewer's attention to her face and voice.

---

**Shot 2: Product Introduction & Initial Demo | 10 seconds**
- **Framing:** Hold medium CU or slight pullback to show product in hand. Bottle is legible (label readable) but not the sole visual focus; talent's face remains in frame, expression visible.
- **Setting:** Same light, same background. Product (serum bottle, clear glass or plastic) enters frame from lower frame edge or from camera-right side.
- **Action:** Talent describes the serum simply and factually ("This is a 2% salicylic acid serum, fragrance-free"). She holds the bottle, showing the front label without turning it repeatedly. She dispenses a small amount (visible drop) onto her fingertip, tilts her finger slightly to show the clear, thin consistency (no color, no viscosity). She then applies the serum to her chin or cheek area, rubbing it in with a gentle motion. Skin remains visible; her face does not leave frame.
- **Sound:** Talent's voice carries the description; natural sound of serum dispensing (soft, not exaggerated) is acceptable. No music bed or SFX.
- **Purpose:** Introduce the product factually and show texture/appearance (clear, lightweight). Demonstrate application as something she does naturally, not a posed beauty tutorial.

---

**Shot 3: Absorption & Texture Feel | 5 seconds**
- **Framing:** Tighter on her face and the application area (T-zone, chin, or cheek where serum is being worked in). Medium or medium-close.
- **Setting:** Same light and background. Skin detail is now more prominent; light remains soft enough to show skin texture without harsh shadows that obscure expression or application action.
- **Action:** Talent continues massaging the serum into her skin gently. She comments on the feel and absorption as she applies: "Sinks in immediately. Doesn't feel heavy or sticky like other serums I've used." Her tone remains conversational, low-key. She touches her skin to show the absorption; fingertip moves across the area. Expression shows a subtle shift toward satisfaction (not exaggerated).
- **Sound:** Natural ambience, voice, and the soft tactile sounds of application. No music bed.
- **Purpose:** Demonstrate the actual product experience (rapid absorption, light feel) through action and direct observation. Anchor the benefit claim ("doesn't feel heavy") to a sensory moment the viewer can relate to.

---

**Shot 4: Result & Purchase Reason | 2 seconds**
- **Framing:** Return to medium CU (similar framing to Shot 1), talent's face centered or slightly off-center right, looking directly at camera.
- **Setting:** Same light and background. Skin now shows the applied serum (may have slight matte sheen or even finish, no shine).
- **Action:** Talent states her reason simply and directly: "It actually controls oil. That's what I was looking for." Expression is calm, assured, and direct. She may allow a small natural smile or remain neutral. Final frame holds for 1 second, then fade to black or end card.
- **Sound:** Talent's voice, natural tone. After her final statement, optional soft warm ambient tone or subtle lo-fi beat may begin under the end card (keep at -12 to -15 dB; do not compete with voice or visual).
- **Purpose:** Deliver the single, benefit-focused purchase reason. Close the narrative arc (problem → solution → result). State the reason as a personal observation, not a clinical claim.

**DETERMINISTIC_OR_NON_GENERATIVE_ELEMENTS**

- **Dialogue:** All four quoted statements are fixed and must be delivered conversationally, not as announcer copy. Talent may vary inflection naturally but not meaning.
- **Product Label:** Bottle must display text readable as "Salicylic Acid Serum" and "Fragrance-Free." Legibility is required; label is not a visual focus, but it must be present and clear enough for a glance.
- **End Card (optional):** 1–2 seconds, black background or soft color, brand logo + "Shop Now" or "Link in Bio" + URL or app link. Typography simple, sans-serif, white or soft color. Fade in and fade out. Music bed (if used) begins under end card, not before.
- **Color Grading:** Warm, natural daylight, slight lift in shadows (typical UGC smartphone or mirrorless aesthetic). No LUT presets, no heavy filters, no desaturation. Skin tones should read natural and warm.
- **Sound Design:** 
  - No voiceover post-production; all dialogue is talent's live voice.
  - Natural room ambience (soft background, no A/C hum or street noise).
  - Soft tactile sounds of serum application are acceptable; do not augment or layer SFX.
  - Optional music bed (lo-fi, ambient, or warm instrumental) under Shot 4 and end card only, at -12 to -15 dB.
  - No music bed during Shots 1–3; voice and ambience carry the narrative.
- **Talent Direction:** "Speak as if you're texting a friend who asked about your skincare—honest, not selling, but genuine. You're not a model; you're someone who found something that works and you're sharing it. Look at the camera, not at the product."
