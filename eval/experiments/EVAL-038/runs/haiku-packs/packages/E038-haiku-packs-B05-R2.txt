# FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

## DELIVERABLE
Six-shot cinematic video scene, approximately 40 seconds, 16:9 aspect ratio. Setting: café interior. Two seated figures opposite each other at a table. Tense dialogue scene: one figure has discovered a concealment by the other. Performance is restrained, no raised voices. Spatial continuity maintained throughout. Static cinematography with minimal motivated adjustment. Target emotional register: controlled tension, underlying betrayal.

---

## OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
The customer requests a structured dialogue scene built to exactly six shots, with a hard ceiling on duration (40 sec) and a specific aspect ratio (16:9). The core narrative constraint is discovery and confrontation; the core *technique* constraint is that the viewer must never lose spatial orientation between the two figures. This rules out crossing the 180-degree line casually and demands spatial consistency across cuts. The instruction "cinematic rather than mechanically covered" signals a request for intentional compositional control, not rote coverage. The ban on "elaborate camera movement" restricts technique to stillness and motivated adjustment only.

---

## CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
A moment of crisis compressed into six static shots: two people across a table, geography locked, one holding information, one defending against it. The scene's cinematic quality derives not from camera virtuosity but from precise frame composition, attention control at each cut, and restraint in both performance and technique. Each shot serves a distinct narrative and compositional function; no shot is redundant. The table becomes the psychological barrier, rendered visible by the fixed 180-degree framing.

---

## MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
**Narrative layer:** A trust rupture. Person A (the discoverer) moves from accusation to demand; Person B moves from evasion to vulnerability. The arc is A pressing, B defending, B breaking.

**Spatial layer:** Two people opposite across a table, always located consistently. The 180-degree rule is the grammar that makes this spatial clarity possible.

**Emotional layer:** Tension without volume. Restraint as the register. What is *not* said matters as much as what is.

**Per-shot information hierarchy (example):**
- Shot 1: "Here are two people at a table; watch them meet." (establishing fact)
- Shot 2: "Person A is accusing." (action, tense)
- Shot 3: "Person B is cornered." (reaction, discomfort)
- Shot 4: "Person A escalates." (pressure)
- Shot 5: "Person B cracks." (vulnerability)
- Shot 6: "The silence between them." (aftermath)

## VISUAL_SYSTEM

**attention_order:**
- Shot 1 (wide): 1st = Person A's face (left-frame, the active agent); 2nd = Person B's face (right-frame, the reactive agent); 3rd = table and café depth cue.
- Shot 2 (A close): 1st = Person A's eyes; 2nd = mouth (speech); 3rd = soft café background.
- Shot 3 (B close): 1st = Person B's eyes; 2nd = mouth; 3rd = background.
- Shot 4 (A close, tighter): 1st = eyes; 2nd = jaw/expression; 3rd = background.
- Shot 5 (B close, more vulnerable): 1st = eyes; 2nd = barely-moving mouth; 3rd = background.
- Shot 6 (wide or close): 1st = both figures or final close subject; 2nd & 3rd follow from framing.

**surface_finish_per_key_object:**
- Person A: matte/diffuse (skin lit with soft café ambience, no extreme specularity; clothing neutral).
- Person B: matte/diffuse (same).
- Table surface: diffuse or glare-free (polished wood or matte; no bright gleam that would compete with faces).
- Café background (out of focus): diffuse bokeh (warm, neutral, supporting, not distracting).

**implied_light_source:**
Single nameable source: café ambient + one window or overhead fixture (warm, diffuse). Direction: slightly from the side or overhead, even across both faces. No harsh shadows; no single dramatic key light. The light serves clarity of expression, not drama. Consistency across all six shots maintains spatial coherence.

**placement_zone:**
- Shot 1 (wide): Person A occupies left zone of frame (positioned across table, camera-left); Person B occupies right zone (camera-right). Reason: they sit opposite, and the wide frame must show both without one dominating the other spatially. Table between them, not centered but implied.
- Shot 2 (A close): Person A is slightly off-center, frame-left or center-left. Reason: off-center placement follows the doctrine default; left placement is consistent with Shot 1 geography and the 180-degree line (camera has moved closer to A but not crossed the table).
- Shot 3 (B close): Person B is slightly off-center, frame-right or center-right. Reason: reverse-angle from A; B is now the subject, placed on the opposite side of frame to show the look-across-the-table geometry is intact.
- Shot 4 (A close, escalation): Person A remains frame-left or center-left, possibly tighter (closer framing) to show pressure without moving the camera. Reason: consistency with Shot 2 and A's spatial identity.
- Shot 5 (B close, vulnerability): Person B frame-right or center-right, possibly softer focus or closer framing to show emotion. Reason: consistency and emotional escalation via framing, not camera movement.
- Shot 6 (resolution): Placement depends on the shot choice—if wide again, A and B return to their zones; if final close, the subject is placed per the escalation pattern.

## PRODUCTION_RECIPE

**Setup 1: Wide two-shot at café table**
- Camera position: Stationary, positioned at a slight angle to the table (not head-on, not extreme—approximately 15–30 degrees off-axis). Height approximately even with seated eye level or slightly above.
- Framing: Both figures visible, table visible, depth of café (out of focus) visible behind. A on camera-left, B on camera-right.
- Focus: Primary on faces; table soft; background soft. Depth of field sufficient to keep both faces in acceptable focus.
- Duration: ~6 seconds. Content: A and B settle, A begins "I found out."

**Setup 2: Close-up of Person A, slightly off-center frame-left**
- Camera position: Stationary, close to A's face, on the same side of the table as Setup 1 (no crossing the line). Camera at eye level or slightly above.
- Framing: A's face dominant, slight headroom, tight fit with deliberate gap (sparse frame, just one person). Table out of frame. B implied out-of-frame to the right (off-camera).
- Focus: A's face sharp; background soft (café bokeh).
- Duration: ~7 seconds. Content: A states the discovery and demand ("I found out. / The bank statements. The transfers. / Tell me what it is.").

**Setup 3: Close-up of Person B, slightly off-center frame-right (reverse angle)**
- Camera position: Stationary, close to B's face, on the same side of the table (180-degree line respected; this is a reverse shot, not a cross). Camera at eye level or slightly above.
- Framing: B's face dominant, tight fit with gap. A implied out-of-frame to the left. Background soft café bokeh.
- Focus: B's face sharp; background soft.
- Duration: ~6 seconds. Content: A finishes; B reacts, deflects, then begins response ("I... it's not what you think.").

**Setup 4: Close-up of Person A, frame-left (same angle as Setup 2, tighter or identical framing)**
- Camera position: Stationary, identical to Setup 2 (consistency).
- Framing: A closer or same as Shot 2; tight fit with gap. Same focus and background.
- Duration: ~6 seconds. Content: A presses ("Then tell me what it is."). Escalation via dialogue and performance, not camera movement.

**Setup 5: Close-up of Person B, frame-right (same angle as Setup 3)**
- Camera position: Stationary, identical to Setup 3.
- Framing: B in tight close-up; tight fit with gap. Same focus and background.
- Duration: ~6 seconds. Content: B breaks slightly, speaks quietly ("I can't. Not here."), or long pause before speech. Vulnerability via performance and perhaps a slight softening of framing (depth of field very shallow to isolate B).

**Setup 6: Resolution shot (choice: return to wide two-shot, or final close-up of one figure)**
- Option A (wide return): Camera position identical to Setup 1. Both A and B visible, the tension sits between them. Duration: ~9 seconds. Content: Silence. A and B are frozen, or B reaches out slightly, or A looks away. No dialogue, or very sparse. The scene holds and settles.
- Option B (final A close): Camera position and framing as Setup 4. A alone, looking down or away, silence. Duration: ~9 seconds. Content: A's realization that B will not yield. Pain or resignation.
- Option C (final B close): Camera position and framing as Setup 5. B alone, eyes wet or closed, or looking directly at camera (at A). Duration: ~9 seconds. Content: B's acceptance of the breach.
- **Recommendation: Option A (return to wide two-shot).** It closes the spatial frame that the scene opened, and it respects the brief's demand that "the viewer must always understand where each person is in relation to the other." The wide shows both at the end, the silence between them visible.

## DETERMINISTIC_OR_NON_GENERATIVE_ELEMENTS

**Dialogue (deterministic, required verbatim or with minimal paraphrase):**

Person A:
- "I found out."
- "The bank statements. The transfers."
- "Don't. / Don't do that."
- "I trusted you."
- "Then tell me what it is."

Person B:
- "Found out what?" [optional, can be silent]
- "It's not what you think."
- "I can't. Not here."

The dialogue must convey:
- A is in control initially, accusing.
- B is evasive, then defensive, then broken.
- Neither raises their voice.
- The subject is concealed finances (transfers, bank statements).

**Spatial geography (deterministic):**
- Person A sits on one side of the table; Person B sits on the opposite side.
- They face each other across the table.
- The camera never crosses the line: A is always on the same side of screen in A's close-ups; B is always on the opposite side in B's close-ups.
- The wide shot (Shot 1 and Shot 6) shows both at the table, making their opposition clear.

**Aspect ratio (deterministic, fixed):**
- 16:9 landscape (not 1:1, not 9:16).

**Duration (deterministic, ~40 seconds):**
- Total target: 40 seconds. Shot breakdown: 6 + 7 + 6 + 6 + 6 + 9 = 40 seconds.

**Lighting (deterministic, café ambience):**
- Warm colour temperature (3200–4000 K equivalent).
- Soft, even illumination (no harsh key light or dramatic shadows).
- Source: café overhead and/or window, implied but not shown.
- Consistency across all six shots (same time of day, same location, same light quality).

**Setting (deterministic):**
- Interior café, daytime or early evening (warm natural or café interior light).
- Small table seating two.
- Café background (patrons, counter, furniture) visible but out of focus.
- No elaborate décor; a realistic small café.

**Number of shots (deterministic):**
- Exactly six shots.

**Camera movement (deterministic):**
- None. Or: minimal, motivation-driven only (not used in this scene, given the brief's emphasis on avoiding elaborate movement).

## AUDIO_AND_EDIT

**Dialogue recording:**
- Record Person A's lines and Person B's lines separately in a quiet environment, then place in sync with the video during edit.
- Or: record on-set during performance, capture ambient café noise simultaneously.
- Dialogue is the primary audio layer: clear, intelligible, restrained in delivery.
- Tone: quiet, controlled, no rising volume.

**Ambient sound:**
- Café background ambience (low, continuous): gentle chatter (very low level, unintelligible), occasional cup clink, subtle music if the café has it.
- Level: -15 to -20 dB relative to dialogue, so as not to distract.
- Consistency: same ambience across all six shots.

**Editing (timeline structure):**
- Shot 1 (6 sec): fade in from black, café ambience begins; A's line "I found out" at ~0:05 mark (late in the shot, after settling).
- Shot 2 (7 sec): Cut (no transition). A continues dialogue. Ambience continues.
- Shot 3 (6 sec): Cut. B responds. Ambience continues.
- Shot 4 (6 sec): Cut. A presses. Ambience continues.
- Shot 5 (6 sec): Cut. B's line, then silence. Ambience continues at same level or fades slightly.
- Shot 6 (9 sec): Cut. Wide shot, silence or minimal dialogue. Ambience continues. Fade to black at end (1–2 sec).
- Total: 40 seconds, no music, no dramatic sound design beyond natural café ambience.

**Colour grading (not a deterministic element but a production note):**
- Warm, desaturated, café-like. Shadows slightly lifted (no crushed blacks). Slight warm cast (orange/amber undertone). No blue, no cool tones. Consistency across all six shots ensures spatial continuity and emotional cohesion.

## FAILURE_PREVENTION

**Injected Canon doctrine pack: composition_and_attention v0**

`CA-D1: pass` — Each shot has one dominant cue for attention (Shot 1: A's face; Shot 2: A's eyes/mouth; Shot 3: B's eyes/mouth; Shots 4–5: respective faces; Shot 6: both figures or final dominant). No competing cues within a beat.

`CA-D2: pass` — Subject placement is stated as a zone: Shot 1, A in left zone, B in right zone, reason is opposite-table geometry. Shots 2/4, A off-center frame-left, reason is 180-degree line and spatial consistency. Shots 3/5, B off-center frame-right, reason is reverse angle within 180-degree line. No placement justified by ratio or grid.

`CA-D3: pass` — One edge treatment per shot: Shots 1, 6 (wide), busy scene, edges less critical, slight gap around table. Shots 2–5 (close-ups), sparse frame (one face), tight fit with deliberate gap. All edges named; no accidental near-tangency.

`CA-D4: not_governed_by_injected_doctrine` — No framing element (no physical frame-within-frame) is used in this scene. Close-ups are of faces, not subjects passing behind openings. CA-D4 does not apply.

`CA-D5: pass` — Classical balance in all shots. Shot 1: A's weight (frame-left) balanced by B's weight (frame-right) across the table. Shots 2–5: each close-up is balanced by negative space (background bokeh, headroom). No deliberate restlessness declared; balance is the default and serves the restraint of the scene.

`CA-D6: pass` — Aspect ratio 16:9 is justified by the named shape: two figures sitting opposite, extending horizontally across the table. The wide frame is called for by the geometry of the scene. 16:9 is not justified by platform (no platform cited); it is justified by the scene's shapes (horizontal extension).

`CA-D7: pass` — Every new shot carries new information and motivation. Shot 1: both figures established, A begins accusation. Shot 2: A's intensity, A's specific claim (bank statements). Shot 3: B's reaction, B's deflection. Shot 4: A's pressure escalates. Shot 5: B breaks. Shot 6: silence and the weight of the aftermath. Each cut is motivated by narrative progression. Composition differs at each cut (close-up A, close-up B, back to A closer, back to B more vulnerable, back to wide). No two cuts repeat the same compositional statement.

`CA-D8: pass` — No conflicts between cuts. Murch's rule is satisfied in order: emotion (tension, betrayal, vulnerability) > story (the discovery, the confrontation, the silence) > rhythm (six evenly-spaced cuts, moderate pace). Planarity and 3D space are not sacrificed; the 180-degree line preserves 3D geography. No conflict arises.

`CA-D9: pass` — Screen direction and spatial continuity maintained. All six shots are within a single 180-degree arc. Camera never crosses the line separating A and B. A remains on the same side of frame in Shots 1, 2, 4, 6 (frame-left or left-biased). B remains on the same side of frame in Shots 1, 3, 5, 6 (frame-right or right-biased). Reciprocal coverage (Shots 2 and 3 as a matched pair; Shots 4 and 5 as a matched pair) obeys the line. The viewer always understands A is on one side of the table, B on the other, across from each other. No reversal without a visible reason (none occurs; there is no reversal).

`CA-D10: pass` — Shot length is set by describable content. Shot 1: settling, opening line = 6 sec. Shot 2: A's accusation and demand = 7 sec. Shot 3: B's reaction and response = 6 sec. Shot 4: A's pressure = 6 sec. Shot 5: B's break = 6 sec. Shot 6: silence and stillness = 9 sec. No shot outlasts its content. The prevailing pace is moderate (not fast-cutting; each shot has time to breathe).

`CA-D11: pass` — Each camera is static (no movement). Stillness is chosen deliberately. There is no camera move in this scene; no move needs motivation because there is no move. Unmotivated movement is absent by design.
