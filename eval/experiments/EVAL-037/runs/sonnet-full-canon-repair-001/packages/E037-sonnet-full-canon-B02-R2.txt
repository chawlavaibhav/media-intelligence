FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
One (1) static promotional poster, 4:5 aspect ratio (e.g., 1080×1350 px, scalable vector-safe), for paid social/web placement and possibly print. Single final image. No variants.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
The customer needs one poster that does two things simultaneously without contradiction: (1) make ₹9 (image) and ₹99 (video) instantly legible and unambiguous — this is the commercial payload and the reason the poster exists; (2) make the brand read as serious AI infrastructure for businesses, not a discount retail flyer, during a festive season context. Per getaight.ai, Aight's actual brand voice is plain, declarative, numeric, and merchant/ledger-coded ("the counter is open," "prepaid rupees," "one domestic GST invoice," "hard caps") — it already talks about itself as a counter/ledger, not a lifestyle brand. That gives a legitimate, source-grounded route to festive without kitsch: treat the poster as a rate card / ledger entry, not a sale banner. Festiveness is expressed through a single restrained warm-gold accent and one small graphic mark, not through discount-flyer devices (bursts, slashes, confetti, "SALE," multiple colors). The brief explicitly asks that typography and hierarchy — not illustration — carry the work, which aligns with treating this as a precision rate-card object.

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
"The Festive Rate Card." The poster is designed as a premium, single-color-accented ledger/invoice line-item card: two price rows (IMAGE / VIDEO) rendered as oversized tabular numerals against labelled line items, framed by the language Aight already uses about itself (prepaid rupees, one GST invoice, no surprise bills). A festive season is acknowledged through one small line-drawn diya mark and a warm gold accent — nothing more. This makes the ₹9/₹99 proposition read like a fact on a merchant's counter (credible, infrastructural, exact) rather than a promotional discount (temporary, negotiable, loud).

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
Priority order (also the reading path, top to bottom, left-aligned ledger grid):
1. Occasion eyebrow (smallest, quietest, sets timing only): "FESTIVE SEASON RATE CARD"
2. Hero tier — the two prices, largest and heaviest elements on the page, each paired with an unambiguous plain-English label:
   a. IMAGE GENERATION — ₹9
   b. VIDEO GENERATION — ₹99
3. Trust/credibility line (mid-size, quieter than prices, louder than footer): "Prepaid rupees. One domestic GST invoice. No surprise bills."
4. Brand identity block (footer, smallest of the named elements but always present): "Aight ." wordmark + "Where Indian businesses buy AI." + getaight.ai
No other copy competes for attention. Nothing is ranked above the two prices. Hierarchy is built entirely from scale and weight contrast within one typeface family and a fixed left-alignment column grid — the two controls (size contrast, spacing/density contrast) that most reliably establish hierarchy in a layout — plus a strict column-width discipline that differentiates the "label" text from the "price" text so the eye never confuses the two roles.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Format: 4:5, portrait, generous fixed margins (~7% of width each side), everything left-aligned to one vertical grid; prices right-aligned to a second column so the eye reads label→price on a straight baseline, invoice-style.
- Palette: near-black charcoal ground (#121212–#161616), warm paper-white text (#F2EEE4 at full and ~70% opacity for secondary lines), one festive accent — muted antique/marigold gold (#C9A24A) used only for: the ₹ glyph in both prices, the thin rule under the eyebrow, the thin rules between the two price rows, and one small line-drawn diya mark. No other colors. No gradients, no photographic imagery, no confetti/burst/ribbon devices, no red "sale" treatment.
- Typography: a single grotesque/geometric sans family in three weights only (Light, Regular, Black/Bold with true tabular lining numerals). Labels and body set in Regular/Light with wide, even letterspacing on the eyebrow and footer for a "printed ledger" feel. The two prices set in the Black/Bold weight at dramatically larger size than everything else on the page (the single largest size jump on the poster, deliberately extreme, since the price is the reason the asset exists). Numerals must use tabular/lining figures so "₹9" and "₹99" align cleanly on their price column despite different digit counts.
- One small non-typographic mark: a minimal single-stroke gold line-drawing of a diya (a shallow open arc + a single flame teardrop, no shading, no photorealism) placed as a divider between the eyebrow and the first price row, or as a small glyph beside the section rule — the only "festive" iconography permitted, kept graphic and abstract, never cute or illustrative.
- Overall mood reference: a merchant's rate card, an invoice line item, a stock-ticker board — precise, tabular, unornamented, confident. Not: a retail sale poster, not a lifestyle/consumer app ad, not a rangoli/collage festive graphic.

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
1. Lock copy exactly as specified below (no paraphrase, no additional claims — see DETERMINISTIC section).
2. Build the layout in a vector/design tool (or via the generative background prompt below plus deterministic text) on a 1080×1350 canvas.
3. Establish the grid: left content column starting at 7% margin; label text and price text share one baseline per row; a second right-aligned column anchors the price numerals so ₹9 and ₹99 both terminate flush right.
4. Set the eyebrow line, thin gold rule, diya mark, two price rows with dividing gold rule between them, trust line, and footer wordmark block in that vertical order with generously increasing space "weight" between tiers (tightest spacing within a row, most open spacing between the price block and the footer) so that spatial rhythm itself reinforces which lines belong together and which are separate ideas.
5. Render at full resolution; verify numeral accuracy against source copy before export (see FAILURE_PREVENTION).

GENERATION_PROMPTS
Single executable prompt (background/texture + composition layer; exact copy to be set deterministically as described in the next section — see note below on why):

"A premium minimalist poster background, 4:5 portrait aspect ratio. Deep charcoal-black flat backdrop (#121212) with an extremely subtle fine paper/ledger-page grain texture, almost imperceptible, no visible photograph or scene. A thin horizontal warm antique-gold rule (#C9A24A) sits near the top third of the frame, with a small minimal single-line gold drawing of a diya (oil lamp) beside it — rendered as a simple open arc with one small teardrop flame, flat line art only, no shading, no gradient, no photorealism, no additional festive clutter. A second, matching thin gold horizontal rule sits roughly at the vertical center of the frame, dividing the composition into an upper and lower price zone. The overall composition is calm, exact, and spacious, with wide even margins on all four sides (roughly 7% of width), evoking a merchant's ledger or an invoice line-item card rather than a retail advertisement — no bursts, no confetti, no ribbons, no discount-sale graphics, no photographic products, no human figures, no additional colors beyond charcoal, warm paper-white, and antique gold. Leave large clear negative-space bands above the top rule, between the two rules, below the second rule, and at the bottom of the frame, sized to later receive large tabular numeral typography and short lines of text — do not render any text, numerals, or letterforms in the image itself. Mood: serious financial/infrastructure precision with one restrained festive accent, premium and quiet, not consumer or playful."

Deterministic text overlay (set in post-production typesetting, exact strings, one grotesque sans family, tabular figures):

DETERMINISTIC_OR_NON_GENERATIVE_ELEMENTS
All copy — the two prices (₹9, ₹99), both labels, the trust line, and the entire footer wordmark/tagline/URL block — must be set as deterministic, precisely controlled typesetting (vector text layer), not left to a generative model's text-rendering. Reasons: (a) price accuracy is a hard commercial constraint and a single misrendered digit is unacceptable; (b) tabular numeral alignment and exact letterspacing are required for the "ledger" effect and are unreliable in current text-in-image generation; (c) the brand wordmark "Aight ." and tagline are verbatim quotes from the only permitted source and must not be altered or reinterpreted. Only the background texture, the two gold rules, and the single diya line-mark may be produced generatively (or drawn directly in a vector tool, which is the lower-risk option and is recommended).

AUDIO_AND_EDIT
Not applicable — static single image deliverable, no motion or audio component.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- Digit/price error: the single highest-severity risk. Mitigation: set price text deterministically, proofread against the brief twice, and never allow a generative model to render the numerals inside the image itself.
- Drift toward "discount flyer" look: mitigation — no burst/starburst shapes, no strikethrough "was/now" pricing, no red, no multiple accent colors, no exclamation marks, no "SALE"/"OFFER" language anywhere in copy.
- Over-decoration of the diya mark: keep it to one flat gold line-stroke; if it starts to look illustrative/cute in any draft, remove it — the poster must still work with zero iconography, since typography is the mandated carrier of hierarchy.
- Copy invention: do not add claims not present on getaight.ai (no fabricated stats, no invented model names, no claims about speed/quality not stated on the site). The trust line used is a close paraphrase of actual site language ("prepaid rupees," "one domestic GST invoice," "no surprise bills" — all present verbatim or near-verbatim on the site) and should not be embellished further.
- Legibility at small size (feed thumbnail): verify the two price numerals remain the single largest, most dominant elements even when the poster is scaled down to thumbnail size; if hierarchy collapses at small size, increase the size gap between price numerals and everything else rather than adding color or ornament.
