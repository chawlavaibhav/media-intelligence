## OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
Produce an authentic creator-style testimonial video that positions the salicylic acid serum as a non-irritating, fragrance-free solution for oily, blemish-prone skin. Establish believable credibility through direct product interaction and genuine communication, avoiding clinical hyperbole while emphasizing the practical benefit of fragrance-free formulation. Drive consideration among adults who are cautious about irritation from skincare actives.

---

## CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
A single Indian woman delivers an honest, conversational recommendation by demonstrating how the serum applies without the typical irritation and heaviness of fragranced products. The narrative is: "I use this because it actually works on my oily skin without the extra irritant." No before/after transformation promised—only the product experience.

---

## MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. **Primary hook**: "I needed something that wouldn't make my skin angrier" (oily, blemish-prone skin insight)  
2. **Product truth**: Fragrance-free, 2% salicylic acid—she shows the bottle and texture  
3. **Demonstration**: Application on face shows it's lightweight and non-drying  
4. **Closing reason**: "No fragrance means no extra irritation for my skin" (honest single benefit)  
5. **CTA**: Soft—"If your skin is like mine, you know why I use it"

## VISUAL_SYSTEM

**Aesthetic:**  
Minimal, natural, vertical-native. Natural window light or soft key light. Single-room setup (bathroom or bedroom). No props beyond the product.

**Color palette:**  
Warm, neutral tones. Skin tones read true. Serum appears clear/lightweight in bottle. Minimal color grading—authentic feel preserved.

**Framing:**  
- Medium close-up (shoulders up) for dialogue
- Extreme close-up (20–40cm from face) for product application  
- Quick cutaway to bottle showing shape and clarity  
- Return to face for final frame

**Talent direction:**  
Natural eye contact with camera. Conversational pace. Minimal makeup (audience expects creator authenticity). Hands relaxed but purposeful during demo.

---

## PRODUCTION_RECIPE

**Setup:**  
Single location. Natural or controlled soft lighting from one direction. Plain, neutral background (white wall, soft curtain, or minimal bathroom setting). Tripod-mounted phone or camera at eye level for 9:16 frame.

**Talent prep:**  
Woman in her 20s–30s. Indian, credibly representing the target demographic. Brief on the script structure but allow conversational phrasing. One take or two-take approach for authenticity.

**Product handling:**  
Real product bottle (labeled or unlabeled depending on brand stage). Actual serum application to face—not faked. Hands should appear natural, not overly staged.

**Camera technique:**  
Static wide shots for delivery. Slow zoom or reframe for product close-up. Handheld-style slight movement acceptable to reinforce UGC aesthetic, but not distracting.

**Duration target:**  
Aim for 24–26 seconds in-edit. Allows 1–2 second buffer for platform optimizations.

## AUDIO_AND_EDIT

**Dialogue:**  
Recorded or generated. Prioritize natural Indian English accent and authentic conversational pacing. No background music required (UGC aesthetic often works without music). If music is used: subtle, minimal, ambient—under 2kHz to not compete with dialogue.

**Sound:**  
Ambient room tone acceptable. Gentle sound effects acceptable (serum bottle cap opening, light pats of application) only if they feel natural and unobtrusive.

**Edit structure:**  
Cut on dialogue beats. No jump cuts or frenetic pacing—slow, deliberate transitions. Fade to black or white frame at close for brand reveal (2–3 seconds).

**Captions/Text:**  
Optional on-screen product name + CTA in final frame. Recommend white sans-serif text, center or bottom-third placement.

## FAILURE_PREVENTION

**Risk 1: Overly polished appearance (defeats UGC authenticity)**  
*Prevention:* Shoot in single natural location. Minimize styling. Allow conversational pauses and natural pacing. Avoid excessive color grading.

**Risk 2: Implied medical claims ("clears breakouts," "heals skin")**  
*Prevention:* Lock dialogue to product experience ("works without irritation") rather than outcomes. Phrase as "does the work" not "fixes it." No before/after promised.

**Risk 3: Serum appearance unclear or looks too thick (contradicts "lightweight" positioning)**  
*Prevention:* Source or simulate a thin, clear serum. Show it in bottle clearly. Ensure lighting highlights transparency and fluidity during application.

**Risk 4: Talent appears uncomfortable or reads as scripted**  
*Prevention:* Provide dialogue framework, not word-for-word script. Conduct 2–3 takes. Select most natural performance. Allow conversational fillers ("like," pauses).

**Risk 5: Audio quality poor or dialogue unintelligible**  
*Prevention:* Use lavalier mic or close-proximity recording. Re-record dialogue in clean booth if generated audio is unusable. Sync with video.

**Risk 6: 9:16 framing crops critical product or face details**  
*Prevention:* Frame all shots with 9:16 safe zone in mind. Avoid extreme head placement or product positioning at frame edges.
