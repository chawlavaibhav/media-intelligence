FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

---

**DELIVERABLE**
A 40-second cinematic video scene (16:9) showing a confrontation between two people at a café table, told through six precisely-composed shots that maintain spatial clarity while conveying emotional tension through restraint and subtext.

---

**OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION**
Produce a scene where spatial geography (two people opposite each other) is always legible to the viewer, tension emerges from what is *not* shouted, and six shots create a complete narrative arc without redundancy or mechanical shot-coverage. The emotion is in the silence and hesitation, not volume.

---

**CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA**
A moment of private confrontation where one person's discovery of a concealment creates fracture in intimacy. The scene is told as a dance of avoidance and compulsion—Person A must speak; Person B must avoid answering. Cinematically, each shot is positioned to maximize tension through composition rather than movement. The camera is a witness, not a participant.

---

**MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY**
1. Establish: two people, one table, intimacy and distance coexist
2. Accusation: Person A breaks silence first; vulnerability in asking
3. Defense: Person B's resistance; the concealment is real
4. Escalation: Person A will not accept deflection
5. Shutdown: Person B withdraws; tension crystallizes
6. Stasis: Resolution is deferred; the fracture remains

---

**VISUAL_SYSTEM**
- **Color palette:** Warm-neutral café interior (beige/cream walls, wood furniture) with soft golden or overcast diffused light. Slightly desaturated to feel serious and intimate rather than bright.
- **Depth of field:** Close-ups shot with shallow focus (subject sharp, background soft café elements) to isolate emotion; wide shots with sufficient depth to show both figures clearly.
- **Framing principle:** Close-ups favor lower-third positioning and slight off-center placement to suggest discomfort. The wide shot is balanced and level to ground the space.
- **Lighting direction:** Soft directional light from one side (no hard shadows), suggesting late afternoon or north-facing window light.
- **Café elements:** Visible but not dominant—coffee cups, napkins, the table itself. Nothing moves or distracts.

---

**PRODUCTION_RECIPE**

**Location:** Small café or coffee shop with soft ambient light and neutral background. Single table, two chairs opposite each other. Minimal foot traffic.

**Cast:** Two actors. Person A (discoverer): mid-30s to 50s, plays controlled but emotionally activated. Person B (concealer): same age range, plays defensive, careful, avoidant.

**Lighting setup:** Single soft source (bounce board or large window) from screen-left or right, creating even facial illumination without harsh shadows. Ambient café light supplemented as needed.

**Camera:** Static or minimal movement. Tripod-mounted for stability. No handheld, no dolly, no whip pans.

**Audio:** Record clean dialogue on-set. Capture subtle café ambience (distant conversations, clink of cups) as texture layer. No music; silence is the sound design.

**Props:** Two coffee cups (filled or empty), napkin, table, chairs. Person B may hold or fidget with cup.

Prompt:

The moment hangs between them. Warm café light, soft background. Cinematic, 16:9, film aesthetic. The tension is now silence. No one moves."

**DIALOGUE (Approximate Timing)**

Shot 1-2: Person A speaks (2-3 sec into scene)  
"When were you going to tell me?"

Shot 3: Person B responds (deflects, 1 sec)  
"It's not... it's complicated."

Shot 4: Person A (presses, 1 sec)  
"After we were already in this?"

Shot 5: Person B (no answer, reaction only, ~2 sec silence + subtle words like "I couldn't" or just breath)

Shot 6: Both sit in silence (~3 sec)

**DETERMINISTIC_OR_NON_GENERATIVE_ELEMENTS**
- **Dialogue script:** Finalized above (non-generative; actors memorize and perform).
- **Shot list:** Six shots in sequence; order is fixed.
- **Camera positions:** Defined in framing notes; no improvisation.
- **Spatial logic:** Table, two chairs opposite; fixed throughout.
- **Color grading:** Warm-neutral, slightly desaturated; applied consistently in post.
- **Edit timing:** Cuts on lines or emotional beats; no montage or abstraction.

---

**AUDIO_AND_EDIT**

**Dialogue Recording:**
- Record each shot's dialogue cleanly with on-camera microphone or lavs.
- Prioritize clear delivery; actors perform takes until lines feel natural (not flat, not theatrical).
- Capture ambient café sound underneath (low level).

**Edit structure:**
- Cut on dialogue deliveries; favor reaction shots over speaker shots when emotion is strongest.
- No cross-dissolves; hard cuts maintain tension.
- Shot 1: 5 sec → Shot 2: 2 sec (question begins) → Shot 3: 2 sec (deflection) → Shot 4: 2 sec (push) → Shot 5: 3 sec (silence and micro-response) → Shot 6: 5 sec (stasis).
- Total with transitions: ~40 seconds.

**Sound design:**
- Dialogue in center channel, clean and intelligible.
- Subtle café ambience underneath (distant voices, distant clink), mixed very low (~-20dB below dialogue).
- No music.
- Silence in shots 5-6 is intentional and holds tension.

---

**FAILURE_PREVENTION**

1. **Spatial confusion:** Both actors in every close-up must be clearly on opposite sides of the table. Shoot close-ups at opposite angles (camera left for Person A, camera right for Person B) so viewer always knows who is where.

2. **Over-direction:** Actors must feel free to micro-perform (eye flickers, breath shifts). Do not demand broad gestures. The scene lives in restraint.

3. **Lighting inconsistency:** Ensure soft light direction does not flip between close-ups. Key light source should remain from the same direction throughout.

4. **Dialogue flatness:** Rehearse actors before shoot. The lines should feel inevitable, not recited. 

5. **Length creep:** Time each shot precisely during production. If a take is 7 seconds instead of 6, trim in edit or retake.

6. **Awkward cuts:** Do not cut mid-word. Cut on the end of phrases or during reaction beats (blinks, exhales).

7. **Background distraction:** Ensure café background is not busy. No movement behind actors. No other people visible.
