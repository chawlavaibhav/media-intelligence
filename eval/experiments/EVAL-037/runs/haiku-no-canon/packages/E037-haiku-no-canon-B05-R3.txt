FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

---

DELIVERABLE
A 40-second cinematic video scene (16:9) composed of six sequential shots showing a confrontation between two people at a café table. The scene concludes with tension unresolved. Suitable for drama, narrative, or character study context.

---

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
Translate a moment of relational breakdown into visual language using exactly six shots. Prioritize spatial clarity (viewer must always know where each person sits relative to the other) and emotional restraint (tension conveyed through stillness, small gestures, and eye contact, not volume). Avoid wide camera moves and shot-reverse-shot mechanicalism; instead use thoughtful scale variation and light to create cinematic presence within a confined geography.

---

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
A deception revealed at a café table where both parties remain physically still. The emotional collision is internal. The camera does the movement by changing distance and angle; no moving camera required. Spatial geography established once and maintained throughout so viewer can "read" the relationship through position in frame.

---

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Two people. One seat position. Tension. (Shot 1)
2. Accuser's question/realization. (Shot 2)
3. Accused's vulnerability. (Shot 3)
4. The gap between them—both in frame, the space becomes visible conflict. (Shot 4)
5. Accuser's emotional reaction to the non-answer. (Shot 5)
6. The relationship broken open but unresolved; they remain seated, unable to leave. (Shot 6)

---

VISUAL_SYSTEM

**Location:** Contemporary café interior. Afternoon indirect natural light from window stage left. Color palette: warm wood, cool concrete or white walls, natural linen/cotton textiles. No busy patterns. The café is secondary; focus is on the two figures.

**Spatial Geography:**
- Person A (Accuser) sits on camera left side of table, facing into the room.
- Person B (Accused) sits on camera right side of table, facing into the room.
- Small café table between them. Two coffee cups. No clutter.
- This orientation remains constant across all six shots.

**Light:** Soft, directional natural light. No hard shadows on faces. Cool undertones suggest emotional distance. Slight rim light on one person per shot to separate them from background.

**Color Grading:** Desaturated, cool. Slight blue-green cast. Avoid warm, comforting tones.

---

PRODUCTION_RECIPE

**Setup:**
- Two actors seated opposite each other at small café table.
- Establish line-of-action: Person A and Person B face each other across the table, with camera positioned to maintain left-right clarity throughout.
- Camera moves are static tripod positions only. No handheld. No zoom. No pan. Only reposition camera between shots.
- Lighting: Key light from stage left window. No fill light; let shadows exist.

**Shot Sequencing & Timing (40 seconds total):**

| Shot | Duration | Setup | Purpose |
|------|----------|-------|---------|
| 1 | 6s | Wide establishing | Introduce both at table; A's realization |
| 2 | 5s | Medium close-up A | A confronts; quiet, direct |
| 3 | 5s | Medium close-up B | B's initial defensive response |
| 4 | 8s | Medium two-shot, side angle | Both visible; the space between them |
| 5 | 6s | Medium close-up A | A's reaction to non-answer |
| 6 | 10s | Wide two-shot, slight distance | Both seated; unresolved; silence |

---

GENERATION_PROMPTS

**SHOT 1 (Wide Establishing) – 6 seconds**

Camera angle: Medium wide, 45-degree angle to the table. Camera height at eye level seated. Position camera to show both Person A (left of frame) and Person B (right of frame) clearly across the table from each other.

Action: Person A sits, holding a coffee cup near their mouth, reading or looking down. Slowly, A's eyes rise. A's face shifts—a small tightening at the jaw. Realization. A sets the cup down deliberately. A's eyes lock onto B. B has been watching A, waiting, shoulders slightly tense.

Lighting: Soft afternoon light enters from stage left, illuminating the left side of both faces. Right side in shadow. Table surface visible.

Emotion: Shift from unknowing to knowing. The moment before confrontation.

Setting: Contemporary café. Small round or rectangular table. Two cups. Window and neutral walls in background, blurred. Time: afternoon.

Duration: 6 seconds. Approximately 4 seconds of silent observation, then 2 seconds of A and B meeting eyes.

---

**SHOT 2 (Medium Close-up, Person A) – 5 seconds**

Camera angle: Medium close-up of Person A's face. Camera positioned slightly lower, at A's eyeline. Framed from shoulders up. A's left shoulder visible on frame right.

Action: A speaks quietly, with precision. No anger in the voice, but weight. A's eyes are steady, searching B's face. Micro-expressions of disappointment.

Dialogue: "Did you tell them?"

No pause for response in this shot. The question hangs.

Lighting: Left side of A's face catches window light. Right side in shadow. Eyes are clear and lit.

Emotion: Hurt beneath the calm. Control. Waiting for a lie or truth.

Duration: 5 seconds. Dialogue delivers in approximately 2 seconds; the remaining 3 seconds are A's face holding steady.

---

**SHOT 3 (Medium Close-up, Person B) – 5 seconds**

Camera angle: Medium close-up of Person B's face, positioned to mirror Shot 2 spatially—B is on the right side of the frame now (maintaining geography). Framed from shoulders up.

Action: B's expression shifts from defiance to uncertainty. Mouth opens slightly, then closes. Eyes flick down momentarily, then back to A. Breathing is visible—chest slight rise.

Dialogue: "I... it wasn't like that."

Emotion: Caught. Not remorseful yet, but unguarded.

Lighting: Right side of B's face lit by window light (since window is stage left, B is angled away). Left side softer. Face is more in shadow than A's—vulnerability.

Duration: 5 seconds. Dialogue approximately 2 seconds; remaining time is B's face struggling for words.

---

**SHOT 4 (Medium Two-Shot, Side Angle) – 8 seconds**

Camera angle: Side angle view (camera positioned at 90 degrees to the table, or at stage left/right edge). Medium shot framing both A and B in the same frame. The table and space between them are visible and emphasized.

Action: A speaks again, quieter. B responds. The words are less important than the silence between them. Both maintain eye contact or look down at the table. A's hand reaches the table, doesn't touch B. B's posture shifts backward slightly—physical recoil.

Dialogue: 
A: "That's not an answer."
B: "I was going to tell you. I was..."

Emotion: Breakdown. The moment where deception becomes undeniable.

Lighting: Both faces visible, but the space between them is slightly darker—it becomes a visual representation of the gap.

Duration: 8 seconds. Dialogue takes approximately 4 seconds. Remaining 4 seconds are silence and reaction.

---

**SHOT 5 (Medium Close-up, Person A) – 6 seconds**

Camera angle: Medium close-up of Person A's face, tighter than Shot 2. Framed from mid-chest up. A's eyes are darker now—the hurt has solidified into something colder.

Action: A listens but doesn't respond. A's jaw tightens. A looks away—to the left, toward the window or into the café—breaking the eye contact. When A looks back, there's a new distance in the gaze.

No dialogue from A. Silence.

Emotion: Processing betrayal. The moment of acceptance that this relationship has changed.

Lighting: Same as Shot 2. Left side lit. A's eyes catch what light is available—they're the brightest element of the frame.

Duration: 6 seconds. All silence. Action is minimal—the look away, the look back.

---

**SHOT 6 (Wide Two-Shot, Final) – 10 seconds**

Camera angle: Wide shot from the opposite side of the café (camera positioned as if the viewer is deeper in the café, looking toward a window). Both A and B visible at the table, the full table and café architecture around them. They are small in the frame but the focus.

Action: Both A and B are seated, still, at the table. Neither moves. Neither speaks. They sit in the broken silence. A's hand rests on the table; B's hands are in their lap or on the table. Both stare down or into the middle distance. The café around them continues—other patrons are blurred in background, oblivious.

Emotion: The aftermath. The thing that cannot be unsaid or undone, but also cannot be resolved in this moment.

Lighting: Same natural light, but from this distance the scene feels isolated and small.

Duration: 10 seconds. All silence. A beat where the relationship ends, but they remain seated.

- Duration timing (6s + 5s + 5s + 8s + 6s + 10s = 40s)

---

AUDIO_AND_EDIT

**Dialogue Delivery:**
- All lines delivered at conversational level, slightly below normal speaking volume.
- Minimal ambient café sound (distant conversation, light clink of cups, background music turned down).
- Pauses between lines are intentional—silence is part of the tension.
- No music score.

**Sound Design:**
- Ambient café atmosphere: soft, constant, unobtrusive.
- Foley: Hand on cup (Shot 1), cup set down (Shot 1), chair creak or breathing audible in close-ups if it serves the intimacy.
- No sound effects or dramatic stabs.

**Edit Rhythm:**
- Cuts are clean and immediate; no dissolves or transitions.
- Shot transitions occur at natural speech/breath pauses.
- Final shot (Shot 6) holds for full 10 seconds—allow silence to conclude the scene.

---

FAILURE_PREVENTION

**Risk 1: Spatial Confusion**
- *Mitigation:* Each shot description specifies left/right placement from camera perspective. Person A always screen left, Person B always screen right. The medium two-shot (Shot 4) explicitly shows both in frame to reinforce geography. Viewer should never be unsure where each person sits.

**Risk 2: Melodrama**
- *Mitigation:* Dialogue is minimal and restrained. No raised voices. Emotional shifts are in the face and stillness, not in physical action. The long final shot (10 seconds) is designed to resist any temptation to add resolution or dramatic music.

**Risk 3: Camera Movement Fatigue**
- *Mitigation:* All cameras are static. No zoom, pan, or gimbal work. Cuts between static positions feel considered, not jerky. The visual interest comes from composition and performance, not camera pyrotechnics.

**Risk 4: Pacing (40 seconds)**
- *Mitigation:* Shot timings are calculated to avoid dead air while allowing silence to breathe. Close-ups are slightly longer on critical emotional beats. The final wide shot is longer intentionally—it's a rest and a closing statement.

**Risk 5: Generative Inconsistency (Actor Appearance/Position)**
- *Mitigation:* This production recipe assumes actors are cast and positioned in-camera before generation, or that the generative system has reference images/models to maintain consistency. If full AI generation, provide character descriptions and spatial constraints in the generation request. Key facial features and positioning must remain constant across six shots.
