FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

---

**DELIVERABLE**  
Short-form cinematic video: Two-person dialogue scene. 16:9 widescreen. Six fixed or minimal-movement shots. Approximately 40 seconds total. Digital delivery.

---

**OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION**  
Produce a tense, restrained confrontation between two cafe patrons in which spatial clarity and emotional subtext override dialogue volume. The viewer must never be confused about their relative positions. The scene ends unresolved, forcing the viewer to infer the emotional weight of the concealed secret.

---

**CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA**  
Confrontation through restraint. The power of this scene lives in what is *not* said loudly, what is *not* done physically. Camera placement, eye-lines, and timing create tension. The cafe setting (public space, strangers nearby) forces both characters toward civility and control—which amplifies the emotional stakes beneath the surface.

---

**MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY**

1. **Primary:** Spatial clarity—we always know who sits where at the table.
2. **Secondary:** Emotional state—Person A is confronting; Person B is caught/defensive.
3. **Tertiary:** The specific secret—deliberately vague. Dialogue hints but never confirms.
4. **Tone:** Intimate despite public setting. Dialogue measured. Subtext > text.

---

**VISUAL_SYSTEM**

- **Lighting:** Soft daylight from cafe windows. No harsh shadows. Slight warmth to suggest intimate space within a mundane location.
- **Color:** Naturalistic. Muted cafe palette (beige, brown, soft greens). Clothing should not distract—neutral or monochromatic.
- **Composition:** Clean, symmetrical table geometry. Eyelines across the table create visual tension. No Dutch angles.
- **Depth:** Each shot reveals spatial relationship—never ambiguous who is across from whom.

---

**PRODUCTION_RECIPE**

**Setup:**  
A small cafe table, two chairs opposite each other. Minimal props (water glasses, a small coffee cup). Mid-morning or afternoon natural light. Minimal background activity (optional: a few extras out of focus, reinforcing public setting).

**Performance Direction:**  
Both actors kept low in register. Voices measured. Long pauses between sentences. Microexpressions and eye contact carry emotion. No fidgeting; only necessary hand and body movement. Person A leans slightly forward or holds steady; Person B retreats slightly or becomes still.

---

**GENERATION_PROMPTS**

**SHOT 1 – Wide Establishing (Duration: 4 seconds)**  
*Prompt:*  
"Wide shot of a small round cafe table set for two. Woman on the left, man on the right, seated opposite each other. Morning light from a window camera left. Warm, neutral cafe interior. Both figures sit upright, composed. Woman's face shows controlled tension; man appears unguarded. A coffee cup sits between them. Camera is at table height, centered on the table's center. Clean, minimal background. 16:9 widescreen. Cinematic, naturalistic lighting."

*Purpose:* Establish spatial geography and initial emotional tone. Anchor viewer in space.

---

**SHOT 2 – Medium Close-Up on Woman (Person A) (Duration: 5 seconds)**  
*Prompt:*  
"Tight medium shot of a woman's face and shoulders from the front. She sits at a cafe table. Her eyes are direct, searching. Her jaw is set slightly. She breathes slowly. Soft natural light across her face, slight shadow on one side. Her hand rests on the table edge, still. Background is a soft blur of cafe interior and the man across from her (out of focus). 16:9 frame. Cinematic, intimate. She appears to be waiting for an answer."

*Purpose:* Isolate Person A's resolve and barely controlled emotion. Establish that she is driving this moment.

---

**SHOT 3 – Medium Close-Up on Man (Person B) (Duration: 4 seconds)**  
*Prompt:*  
"Tight medium shot of a man's face and shoulders from the front. He sits opposite at a small cafe table. His eyes shift slightly downward, then back to meet the camera. His expression is guarded, caught. A small muscle in his jaw tightens. His hand is out of frame or rests flat on the table. Soft cafe lighting. Blurred cafe background and the woman across from him (out of focus). 16:9 frame. Cinematic. He appears to be choosing his words carefully."

*Purpose:* Isolate Person B's discomfort and concealment. Reverse-angle intimacy. Viewer now sees both perspectives.

---

**SHOT 4 – Two-Shot: Mid-Framing on Both Figures (Duration: 7 seconds)**  
*Prompt:*  
"Medium two-shot from a slight angle, framing both the woman (left) and man (right) across their small cafe table. Both are visible, facing each other. The woman leans in very slightly, her hand now near the center of the table. The man sits back, his shoulders opening defensively. Their eyelines cross toward each other. Warm, soft daylight. Cafe background subtle. 16:9 widescreen. Cinematic depth. The table surface visible between them, anchoring spatial relationship. Tension in stillness."

*Purpose:* Return to shared space after isolation. Dialogue exchange. Eyeline geometry reinforces emotional dynamic.

---

**SHOT 5 – Close on Woman's Face (Person A) (Duration: 6 seconds)**  
*Prompt:*  
"Very close shot on a woman's face. Eyes slightly widened. Her voice is audible but controlled. She asks a direct question. Her eyebrows lower slightly. A flash of hurt beneath the composure. Soft side-light from cafe window. Shallow depth of field. The blurred shape of the man and cafe table visible behind her. 16:9 frame. Cinematic. She is waiting. Silence stretches. Her jaw relaxes slightly as she exhales."

*Purpose:* Close-in on Person A's emotional peak. Micro-expressions convey what dialogue withholds.

---

**SHOT 6 – Final Two-Shot Tight Framing (Duration: 5 seconds)**  
*Prompt:*  
"Tight two-shot: both faces now closer in frame, framing both from mid-chest up across the small table. Woman's face on the left, man's on the right. Both are now still, looking at each other. No one is speaking. The man's eyes are softer, resigned. The woman's face shows a mix of disappointment and sadness. Warm light continues. Background entirely soft and blurred. 16:9 frame. Cinematic, intimate. Silence. A moment passes. Camera holds steady."

*Purpose:* Resolution without resolution. Emotional truth. Viewer understands something has shifted between them, but what was concealed remains partly secret. Scene ends on stillness and unspoken acknowledgment.

**DETERMINISTIC_OR_NON_GENERATIVE_ELEMENTS**

- **Dialogue (provided in shot-level detail):** Exact words must be captured. Actors deliver lines in voiceover or sync sound during generation if required, or text overlay can clarify (not recommended—keep visual). *Alternatively:* Brief dialogue sample to guide tone: 
  - Person A: "You never told me. I had to find out another way."
  - Person B: [Pause] "I was protecting you."
  - Person A: "From what? The truth?"
  - Person B: [No response. Looks down.]
  
  *Recommend:* Generate video with sync-sound dialogue performed by actors, OR generate silent video and add dialogue in post-production to ensure clarity.

- **Table, cafe setting, lighting:** Deterministic. Use reference images if available (cafe table setup, soft natural light, neutral tones).

- **Actor placement and eyeline direction:** Deterministic. Shot compositions must not vary—spatial clarity is non-negotiable.

---

**AUDIO_AND_EDIT**

- **Dialogue:** Tightly scripted. No ad-lib. Delivery: measured, low register, long pauses.
- **Ambient Sound:** Soft cafe background—distant voices, very faint espresso machine, slight clatter. *Not* obtrusive. Sits under dialogue.
- **Sound Design:** Minimal. Let silence do work in moments of tension. No music.
- **Edit Rhythm:** Cuts on pauses, not on dialogue start. Lingering final two-shot (no cut for 2–3 seconds of silence). No J-cuts or L-cuts needed; straightforward sync sound.

---

**FAILURE_PREVENTION**

1. **Spatial confusion:** Every shot must show table and both chairs or clear eyelines. If doubt, re-shoot with wider framing.
2. **Melodrama:** Actors must not over-perform. Micro-expressions only. One take per shot if possible to preserve naturalism.
3. **Dialogue clarity:** If generating with AI, test dialogue sync carefully. Favor subtitles if sync-sound fails.
4. **Lighting continuity:** All six shots must share the same light direction and warmth. Golden hour or diffused morning light recommended.
5. **Unintentional camera movement:** Lock camera to tripod. No handheld drift. Each shot static or uses very slow push-in (no more than 4–5 pixels per second, imperceptible).
