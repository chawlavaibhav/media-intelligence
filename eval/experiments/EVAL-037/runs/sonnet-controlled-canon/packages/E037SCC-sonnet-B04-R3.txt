FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
One 9:16 vertical video, ~25 seconds, single continuous "creator" persona, direct-to-camera + handheld product demo, captioned, delivered as one finished cut (no alternate versions).

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
The customer needs performance-style short-form video that reads as organic creator content (UGC) on Instagram Reels/YouTube Shorts, not a studio TVC, for a fictional Indian D2C brand's fragrance-free 2% salicylic-acid serum aimed at oily, blemish-prone adult skin. Success = a viewer believes a real person is genuinely recommending a product she uses, receives exactly one clear, honest reason to buy, and can act on it — while the packaging stays legible and the brand-safety/legal boundary (no invented clinical claims, no guarantees, no medical claims) is respected throughout.

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
"The one thing I actually needed to hear." A woman with oily, blemish-prone skin talks to camera the way she would to a friend: she's tried actives that either did nothing or irritated her skin, and this serum's single distinguishing virtue — a 2% salicylic-acid formula that's fragrance-free, lightweight and doesn't feel harsh — is the one reason she's stuck with it. No before/after, no percentage-improvement claims, no dermatologist voice-over: just one credible, specific, sensorial reason, demonstrated live.

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Hook (who this is for): "if you've got oily, breakout-prone skin" — instant relevance, 0–3s.
2. Proof-by-demonstration: she actually applies it on camera (texture, absorption, no shine left behind) — 3–9s.
1a/anchor. The single purchase reason, stated once, plainly: fragrance-free 2% salicylic acid, formulated for oily/blemish-prone skin, doesn't sting or leave a film — 9–16s.
3. Product identification: clean legible pack shot so the brand/product is recognizable — 16–21s.
4. Call to action: casual, low-pressure sign-off pointing to purchase — 21–25s.
Only one claim is allowed to be the "reason"; everything else is texture/credibility support.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Aspect: 9:16, full-bleed, phone-shot aesthetic (slight handheld micro-movement throughout, no gimbal-smooth moves).
- Setting: home bathroom counter or bedroom vanity with real morning window light — believable Indian urban household, warm neutral tones, slightly cluttered-but-tidy counter (real toothbrush, other bottles) to signal "lived-in," not a set.
- Talent: one Indian woman, late-20s, everyday styling, minimal/no visible makeup, oily-prone but not medically inflamed skin (no depiction of active lesions/medical treatment), natural hair, casual homewear.
- Lighting: soft, direction-consistent daylight (window key + bounce fill), matching Light & Magic's reflection-control logic — the glass/plastic serum bottle should be lit so its highlight sits inside a "family of angles" that reads as clean glass rather than blown-out glare, and kept consistent between the demo shot and the pack-shot insert so the product doesn't visually "jump" between takes.
- Color: neutral warm-naturalistic grade, minimal stylization — deliberately not glossy/commercial-grade, consistent with UGC credibility.
- Framing: mix of tight direct-to-camera (chest-up) and true macro inserts (dropper, fingertip, cheek) — tight framing and a mid-action or close-up opening are used deliberately to hook attention fast without an artificial "ad" cold-open.

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
Format: 5 shots, vertical, ~25s total, edited with hard/jump cuts (not dissolves) to preserve amateur-creator rhythm.

- Burned-in captions (auto-caption style, matching UGC convention) for accessibility and scroll-sound-off viewing, synced to dialogue exactly as written above.
- End-card/CTA text overlay in final ~1s freeze or hold: brand name + generic purchase prompt (e.g., "Shop now" / handle), styled as a simple platform-native sticker, not a broadcast lower-third.
- Any on-screen ingredient callout ("2% Salicylic Acid," "Fragrance-Free") shown as text must mirror only the actual formulation facts already given — no numeric outcome claims added.

AUDIO_AND_EDIT
- Dialogue: single continuous natural read across all 5 shots, casual conversational pacing, minor imperfections (small pauses, informal phrasing) intentionally kept in.
- Room tone: light natural ambience (bathroom echo/room tone), phone-mic quality — not a clean boom recording — to preserve authenticity.
- SFX: soft cap-unscrew and dropper-squeeze sound in Shot 2, kept subtle and diegetic.
- Music: none, or a very low trending-style bed under Shots 4–5 only, kept under dialogue level — never masking speech (per platform-audio-reliance caution below).
- Edit: hard jump cuts between all shots, no dissolves/wipes, mild handheld continuity break between cuts (expected/typical of UGC, not a flaw), captions burned in.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- No invented statistics, before/after percentages, "clinically proven," "dermatologist recommended," guarantee language, or medical/therapeutic claims anywhere in dialogue or on-screen text — only the stated formulation facts (2% salicylic acid, fragrance-free) and subjective sensory language ("doesn't sting," "doesn't feel greasy").
- Do not depict active medical-grade acne lesions, treatment devices, or implied dermatological procedure — keep skin depiction realistic but non-clinical.
- Keep product label/bottle geometry and highlight position consistent across Shots 2 and 4 so the pack doesn't visually "shift" between inserts (continuity, not just realism).
- Avoid over-polished lighting/grade that reads as a TVC — resist the instinct to smooth skin, add glamour lighting, or use a cinematic LUT; the deliberately "amateur" handheld quality is a credibility feature, not a defect, consistent with testimonial-credibility guidance that overly polished delivery reads as less trustworthy.
- Watch for common generative-hand/product artifacts during the dropper/application close-up (extra fingers, bottle geometry warping) — this insert should get the most QC scrutiny.
- Ensure captions match spoken claim exactly (no caption drift into stronger claims than the audio states).
