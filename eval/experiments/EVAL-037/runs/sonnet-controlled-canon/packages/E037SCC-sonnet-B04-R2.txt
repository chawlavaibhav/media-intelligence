FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
One 9:16 vertical video, ~25 seconds, single continuous "creator" narrative (edited as 5 quick-cut shots in the same location), for use as a paid social / performance ad (Reels/Shorts/Stories-style placement). No external website referenced or required.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
This is a performance-marketing asset that must pass as native creator content inside a feed, not read as a studio TVC, while remaining brand-safe, legally conservative, and edit-controlled enough to be reused as a paid ad. Success = a viewer believes a real person is talking to them, understands in one sentence why this serum suits her specifically, and is moved toward a purchase action — without any invented clinical percentage, before/after claim, guarantee, or medical framing beyond the two factual formulation facts the customer already supplied (2% salicylic acid, fragrance-free).

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
"Finally, a serum made for MY skin, not everyone's skin." A relatable Indian woman, filming herself at her own bathroom/vanity counter, interrupts her own thought to explain the one reason she switched: this serum was actually formulated for oily, blemish-prone skin — salicylic acid at 2%, no fragrance to irritate it — so it behaves the way her skin actually needs, not the way a "universal" serum behaves. The single, clear purchase reason is fit-for-skin-type + irritation-avoidance, demonstrated sensorially (fast absorption, no sting, no greasy film, no scent), never claimed clinically.

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Hook (0–3s): relatable oily/breakout-prone-skin pain point, mid-action, no logo bumper.
2. Context (3–9s): why past serums failed her (too harsh, too heavy, fragranced).
3. Product + single purchase reason (9–16s): formulated for oily/blemish-prone skin, 2% salicylic acid, fragrance-free — shown while demonstrated on skin.
4. Sensory payoff (16–22s): honest, small, non-clinical reaction (absorbs fast, no sting, no residue).
5. CTA (22–25s): brand name spoken + shown, soft direct ask, static end-card.
This order deliberately places the ask after context is established rather than front-loading it, and pairs the spoken brand name with the on-screen product/logo at the same moment for reinforcement.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Aspect ratio: 9:16, full-bleed, phone-camera field of view (mild wide-angle distortion typical of a front camera).
- Camera: handheld, natural micro-shake, no gimbal-smooth movement, occasional minor reframe as if she adjusted her phone mid-take.
- Lighting: single practical source — window daylight key from one side + ordinary bathroom/vanity practical light as fill; no beauty dish, no rim light, no glamour lighting. Skin must show real texture (light shine on T-zone, a couple of visible closed blemishes) — not airbrushed, no beauty filter.
- Setting: her own home bathroom/bedroom vanity, lived-in, minor real clutter (toothbrush, one other skincare bottle), consistent across all shots.
- Wardrobe/appearance: mid-to-late-20s Indian woman, casual home top, hair tied back or loose, bare face at start, natural unfiltered skin tone appropriate to a general Indian audience (avoid uniform fairness bias).
- Editing texture: jump-cut style typical of UGC (not cross-dissolves), slightly inconsistent framing shot to shot as if self-filmed in one sitting, burned-in bold sans-serif captions synced to speech, one factual on-screen super during the demonstration beat only.
- No studio graphics, no slow-motion glamour inserts, no dramatic score swells.

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
1. Lock a single continuous location and consistent character description (reused verbatim across every generation prompt) to preserve identity/continuity.
2. Generate/shoot the 5 shots below in order; treat cuts as intentional jump-cuts, not seamless transitions — this is a UGC authenticity cue, not an error.
3. Record/generate her own scratch dialogue as guide audio; keep natural pacing including one small self-correction or stumble (e.g., "it's- okay let me start over") to avoid an over-rehearsed, ad-like delivery register.
4. Add burned-in captions (karaoke/word-highlight style common to creator content).
5. Composite one factual on-screen text super ("2% Salicylic Acid · Fragrance-Free") only over the demonstration shot — factual, ingredient-level, not a performance claim.
6. Insert a static end-card (brand lockup + product name + soft CTA text) as the final ~1 second — this is a designed, non-generative graphic asset, not part of the live-action generation.
7. Grade lightly to match a phone-camera colour profile (slightly warm, restrained saturation) — avoid a cinematic LUT.

DETERMINISTIC_OR_NON_GENERATIVE_ELEMENTS
- End-card graphic: brand logo lockup, product name, one-line CTA text, and a small standard skincare disclaimer ("Patch test recommended before first use") — designed as a static asset in post, not generated.
- Burned-in captions: produced from the locked VO script, not generated per-frame.
- On-screen ingredient super ("2% Salicylic Acid · Fragrance-Free"): a designed text overlay, factual and static, composited once over Shot 3.
- Product label/packaging artwork: should be a real designed asset (typography, brand mark) composited onto the bottle prop/insert shots where legibility matters, rather than left to open-ended generation, to guarantee the label is legible and commercially correct.

AUDIO_AND_EDIT
- Primary audio: her own spoken VO, synced to lips, natural room tone underneath (not studio-clean).
- Optional low-bed background: a single subtle, low-volume trending-style music bed (well under dialogue, ~-18dB), consistent with native creator content conventions — not a cinematic score.
- Minimal SFX only where diegetic: bottle cap/pump click, soft patting sound during application.
- Edit: hard jump-cuts between the 5 shots (not dissolves), captions cut in sync with speech, end-card is the only fully static, non-handheld frame in the piece.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- Do not add any statistic, percentage-improvement, dermatologist-endorsement, before/after, or "clears acne" guarantee language anywhere in dialogue or supers — only the two given formulation facts (2% salicylic acid, fragrance-free) and sensory description are permitted.
- Do not let the delivery become too polished/rehearsed-sounding — retain the natural stumble/self-correction; an overly smooth read will register as a TVC, undermining the brief.
- Do not apply beauty filters, skin smoothing, or glamour lighting — this breaks the "credible creator" requirement and can visually contradict an oily/blemish-prone-skin narrative.
- Do not show broken/inflamed skin in extreme close-up (compliance/sensitivity), and do not stage a clinical/white-coat setting.
- Keep brand name/CTA legible and spoken close together (audio+visual pairing) so the ask isn't missed on sound-off autoplay; ensure captions cover 100% of dialogue for sound-off viewing.
- Keep the ask (CTA) after context is established, not front-loaded, to match how this format is meant to land as an "action" objective piece.
