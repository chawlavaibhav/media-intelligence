FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
One (1) premium advertising still image, 4:5 vertical (deliver at 4000×5000 px, RGB, 300ppi print-safe / social-safe), for a new Indian mosambi (sweet lime) + sparkling water beverage. Single hero-product composition. No celebrity, no external location/website branding, no logotype of any existing company.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
The brief asks for a beverage image that reads as premium and contemporary to a 22–35 urban Indian audience while keeping the fruit (mosambi) recognisable and emotionally familiar — without using the usual "Indianness" shorthand (flag colours, paisley, diyas, marigolds, Taj Mahal silhouettes, Devanagari flourishes) and without the visual grammar of a mass-market fizzy soft drink (neon colour, cartoon condensation, cluttered can graphics, harsh top-light). The task is therefore a materiality and restraint problem: signal "premium sparkling citrus" through glass, light, colour and fruit realism rather than through cultural iconography or soda-category tropes, and keep the product itself as the unambiguous focal subject.

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
"Modern Citrus Clarity" — the bottle is treated as a small glass object of quality (closer to a fine tonic/craft-soda or premium water aesthetic than a soda-fountain drink). Mosambi's familiarity is carried entirely through colour (pale, sun-washed chartreuse-gold), a single freshly cut mosambi half/twist, and visible natural pulp/texture in the liquid — never through text, ethnic props, or literal "Indian" scene-setting. Sophistication comes from restrained studio lighting, a soft tonal gradient backdrop, and jewel-like carbonation detail (fine bubbles, a controlled droplet field, a frozen micro-splash), positioning the product as contemporary craft rather than commodity soda.

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Product bottle — largest, sharpest, highest-contrast element, dead-centre vertical axis (unmistakable hero, per the classic "make the product the hero" print-advertising principle).
2. Mosambi cue — single fresh-cut fruit element (half + twist) at the base/side, colour-matched to the liquid, confirming flavour instantly without a fruit pile or crate cliché.
3. Carbonation/refreshment cue — rising bubble trail inside the liquid + light condensation on glass + one restrained arrested droplet/mist detail, communicating "sparkling" and "cold" without a splashy soda-ad explosion.
4. Brand mark — small, minimal wordmark/lockup in the upper third negative space, Latin-script only, no supporting clutter.
5. Optional micro-tagline (e.g., a two-to-four-word line) — smallest element, bottom third, only if brand supplies copy; otherwise omit rather than force text.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Palette: pale chartreuse-gold liquid; warm ivory/soft stone background gradient (not white studio, not saturated soda-green); one metallic accent (brushed brass/warm gold cap or foil detail) to signal premium; no red/orange/tricolour soda cues.
- Container: slim, tall glass bottle (not a can, not moulded PET) with a minimal debossed or single-colour label — glass chosen specifically to read "premium" rather than "mass beverage."
- Light: soft, large top-rear key to build a clean vertical specular highlight down the bottle's curve; low-angle bounce fill at front to hold shadow detail; background lit separately on a gentle gradient so the bottle silhouette separates cleanly. Key raised high enough, and a small gobo positioned at the label's reflection angle, so the label stays legible and free of blown specular glare while the glass still gets its describing highlight (a standard product-photography glass/label conflict resolution).
- Composition: vertical thirds; bottle occupies the central third at ~60–65% of frame height; generous negative space above for the brand lockup and below for the fruit garnish and a soft reflection/shadow anchor; background kept clean with only a very soft, out-of-focus suggestion of citrus-grove greenery (barely legible, purely atmospheric — not a literal orchard scene) to avoid both blankness and cliché.
- Texture: photographic realism — real-glass refraction, true condensation bead behaviour, natural pulp flecks in the liquid, matte-not-glossy label finish.
- Typography direction (for the deterministic overlay, not generated): clean modern sans or a contemporary serif, tracked out, lower-case or small-caps, no ornamental Indian script styling — contemporary Indian design increasingly signals identity through material and colour rather than through overt script/text ornament, which supports this restraint.

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
- Framing: 4:5 vertical, product-macro perspective (equivalent ~90–100mm macro look), camera at bottle mid-height, very slight downward tilt (~5°) to show liquid clarity and the fruit garnish plane.
- Depth of field: moderate-shallow (bottle fully sharp front-to-back label and cap; background falls to soft bokeh within ~1 bottle-width behind it).
- Lighting rig: large soft key above and slightly behind subject for the vertical glass highlight; secondary soft fill camera-left/front-low to open shadow side of bottle without flattening it; small negative-fill or flag camera-right to preserve one deep edge for dimensionality (bright side / shadow side / mid side, not a fully lit flat wrap); background gradient lit independently, warm ivory to pale stone, no hot spots.
- Styling: bottle chilled to natural, not artificially heavy, condensation — light bead pattern concentrated lower third, one or two larger runoff beads, not uniform "spray" coating; one clean mosambi half with visible pulp and a thin peel twist resting against/near the base; a small number of natural-looking bubbles rising inside the liquid; optional single arrested droplet or micro-splash accent near the cap, not a violent splash crown.
- Surface: matte pale stone or brushed concrete plinth (very subtle, out of focus) to avoid a "coaster on a kitchen counter" read and avoid an overtly rustic/ethnic surface choice.
- Reflection: soft, short, controlled tabletop reflection under the bottle for premium studio polish.

GENERATION_PROMPTS
Single final generation prompt (image):

"Premium advertising product photograph, 4:5 vertical composition, a single slim tall glass bottle of pale chartreuse-gold sparkling citrus beverage standing centered on a softly lit warm ivory-to-stone gradient background. Photorealistic glass refraction, fine natural condensation beads concentrated on the lower half of the bottle, a few natural rising carbonation bubbles visible inside the pale liquid, a minimal unlabelled or single-tone matte label area left blank for text, a small brushed-brass or matte gold bottle cap. Beside the base, one freshly cut mosambi (sweet lime) half showing visible pulp and juice sheen, plus a thin curled peel twist, softly resting on a pale matte stone surface. Soft large top-rear key light creating one clean vertical specular highlight along the bottle's curve, gentle front-low fill opening the shadow side, one edge kept slightly darker for dimensional roundness, no harsh hotspots on the label area. Background gradient clean and airy, with only a barely-visible, extremely soft out-of-focus suggestion of green citrus-leaf bokeh at the very top edge, kept subtle and non-literal. Short soft reflection of the bottle on the stone surface. Overall mood: sophisticated, contemporary, minimal, refreshing, high-end still-life beverage photography — not a soda advertisement. Studio product photography, ultra-sharp on bottle and fruit, soft shallow falloff on background, natural colour grading, no text, no logos, no additional props, no people, no saturated primary colours, no traditional Indian iconography (no paisley, no marigold, no diya, no tricolour, no Devanagari script), no plastic bottle, no can, no splashing crown or violent liquid explosion, no cartoon-style bubbles."

Negative/exclusion terms to enforce: celebrity/human figures, tricolour or flag palette, paisley/ethnic pattern, diya/marigold/festival props, Taj Mahal or monument silhouettes, Devanagari lettering, neon soda colours, plastic PET bottle, aluminium can, cluttered label design, cartoonish splash, visible brand names of real products.

CONSTRUCTION_PLAN (post-generation, single-image workflow):
1. Generate base hero image per the prompt above at final 4:5 resolution.
2. Colour-grade pass: confirm liquid reads as pale citrus-gold (not neon lime, not orange-soda), background stays warm-neutral, one metallic accent present.
3. Retouch pass: clean up any label artefacting/text-garbling from generation; if the model rendered illegible pseudo-text on the label, remove/smooth it to a blank matte field.
4. Deterministic overlay pass (see next section): place brand wordmark/lockup and optional tagline as vector type in the pre-defined upper-third negative space; place any mandatory regulatory text (net quantity, FSSAI logo placeholder, etc.) in small print at the bottom margin per packaging-law convention — added as clean vector type, not generated.
5. Final polish: verify product remains the single largest, sharpest, most contrasted element in the frame (hero check) before lock.

DETERMINISTIC_OR_NON_GENERATIVE_ELEMENTS
- Brand wordmark/logotype and tagline: created and placed as vector type in post-production, not left to the generative model, since generative text is unreliable and brand identity must be pixel-precise.
- Any mandatory regulatory/label copy (net volume, FSSAI-style mark, ingredient callouts): added as deterministic vector overlay in the final compositing step, positioned in the bottom margin, kept small and out of the primary sightline.
- Final colour calibration/grading pass: done deterministically in post to lock exact palette values (liquid hue, background gradient stops, metallic accent tone) rather than trusting generation variance.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- Risk: generated label text becomes garbled pseudo-script → Mitigation: prompt for a blank/minimal label field; add all real type in post as deterministic vector overlay.
- Risk: image drifts toward mass soft-drink visual grammar (neon colour, cartoon splash, can shape) → Mitigation: explicit negative prompts against can/plastic/neon/cartoon splash; palette locked to pale chartreuse-gold + ivory + brass; container specified as glass.
- Risk: "Indian" cues rendered as literal ethnic iconography → Mitigation: explicit exclusion list (paisley, diya, marigold, tricolour, Devanagari, monuments); cultural identity carried only through the mosambi fruit itself and warm/earthy tonal choice, not through symbolic props — avoiding the common category habit of grafting generic "heritage" signifiers onto packaging for differentiation.
- Risk: product loses hero status to background or garnish → Mitigation: composition rule enforced (bottle largest/sharpest/highest-contrast, centred, background and fruit strictly secondary in scale and focus).
- Risk: glass highlight vs. label legibility conflict (light needed for the glass sheen also glares the label) → Mitigation: key light raised/angled and a gobo used at the label's reflection angle so the glass keeps its describing highlight while the label plane stays clean, per standard glass-product lighting practice.
