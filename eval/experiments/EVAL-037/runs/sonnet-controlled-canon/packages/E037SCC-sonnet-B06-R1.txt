FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
One (1) still image, 4:5 vertical aspect ratio (e.g., 3200×4000 px final), premium e‑commerce hero/PDP-hero image for the "Aster Meridian 38" mechanical watch. Single deliverable, no variants.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
The image must function as a primary product-page hero: it has to sell the watch on sight by making material quality, dial craftsmanship and wrist-desirability legible within a fraction of a second, while being clean and neutral enough to sit next to price, buy button and spec copy without visual competition. It must read as a real, physically credible object — not a rendered floating asset — because credibility of materials (steel, sapphire, leather, sunburst finish) is the entire commercial argument for a watch at this positioning. All stated product facts are treated as fixed and non-negotiable identity constraints, not stylistic suggestions.

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
A grounded, tabletop studio photograph aesthetic: the watch rests at a confident three-quarter angle on a dark, softly gradated surface, angled so the crown and dial catch a controlled raking light. The sunburst dial's radial brushing is caught mid-sweep (glinting arc across the blue, not flat), the sapphire dome shows a single believable curved highlight along its edge (proof of domed glass) without a reflection blob over the hour markers, the case's brushed finish shows directional grain with one bright specular spine along the bezel, and the strap curls naturally into soft shadow in the lower foreground, showing grain and stitching. Nothing floats, nothing is CGI-clean; it looks shot, not rendered.

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Primary read (first glance): "This is a real, finely made watch" — silhouette, blue dial glow, steel gleam.
2. Secondary read (on dwell): dial detail — baton markers, dauphine hands, sunburst texture, absence of clutter (no date window, confirming the clean dial promise).
3. Tertiary read: material story — brushed steel case texture, domed sapphire curvature, leather strap grain/stitching, all confirming "craftsmanship" as the underlying brand claim.
4. No text, logo or price is baked into the image; it is a pure hero photograph meant to carry copy placed by the page template outside the image (safe margins reserved top and bottom).

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Camera framing: 3/4 view, watch head as clear visual hero (Ogilvy's product-as-hero principle: product occupies the visual and psychological center, no competing elements) — dial and case fill approx. 55–65% of frame height, strap trailing into soft lower-frame shadow, generous negative space above for page headline overlay.
- Angle: watch tilted ~30–35° toward camera, crown pointing lower-right, so the sunburst dial's radial grain catches a distinct sweep of light rather than reading flat.
- Background: seamless dark charcoal-to-near-black gradient, cool-neutral, subtly darker at edges (vignette) to isolate the product without looking like a void; no props, no hands, no wrist.
- Surface: dark matte-satin tabletop with a very soft, short, believable contact shadow/reflection gradient directly under the case — enough to ground the object physically, not a mirror-clone reflection.
- Color palette: deep blue dial as the single chromatic accent against neutral charcoal/steel/brown; strap brown kept warm but not competing with the cool blue-steel dominant.

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
Lighting logic (grounded in classic reflective-surface product lighting principle — "the elegant compromise": one large soft key does double duty, since a polished/metallic surface only responds to light within its narrow reflection "family of angles" while the diffuse background responds to the whole source):
- Key: one large soft overhead-to-front softbox, positioned so a portion of it sits inside the reflection family of the bezel and hands (making steel and dauphine hands bright and defined) while its broader spread lifts the dial and case body softly.
- Fill: low-contrast fill card camera-left to hold shadow detail in the brushed case flank and strap without flattening the sunburst texture.
- Negative fill / gobo: a black flag opposite the key, positioned to place a dark, edge-defining reflection along the case's far rim and bezel edge — this is what separates the steel silhouette from the dark background and reads as "premium," rather than the metal blending into a flat grey.
- Crystal control: light and camera geometry set so the domed sapphire's direct reflection falls as a slim curved arc near the crystal's edge/bevel, deliberately kept off the hour markers and hand-cluster so dial legibility is not sacrificed for material proof.
- Strap: soft secondary light or bounce, low angle, to skim across the leather grain and stitching without hot spots.
- Distance/size control used as the fine-tuning tool: source size and distance are set so the case reads as a rich, deep steel tone (not blown white, not muddy grey) while the background stays a clean dark gradient independently.

Post-checks before accepting a frame (derived from standard polished-metal QA logic):
- Does the steel show real tonal range (dark-to-bright sweep across the brushed grain), or has it collapsed into a flat mid-grey card look? If flat, the piece will look like plastic — reject.
- Does the case's silhouette separate cleanly from the background at every edge (no edge merging into a same-tone backdrop)?
- Is there any hard-edged rectangular light-source reflection sitting artificially in the crystal or bezel? If yes, it must be softened/curved to look like a natural bevel highlight, not a studio-light cutout.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- Date window reappearing on the dial (common generative default) → explicitly excluded in both positive framing ("no date window") and negative constraints; verify by inspection at 3 and 6 o'clock positions before acceptance.
- Hands rendered as generic straight batons instead of dauphine (faceted, tapered) → explicitly specified and checked.
- Crystal glare or a hard rectangular light-source reflection sitting over the dial center, killing legibility → controlled via geometry (arc near edge only) and flagged as a rejection condition.
- Steel case rendering as flat mid-grey (loses "brushed steel" credibility) or as blown-white/plastic-looking → checked against the tonal-range QA rule; regenerate lighting pass if it fails.
- Case/dial proportions drifting (oversized case, wrong marker count/spacing, asymmetric hands, extra complications) → locked via explicit geometry/count language and rejected on inspection.
- Drift toward generic "floating product on white" CGI render look → explicitly excluded via grounded tabletop/shadow/background staging and "no CGI sheen" negative constraint.
- Strap looking synthetic/rubbery instead of leather → grain, stitching and natural curl specified, checked visually.
