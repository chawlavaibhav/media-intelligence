FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
A single 16:9 short cinematic dialogue scene, approximately 40 seconds, told in exactly six shots, depicting two people at a café table in the moment one confronts the other over a concealed matter. Restrained performance, no camera flourish, unbroken spatial logic throughout.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
The customer wants a scene that reads as authored cinema, not "coverage" — six shots is a tight budget, so every shot must earn its place (establish, escalate, or land the beat) rather than mechanically alternating faces. The two non-negotiable craft requirements are: (1) the viewer must never lose track of who is where relative to whom, and (2) tension must be carried by restraint — stillness, micro-expression, silence, precise blocking — not by movement or volume.

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
A locked-off, geometrically disciplined dialogue scene built on one consistent axis of action, bookended by a returning wide shot. The confrontation escalates through two clean reciprocal singles, then resolves into a single wordless insert of the concealed object before returning to the opening wide framing — so the scene has a visual rhyme (out → in → object → out) instead of a flat back-and-forth, giving it a felt shape rather than a mechanical shot/reverse-shot rhythm.

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Space and relationship (who they are to each other, that they are opposite one another) — Shot 1.
2. The accusation is made, quietly — Shot 2.
3. The concealer's controlled reaction — Shot 3.
4. The confronter's precise follow-through — Shot 4.
5. Visual proof / the concealed thing itself, silent — Shot 5.
6. Unresolved aftermath, held in the same space we started in — Shot 6.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Characters: ELARA (discoverer, frame-left throughout) and MARCUS (concealer, frame-right throughout) — fixed screen positions, established in Shot 1 and never crossed.
- Wardrobe: muted, contemporary, unbranded — Elara in a soft charcoal knit, Marcus in a plain slate-blue shirt — low-saturation palette so nothing pulls focus from faces/hands.
- Location: small independent café, window-side two-top table, shallow depth of field background with soft bokeh of other patrons/counter activity for life without distraction.
- Light: naturalistic overcast window light, cool-neutral, slight warm practical fill from an interior lamp off-frame; soft contrast, no dramatic chiaroscuro — restraint in lighting matches restraint in performance.
- Lens family: consistent optics across the scene — wide two-shots on a ~35–40mm equivalent, singles and the insert on ~85mm equivalent, shallow stop for singles to isolate faces once the geography is locked in.
- Camera support: locked-off tripod for all six shots. No pans, no dolly, no handheld. Only the bookend wide shots may carry an almost imperceptible breathing drift (sub-pixel, not a move) to avoid a dead, static-photo feel.
- The concealed object: a worn, unmarked folded letter/envelope sitting on the table from the start of Shot 1, positioned consistently (same table position, same orientation) in every shot where it's visible, so it reads as one continuous prop rather than continuity-breaking set dressing. No legible text is ever needed or shown — its meaning is carried by handling and framing, not by readable content.

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
Governing rule: establish the action line once, in Shot 1, via the two characters' mutual sightline across the table; every subsequent camera setup stays on the same side of that line (Elara camera-left/looking frame-right, Marcus camera-right/looking frame-left) with no exceptions. Successive same-subject shots differ in angle by a clearly perceptible amount (well over a marginal shift) so no pair reads as a jump cut. The two singles (Shots 3 and 4) are true reciprocal pairs — matching height, matching look-room, mirrored across the line — so the cut between them produces a clean eye-trace from one face to the other. Shot 5 breaks the face/face pattern deliberately with a non-face insert, both to vary the rhythm cinematically and to let the object itself carry a beat of information the dialogue doesn't state aloud. Shots 1 and 6 are the same setup, reused, to bookend the scene and make the six shots feel like a composed structure rather than raw coverage.

GENERATION_PROMPTS
(16:9, locked-off camera unless noted; restrained, near-whisper-level delivery, long pauses, no raised voices)

SHOT 1 — WIDE TWO-SHOT (establishing) — ~8s
"Static wide shot, 16:9, small café interior, soft overcast window light. A two-top table centered in frame, two people seated opposite each other: a woman in a charcoal knit sweater on the left of frame, a man in a slate-blue shirt on the right of frame, both in profile-three-quarter facing each other across the table. A worn, folded envelope sits on the table between them, closer to the woman's side. Shallow bokeh of café activity behind them. Both figures still, tense stillness, coffee cups untouched. Locked-off camera, no movement. Naturalistic ambient light, muted color palette."
Purpose: establish geography, the axis of action, and the object.

SHOT 2 — MEDIUM TWO-SHOT favoring Elara — ~6s
"Static medium two-shot, 16:9, same café table, camera on the same side as Shot 1. Woman (charcoal knit) in the foreground-left, sharp focus, facing frame-right; man (slate-blue shirt) soft-focused over her shoulder on the right, facing frame-left. She speaks quietly, controlled, restrained anger — no raised voice, minimal gesture, her hand resting near the envelope. Natural window light, shallow depth of field, locked-off camera."
Dialogue (restrained, quiet): "You said you didn't know."
Purpose: the accusation, delivered without heat.

SHOT 3 — SINGLE, Marcus (reciprocal) — ~6s
"Static medium close-up, 16:9, man in slate-blue shirt, positioned right of frame, eyes looking frame-left, matching the sightline established in the wide shot. Café bokeh behind him. His expression is tightly controlled — a small tell: jaw tightens, eyes drop briefly to the table then rise again. He does not raise his voice. Shallow depth of field, naturalistic light, locked-off camera, no movement."
Dialogue (low, measured): "It wasn't — it isn't what you think."
Purpose: controlled reaction from the concealer; reciprocal single, mirrors Shot 2's eyeline exactly.

SHOT 4 — SINGLE, Elara (reciprocal) — ~6s
"Static medium close-up, 16:9, woman in charcoal knit, positioned left of frame, eyes looking frame-right, mirroring the reciprocal framing of the previous single. Her hands are still, folded near her cup. Quiet, precise delivery, no shouting, sustained eye contact implied toward off-frame subject. Shallow depth of field, same lighting continuity as prior shots, locked-off camera."
Dialogue (quiet, precise): "Then tell me what it is."
Purpose: the follow-through; completes the reciprocal pair, escalates tension without volume.

SHOT 5 — INSERT, the object — ~5s
"Static extreme close/insert shot, 16:9, tabletop surface, shallow depth of field. A worn, folded envelope sits exactly where it was placed in Shot 1, same orientation, same table position. A hand (woman's, charcoal sleeve visible at frame edge) enters and rests two fingers on the envelope without opening it. No dialogue. Ambient café sound only. Locked-off camera, no movement."
Purpose: wordless proof beat; breaks the face/face rhythm deliberately for cinematic pacing.

SHOT 6 — WIDE TWO-SHOT (return) — ~9s
"Static wide shot, identical framing and camera position to Shot 1, 16:9, same café table, same soft window light. Woman on left, man on right, same postures re-established. The envelope has been moved slightly toward the center of the table by the hand from the previous shot. Neither speaks. He looks down; she watches him, unmoving. Long held silence, ambient café sound only, locked-off camera, no movement, hold to end of scene."
Purpose: bookend return; unresolved aftermath held in the same space as the opening — closes the structure.

DETERMINISTIC_OR_NON_GENERATIVE_ELEMENTS
- Fixed character wardrobe and prop (envelope) descriptions to be locked as a style/continuity reference before generation, reused verbatim across all six prompts to maximize cross-shot consistency.
- Envelope kept free of legible text/graphics by design, avoiding generative-text legibility failure and continuity mismatches across shots.
- Screen-left/screen-right assignment (Elara left, Marcus right) fixed as a hard constraint carried into every prompt, never varied.

AUDIO_AND_EDIT
- Dialogue: near-whisper to quiet-conversational volume throughout; no shouted lines anywhere in the scene.
- Ambient bed: low café room tone (distant chatter, cup clinks, espresso machine) present under all six shots, slightly recessed under dialogue shots, slightly more present in the silent Shot 5 and Shot 6.
- No score until Shot 6, where a single sustained low tone (piano or string, very quiet) enters under the silence and is allowed to hang past picture out.
- Cuts are placed on the ends of held beats/pauses rather than on movement, reinforcing restraint; the cut from Shot 4 to Shot 5 is the one deliberate rhythm-break (face to object) and should feel like a intentional held breath, not a jump.
- Edit pacing: 8s / 6s / 6s / 6s / 5s / 9s ≈ 40s total.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- Maintain one continuous action line across all six setups: Elara always frame-left/looking frame-right, Marcus always frame-right/looking frame-left; the camera never crosses to the opposite side, which prevents both characters from appearing to address an unseen third person.
- Shots 3 and 4 are true reciprocal singles (matched height, matched look-room, mirrored across the line) so the eye-trace at the cut lands cleanly on the new face rather than confusing the viewer.
- Successive shots of the same subject are angled distinctly enough (wide vs. medium vs. close, not marginal reframes) to avoid jump-cut ambiguity.
- Shot 5 deliberately breaks the pure shot/reverse-shot pattern with a non-face insert — this is the specific technique for making a small shot count feel authored rather than mechanically covered, and it also gives a safe fallback beat if a performance take doesn't read well on faces.
- Prop (envelope) position is held identical in Shots 1, 5, and 6 to avoid a continuity break the viewer would register as a spatial error.
- No camera movement is used to manufacture tension — tension is carried entirely by blocking, stillness, and restrained delivery, per the brief's explicit ban on shouting and elaborate movement.
