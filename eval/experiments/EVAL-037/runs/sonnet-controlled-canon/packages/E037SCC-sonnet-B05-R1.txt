FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
A single 16:9 short-form cinematic dialogue scene, approx. 40 seconds, six shots, café interior, two seated actors ("A" = the discoverer, "B" = the concealer). Final output: one graded video sequence with synced dialogue, ambient sound design, no score-driven manipulation, delivered as one continuous edit.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
The customer wants a self-contained confrontation scene that reads as directed cinema, not generic AI-dialogue coverage: restrained performance, no shouting, unbroken spatial logic between the two people, and visible craft in shot choice within a tight six-shot, ~40-second, minimal-camera-movement budget. The success criterion is that a viewer always knows, without thinking about it, who is on which side of the table and who is speaking/reacting — while feeling tension escalate through blocking, object, and silence rather than through cutting rate or camera movement.

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
The scene is built around one static geometric fact — a fixed table axis between A (left) and B (right) — and one physical object: a folded letter/document A has found, placed on the table as evidence of concealment. All six shots are variations on that fixed axis (wide, two OTS singles, one insert on the object, one isolated close single, return to the wide) so tension rises purely through what is chosen to be shown, not through how the camera moves. The confrontation ends unresolved — held eye contact over the folded letter — rather than a shouted climax, matching "restrained but tense."

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Geography first: two people, a table, a charged object between them (Shot 1).
2. The accusation is understated and object-anchored, not verbal-exposition-heavy: "You knew." (Shot 2).
3. The hurt is visible in restraint, not volume (Shot 3).
4. A beat of pure physical evidence — the object itself, trembling hand — carries subtext without dialogue (Shot 4).
5. The defense is quiet and defensive, not combative (Shot 5).
6. No resolution — held tension, geography reaffirmed (Shot 6). The viewer is left to judge, which is the emotional payload.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Axis of action: an imaginary line runs along the table between A and B. All six camera setups stay on one side of that line for the entire scene; screen sides are fixed for the whole scene — A is always frame-left with attention/eyeline to frame-right, B is always frame-right with attention/eyeline to frame-left, including in singles where the other person is off-frame.
- Shot sizes vary (wide → OTS medium → insert close → isolated close → wide) to avoid mechanical shot/reverse-shot monotony; only one true insert (the object) breaks the face-pattern and doubles as a breathing beat.
- Camera: locked-off tripod for every setup. No pans, no dollies, no reframing. Motion in the scene comes from actor blocking (hands, posture, a slid document, a flinch) rather than camera movement — this keeps coverage feeling composed rather than "camera-operated."
- Lighting: soft window key from camera-left, low fill, practical café ambience; keeps both faces readable without dramatizing via lighting change (restraint is carried by performance, not chiaroscuro).
- Palette: neutral warm café tones, desaturated slightly toward the confrontation shots (2–5) for psychological compression, returning to neutral in the final wide (6) for closure/distance.
- Aspect ratio: 16:9 throughout, consistent lens height (~eye level, slight downward table tilt in the wide only) across all setups.

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
Setup (fixed for entire scene): single café table, two chairs facing each other, camera positioned on one side of the table-axis line only. Three camera positions total, reused across the six shots:
- Position W (wide, centered, slightly camera-left of axis): used for Shots 1 and 6.
- Position OTS-A (behind A's shoulder, framing B): used for Shot 2.
- Position OTS-B (behind B's shoulder, framing A): used for Shot 3.
- Position CU-B (tight single on B, same side of line as OTS-A): used for Shot 5.
- Position Insert (overhead/table-level macro on the letter): used for Shot 4.
Wardrobe/props locked before shooting/generation: A wears muted cool tone, B wears muted warm tone (visually separates them at a glance); the letter/document prop must be visually identical across Shots 1, 2, 4, 6 (same fold, same paper stock, same position on table when not being handled).

Shot list, order, purpose, duration:
1. WIDE TWO-SHOT (establishing) — Purpose: lock geography, establish axis, silent beat. ~6s.
2. MEDIUM OTS on B (over A's shoulder) — Purpose: deliver the accusation, introduce the object into frame. ~7s.
3. MEDIUM OTS on A (over B's shoulder) — Purpose: restrained hurt/response. ~7s.
4. INSERT/CUTAWAY on the letter + B's hand — Purpose: subtext beat, no dialogue, breathing room, protects pacing without adding a face-cut. ~3s.
5. CLOSE SINGLE on B (isolated, same screen side as established) — Purpose: quiet defensive line, most intimate moment. ~6s.
6. WIDE TWO-SHOT (return to Shot 1's exact framing) — Purpose: unresolved closure, reaffirm geography, hold silence. ~9s.
Total: ~38–40s including 1–2s of held silence at head/tail.

DETERMINISTIC_OR_NON_GENERATIVE_ELEMENTS
- Character and prop continuity must be locked via fixed reference images (same actor likeness, wardrobe, letter prop, table/café set) reused across all six shot generations — this is the single highest risk point in generated (non-photographed) production and must be handled deterministically, not re-prompted per shot.
- Dialogue audio: record/generate once per line and treat as a locked deterministic audio asset synced to lip movement in each relevant shot, rather than regenerating audio per shot.
- Color grade: single deterministic LUT applied uniformly across all six shots in post to guarantee visual continuity that generation alone won't reliably produce.
- Subtitle/caption burn-in (if used) must exactly match spoken audio — no divergence between displayed and spoken text.

AUDIO_AND_EDIT
Dialogue is sparse and quiet by design; no non-diegetic score. Because so little dialogue is spoken, the remaining soundtrack (café ambience, cup, paper fold, chair creak, breath) becomes the vessel for tension — kept entirely diegetic so the audience isn't consciously aware of being cued, preserving the "restrained, not manipulated" tone. Edit rhythm: hard cuts only, no dissolves; each cut motivated by new information (a look, the object, a line) rather than mechanical alternation. The insert (Shot 4) functions as a reaction/breathing beat that interrupts the face-pattern without breaking spatial logic, and the return to the identical wide framing in Shot 6 provides closure by symmetry.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- Enforce one fixed action line for the entire scene; every shot (including singles) preserves each character's established screen side and eyeline direction even when the other is off-frame — this is the single most common way two-hander scenes become spatially confusing.
- Avoid shot/reverse/shot monotony by varying shot scale and inserting one true cutaway (the object) rather than a third face angle.
- Let blocking (a slid letter, a trembling hand, a slow fold) carry the scene's motion instead of camera movement, keeping coverage feeling directed rather than mechanically alternated.
- Guard prop/wardrobe/lighting consistency across all six generations explicitly — inconsistency here is the primary way a generated dialogue scene collapses believability.
- Keep dialogue lines short and vocally restrained in direction notes (quiet, low, controlled) to prevent generative delivery from drifting toward melodrama or raised volume.
