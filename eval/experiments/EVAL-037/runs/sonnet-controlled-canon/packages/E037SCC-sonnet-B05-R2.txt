FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
One continuous 16:9 narrative video, ~40 seconds, built from exactly 6 shots, cut together into a single scene: two people at a café table, restrained confrontation after one discovers the other concealed something important. No shouting, no camera acrobatics, full spatial clarity throughout.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
The customer wants a small, controlled dramatic scene that reads as directed cinema, not "coverage." The core creative risk is that a two-person table dialogue, cut conventionally, collapses into mechanical shot/reverse-shot ping-pong that feels like a video call. The brief's real requirements are: (1) unmistakable, stable geography between the two people across all six cuts, (2) tension carried by restraint — stillness, held silences, small physical tells — rather than volume, (3) a shot count and camera economy (six shots, minimal movement) that still reads as authored rather than default. The solution is to treat the six shots as a closed structure — establish, break into the two people, insert the physical proof of the concealment, hold on the accused, and return to the opening master — rather than as an open-ended alternation.

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
"The table doesn't move, but the ground shifts." The camera is almost entirely locked off; the scene's only real movement is the small object that changes hands/position on the table (the concealed thing, made physical — e.g., a folded receipt, ticket stub, or photograph placed down between them) and the shift in who can hold eye contact. Bookending the scene with the identical wide master at the start and end gives it a closed, authored shape — the audience registers that nothing about the room changed, only what is now known.

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Primary read (must land in first 8s): two people, a café table, calm surface, unspoken tension already present.
2. Secondary read (mid-scene): a concrete object surfaces — the "proof" — and the audience understands one person has been caught in a concealment.
3. Tertiary read (closing beat): restraint over resolution — no confession, no apology, no raised voice; the final wide shot leaves the outcome open but the rupture confirmed.
Nothing textual, no captions — all hierarchy carried by staging, object placement and performance.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Aspect ratio: 16:9, locked/static or near-imperceptible micro-push only (no pans, no whip, no handheld shake).
- Axis of action: drawn once, along the table, through the two people's sightline established in Shot 1. Person A (the one who discovered) is fixed frame-left throughout; Person B (the one who concealed) is fixed frame-right throughout. Every subsequent setup stays on the same side of this line so their eyelines always cross correctly and neither ever appears to address an unseen third person.
- Lighting: naturalistic café daylight or warm interior practical light, soft and consistent across all six setups (same time of day, same color temperature) — continuity of light sells continuity of geography.
- Lens/scale discipline: matched focal lengths and framing scale for the two reciprocal singles (Shots 2 and 3) so the cut between them doesn't visually jolt the audience.
- Color: muted, slightly desaturated naturalistic palette; no stylization that would compete with restraint.

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
Six shots, static or near-static camera, single afternoon café interior, two actors, one small physical prop (the "proof" object) and two beverage props (cups) for hand-business. Total runtime ≈ 40s.

Shot 1 — WIDE MASTER (establishing) — ~8s
Purpose: Establish geography and the axis of action; both people visible, A frame-left, B frame-right, table between them. A is still, hands around cup; B is mid small-talk gesture, unaware anything has shifted. Locked-off camera, eye-level, slight table-height angle.

Shot 2 — MEDIUM CLOSE-UP, Person A — ~6s
Purpose: A speaks the accusation quietly and flatly, looking frame-right (matches Shot 1 geometry). Minimal movement in frame; tension carried in stillness of the face and voice.

Shot 3 — MEDIUM CLOSE-UP, Person B (reciprocal) — ~6s
Purpose: B's reaction — stirring/hand motion stops mid-gesture, matched shot scale and eyeline frame-left. This is the "caught" beat, played through stillness, not shock.

Shot 4 — INSERT / CUTAWAY on the object — ~4s
Purpose: A places the concealed proof (folded paper/photo) on the table between them; frame holds both hands in shot (A's placing it, B's not yet reaching), reconfirming relative positions without a new master. This is also the buffer shot per continuity practice — it lets the scene breathe before returning to faces.

Shot 5 — WIDER TWO-SHOT / OVER-SHOULDER FAVORING B — ~9s
Purpose: Camera stays inside the same established arc, framed past A's shoulder (soft foreground, frame-left) onto B (frame-right), who delivers the restrained, non-shouted response — quiet, guarded, incomplete ("I was going to—"). A doesn't answer immediately.

Shot 6 — RETURN TO WIDE MASTER (identical setup to Shot 1) — ~7s
Purpose: Close the scene on the same locked-off wide frame; neither moves for a beat, the object remains on the table between them, one of them breaks eye contact or reaches for their cup. No resolution stated — the geometry that opened the scene now holds a different meaning.

GENERATION_PROMPTS
(Each is one executable shot-level prompt; static/locked camera unless noted; 16:9; naturalistic café interior, warm daylight, continuity of wardrobe/props across all six.)

SHOT 1: "Static wide shot, eye-level, inside a quiet daytime café. A woman (A) sits at a small table, frame-left, hands around a coffee cup, still and composed. Opposite her, a man (B) sits frame-right, mid-gesture, relaxed, saying something casual. Table and both figures fully visible, natural window light, muted color palette. No camera movement. Ambient café atmosphere, soft background blur of other patrons out of focus."

SHOT 2: "Static medium close-up on the woman (A), frame-left of center, looking screen-right toward her unseen companion. Her expression is controlled, flat, restrained — she speaks quietly, no raised voice, a slight tightening around the eyes. Warm natural light matching previous shot. No camera movement."

SHOT 3: "Static medium close-up on the man (B), frame-right of center, looking screen-left. His hand, holding a spoon or cup, stops moving mid-gesture as he registers what was just said. Controlled, guarded stillness in his face — no overt shock, just a held breath. Matched lighting and lens scale to prior close-up. No camera movement."

SHOT 4: "Static close insert shot, table-top level, shallow depth of field. A woman's hand (frame-left) places a small folded paper/photograph onto the café table between two coffee cups. A man's hand (frame-right) remains still, not yet reaching. Natural light, soft focus on background figures. No camera movement."

SHOT 5: "Static over-the-shoulder shot from behind the woman's shoulder (soft out-of-focus foreground, frame-left), framing the man (B) in focus, frame-right, across the café table. He speaks haltingly, quietly, restrained — an unfinished, guarded sentence, no shouting. Natural warm light, same café setting. No camera movement, or at most an almost imperceptible slow push-in of a few inches over the duration of the shot."

SHOT 6: "Static wide shot, identical framing and lighting to the opening shot — same café table, woman frame-left, man frame-right, the small paper object visible on the table between them. Neither speaks; one of them (A) looks down or reaches for her cup, breaking eye contact. Held for a beat of stillness before the scene ends. No camera movement."

DETERMINISTIC_OR_NON_GENERATIVE_ELEMENTS
- Exact table/prop continuity (cups, the folded paper/photo object, wardrobe) must be identical across all six generations — treat as a fixed asset list supplied to every shot generation, not re-imagined per shot.
- Screen-left/screen-right assignment (A=left, B=right) is a hard, non-negotiable continuity rule applied identically to every shot including the insert.
- Light direction and color temperature must be held constant across all six shots (single locked lighting reference).

AUDIO_AND_EDIT
- Dialogue delivered quietly, in a near-monotone, restrained register; no shouting, no overlapping speech. Lines are short, with real silences between them (silence is doing dramatic work).
- Suggested minimal line set: A: "You told me you sold it. Two years ago." → [silence, Shot 3] → A (over Shot 4 insert, V.O. or continued sync): "I found this last month." → B (Shot 5): "I was going to tell you—" → A: "When." → [Shot 6: silence].
- Ambient café bed (soft murmur, cup clinks, no music) under the whole scene; consider a single sparse, low piano or string note entering only under Shot 5–6 to underscore restraint, not to manipulate volume/drama.
- Cuts are hard cuts, no dissolves; the buffer/insert (Shot 4) functions as the breathing space between the two reciprocal close-ups, consistent with using a brief intervening shot to let a beat land before returning to faces.
- Shot 6 uses the exact Shot 1 framing to bookend the scene — an intentional "return to master" structure rather than continuous alternation, which is what keeps six shots from reading as generic coverage.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- Do not let any single shot cross to the far side of the table's axis of action — this is the single biggest risk to spatial clarity; if a generated shot yields the wrong side, discard and regenerate from the same locked reference rather than trying to fix it in the edit.
- Keep both reciprocal close-ups (Shots 2 and 3) at matched shot scale and lens feel; a mismatch in framing size between them will read as an error, not a style choice.
- Do not add camera movement beyond the single optional micro-push in Shot 5; any pan, whip, or handheld shake will break the "restrained" tone the brief requires.
- Do not let performers raise volume or gesture broadly — restraint must be visible in stillness and micro-expression, not just implied by the dialogue text.
- Ensure the prop (the concealed-thing object) is visually consistent in Shots 1, 4, 5, and 6 — a changed object undermines the "proof" beat.

HARD_CONSTRAINT_CHECK
- Two people, café table, opposite seating: met.
- One discovers the other concealed something important: met (proof object + dialogue).
- Tense, restrained dialogue, nobody shouts: met (staging + performance + audio notes enforce this).
- Viewer always understands spatial relationship: met via locked axis of action, matched eyelines, bookended master shots.
- Exactly six shots: met.
- Minimal/no elaborate camera movement: met (static/locked, one optional micro-push).
- Feels cinematic, not mechanical coverage: addressed via bookend structure, insert shot, and restrained blocking rather than plain alternating shot/reverse-shot.
- ~40 seconds total: met (8+6+6+4+9+7 = 40s).
- 16:9: met.
- No external website used: met — no website material referenced.

KNOWLEDGE_AND_WEBSITE_USE
Canon knowledge used:
- Grammar of the Shot (Ch. 4, continuity) — axis of action / 180-degree line established by talent sight-line, and the requirement that all coverage stay within one arc so screen position and direction of attention persist even when the other subject is off-frame. Used to fix A=left/B=right for the entire scene and to police every shot's camera side.
- Grammar of the Edit (Ch. 3–5, editing decisions) — the action line applied to reciprocal singles (matching look room prevents the "both looking the same way" error), eye-line match/eye-trace across cuts, and the practice of cutting in a reaction/insert shot to break up sustained address and buy the audience time before returning to faces. Used to design the reciprocal close-ups (Shots 2–3), the insert (Shot 4), and the cutting logic overall.
- Kenworthy, Master Shots (Ch. 8, directing attention) — static wide master with blocking (rather than camera movement) as the way to carry a tense two-person scene, with the dominant speaker facing camera. Used to justify keeping Shots 1 and 6 fully static and to shape the "who faces camera when" blocking logic across the scene.
