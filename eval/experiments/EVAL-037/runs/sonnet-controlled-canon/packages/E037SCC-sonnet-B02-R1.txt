FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
One (1) static poster, 4:5 aspect ratio (e.g., 1600×2000 px minimum, scalable to print), for digital + print festive campaign use. Single final composition, no variants.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
The customer needs a poster that does two things simultaneously without contradiction: (1) make ₹9 (image) and ₹99 (video) instantly, unambiguously readable as the commercial hook, and (2) read as serious AI infrastructure branding — precise, metered, invoice-grade — not a retail discount flyer. Per aight's own site language ("prepaid rupees," "hard caps," "one domestic GST invoice," "a founder who answers"), the brand's actual differentiator is operational precision and trustworthy plumbing, not cheapness. The poster must translate low price into evidence of engineering efficiency ("this is what infrastructure pricing looks like"), not into evidence of a sale. Festive season is the occasion/context, not the visual language driver — it should be present as a restrained seasonal cue, not a decorative takeover.

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
"The Rate Card, Engineered." Treat the poster as a precision price ledger / meter reading rather than a promotional badge. Two SKU-style line items — IMAGE and VIDEO — are set with tabular, monospace-style numerals at equal visual weight, aligned on a shared vertical rule like a utility bill or infrastructure dashboard. Festive identity is carried through a single restrained motif (a minimal foil-line diya/rangoli mark used as a typographic divider/ornament, not a scene) and a jewel-tone palette (deep indigo/charcoal ground, warm gold linework, one small vermilion/marigold accent) — festive through material and color discipline, not through clutter or starbursts. Hierarchy is built entirely by grid geometry and type weight (per structural design principle: assign each kind of information — brand, price, proof, CTA — its own column width and weight so the ordering is read off the structure itself, not off decoration).

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Brand identity block (smallest zone, top): wordmark "aight." + one-line positioning "Where Indian businesses buy AI." — establishes credibility register before price is seen.
2. Festive ornament divider (thin band): single gold foil-line diya/rangoli mark — signals occasion, does not compete for attention.
3. PRIMARY zone (largest, visual center of gravity, ~45% of vertical space): the two-line price ledger —
   IMAGE GENERATION — ₹9
   VIDEO GENERATION — ₹99
   Set at identical scale/weight to imply parity of the two product lines and precision, not a "starting from" teaser.
4. Proof/trust line (small, directly below ledger): "GPT · Claude · Gemini · Sarvam — one prepaid wallet. One GST invoice."
5. CTA + footer band (bottom, smallest, quiet): "Talk to us" · getaight.ai · one-line reassurance "Prepaid credits. Hard caps. No forex. No reverse charge."
No countdown clocks, no "OFFER," no slashed prices, no percentage-off language — nothing in the discount-flyer register.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Palette: near-black indigo/charcoal ground (#0E1015–#12131A range); warm metallic gold linework and primary numerals (#C9A24B–#D4AF37 range); single restrained vermilion/marigold accent (#D9642F–#E2703A) used only on the ornament flame-tip or the shared vertical rule — festive without being bright/retail.
- Typography: a disciplined two-family system — a geometric/grotesk sans for brand, tagline, proof line, CTA (small, controlled tracking); a tabular/monospace-leaning numeral face for the ₹9 / ₹99 figures so they read as metered data, not marketing type. Rupee symbol and numerals given generous scale and letterspacing precision; unit text ("/ generation") set much smaller and lighter beside each number so the number itself is the unmistakable read.
- Grid: strict single-column-width system top to bottom; every information class (brand, ornament, price ledger, proof, footer) occupies its own fixed band and shared left/right margins — no floating badges, no diagonal banners, no starburst shapes. A single thin vertical or horizontal gold rule ties the two price lines together, reinforcing "these are line items of one system," not two separate promos.
- Texture: subtle premium paper/foil-stamp micro-texture on the dark ground; no photographic clutter, no crowded festive iconography (no marketplace bunting, no confetti, no glitter bursts).
- Negative space: generous margins around the price block so it reads as confident and engineered, per the discipline that reduced, well-spaced elements differentiate themselves more effectively than a busy field.

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
Two-layer build to guarantee price/text accuracy (generative models are unreliable at exact small numerals/currency symbols at high fidelity):
Layer 1 — AI-generated background/ornamental plate: dark jewel-tone textured ground with the gold foil-line diya/rangoli ornament, subtle premium texture, and the grid guide bands, with all typography areas left clean/negative-space only (no AI-rendered text).
Layer 2 — Deterministic typesetting overlay: all copy (wordmark, tagline, "IMAGE GENERATION — ₹9," "VIDEO GENERATION — ₹99," proof line, CTA, footer) set as vector type in design software using the specified type system, composited exactly per the grid. This is the layer that must render the prices — never leave ₹9/₹99 to open-ended generative text rendering.
Final export: flatten to single 4:5 image, check contrast/legibility at thumbnail size (mobile feed scale) before finalizing.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- Risk: generative model renders incorrect/garbled currency figures → mitigated by keeping all numerals and currency symbols in the deterministic typesetting layer only.
- Risk: festive iconography overwhelms the price message, tipping into "discount flyer" territory → mitigated by restricting festive cues to one restrained gold-line ornament and jewel-tone palette, with the grid enforcing equal, generous negative space around the price ledger.
- Risk: two different prices for two different products confuse hierarchy (which is the "headline" price?) → mitigated by giving IMAGE and VIDEO equal type weight/scale in the same ledger format, explicitly framing them as two SKU line items of one system rather than one hero price with a smaller second price.
- Risk: poster reads as cheap because price is low → mitigated by pricing being presented as a precise, metered ledger entry (tabular numerals, ledger rule, invoice-adjacent language "one GST invoice") rather than a slashed/highlighted discount, per the structural insight that low price signals value-engineering rather than desperation when framed as supplier precision, not consumer bargain-hunting.
- Risk: illegibility at small (mobile feed) sizes → mitigated by testing the price block's scale and contrast against the primary hierarchy requirement before final export; unit labels kept secondary in size so the numerals alone are legible at thumbnail scale.
