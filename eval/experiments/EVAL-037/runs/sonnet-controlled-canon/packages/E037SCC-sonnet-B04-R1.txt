FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
One 9:16 vertical UGC-style performance video, target length ≈25 seconds, single deliverable (no alternates). Fictional D2C skincare brand: "Tvacha Skin," product: "Tvacha Clear Base — 2% Salicylic Acid Serum, Fragrance-Free," for oily, blemish-prone adult skin. One Indian woman, direct-to-camera, home setting.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
The brief asks for content that reads as an organic creator recommendation, not an ad shoot — because on short-form platforms, visible production polish reads as a sales pitch and triggers skepticism, while an unglossy, slightly imperfect, "caught mid-life" presentation reads as a peer sharing something that worked. The single job of this asset is to deliver exactly one believable, non-clinical purchase reason (this serum treats oily/blemish-prone skin without added fragrance/irritation) inside a runtime too short for tangents. Everything else — set, wardrobe, camera handling, edit rhythm — exists to support that credibility and that one reason, not to showcase filmmaking.

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
"The serum that finally makes sense for my skin." A relatable Indian woman, filming herself at home the way she'd film any get-ready-with-me clip, names the exact frustration of oily/breakout-prone skin, shows the product and how she actually uses it, states one plain reason it earned a place in her routine (fragrance-free, 2% salicylic acid, made for oily/blemish-prone skin — not something that adds irritation), and closes with a low-key, non-hyped call to action. No graphs, no before/after, no dermatologist claim — just one honest reason, demonstrated.

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Primary (must land): This serum is formulated specifically for oily, blemish-prone skin and is fragrance-free — so it treats the problem without adding a common irritant.
2. Secondary: It's lightweight/fast-absorbing, not sticky or greasy (sensory reassurance for oily-skin users who fear heavy products).
3. Tertiary: Soft CTA ("linking it below") — no urgency language, no discount claim, no guarantee.
Explicitly excluded: percentage-improvement claims, "clinically proven," "dermatologist recommended," "cures/removes acne," before-after imagery, timeframes for results.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Aspect ratio: 9:16, full-bleed vertical, safe-margin text zone top 12% / bottom 20% for platform UI overlap.
- Look: handheld phone-selfie aesthetic — natural window/room light (soft daylight, slightly warm), no studio lighting rig, no beauty dish, visible minor asymmetries (slight camera shake, natural room clutter softly out of focus).
- Talent: one Indian woman, appears mid-to-late 20s, realistic skin texture retained (visible pores/texture — not airbrushed; skin texture is itself part of the credibility of a skin product), casual homewear (oversized tee or plain kurta), minimal/no makeup, hair loosely tied.
- Setting: home bedroom or bathroom counter, believable Indian middle-class domestic detail (mirror, everyday items), consistent across all shots.
- Product asset: one fixed bottle design — matte off-white or sage bottle, minimal sans-serif label reading "Tvacha Skin — Clear Base, 2% Salicylic Acid, Fragrance-Free," reused identically in every shot (no regeneration drift).
- Color: naturalistic, slightly warm, no heavy grade; avoid glossy commercial color science.

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
Format: 6-shot creator-style sequence, cut like a real phone edit (hard cuts, no crossfades/transitions), total ≈25s.
Identity & asset lock: establish one fixed character reference (face, hair, wardrobe, room) and one fixed product-label reference before generating shots; reuse both references in every shot to avoid drift.

Shot list:

1. HOOK (0:00–0:04, ~4s) — Purpose: stop-scroll relatability.
Framing: medium close-up, slight low handheld angle (selfie POV), direct eye contact.
Action/VO: "Okay if you have oily, breakout-prone skin like me, you know—"
Mood: casual, mid-sentence energy, as if caught naturally talking.

2. HOOK CONTINUED (0:04–0:08, ~4s) — Purpose: complete the frustration, build identification.
Framing: same handheld medium close-up, tiny natural handheld drift.
Action/VO: "—finding something that actually works without messing your skin up further is SO hard."
Small natural gesture (hand near face, slight head tilt).

3. PRODUCT REVEAL / DEMO (0:08–0:13, ~5s) — Purpose: show the product, deliver core reason.
Framing: cut to closer insert shot, hands + product in frame, bottle label clearly legible, dispensing one drop onto fingertip.
Action/VO: "I've been using this — it's fragrance-free, 2% salicylic acid, literally made for skin like ours."
Detail: natural hand motion, real twist-cap/dropper action, no CGI-perfect liquid.

4. APPLICATION (0:13–0:18, ~5s) — Purpose: prove real use, sensory reassurance.
Framing: close-up on face/cheek, fingertips patting product in, slight camera handheld sway.
Action/VO: "It's so lightweight, sinks in in seconds — no sticky film, no fragrance stinging."
Skin should look normal/textured, not glossy or retouched.

5. PAYOFF / DIRECT ADDRESS (0:18–0:23, ~5s) — Purpose: restate the one purchase reason with conviction.
Framing: back to medium close-up direct-to-camera, bottle held near jaw/shoulder, casually not "presented."
Action/VO: "This is the first serum that's actually made sense for my skin."
Tone: quietly confident, not performative/salesy.

6. CTA (0:23–0:25, ~2s) — Purpose: soft close.
Framing: same medium close-up, slight smile, small nod.
Action/VO: "Linking it below."
On-screen (deterministic overlay, not generated): brand wordmark + product name small lower-third, appears only in this final 2s.

DETERMINISTIC_OR_NON_GENERATIVE_ELEMENTS
- Final lower-third text card (shot 6 only): brand wordmark "Tvacha Skin" + product name "Clear Base Serum" — added as a static post-production overlay, not generated, using fixed brand font/color.
- Burned-in captions synced to VO across all 6 shots (standard UGC/accessibility convention), plain sans-serif, high-contrast, added in edit — not generated.
- Product label artwork: fixed, pre-approved asset reused via the identity-lock reference described above; never freshly regenerated per shot to prevent label drift/misspelling.
- Any legal/mandatory disclaimer text (e.g., "Results may vary," if brand's legal team requires it) — static text card, post-production, not scripted into dialogue.

AUDIO_AND_EDIT
- Sync dialogue: her own voice, natural conversational pace, mild authentic Indian-English inflection, no voiceover-artist cadence.
- Room tone kept under dialogue (not fully clean) to preserve "phone-recorded" authenticity.
- Low-level trending-style background music bed (licensed/production-library, not identifiable copyrighted commercial track), volume well under dialogue, fades in from shot 3.
- Edit: hard cuts only between the 6 shots, no dissolves/wipes/lower-third animations except the final static text card — mimics native creator editing rather than agency editing.
- Captions burned in throughout, key phrase ("fragrance-free," "2% salicylic acid," "oily, blemish-prone skin") may be lightly emphasized in caption styling since this is the one purchase reason.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- Risk: image looks like a commercial, not UGC → mitigated by handheld framing, natural light only, unretouched skin, hard cuts, no glossy grade (per credibility-through-imperfection principle below).
- Risk: talent reads as scripted/actorly, which undercuts trust → keep line lengths short, conversational, mid-sentence hook start, natural micro-gestures; avoid overly polished delivery or a "studied" sales tone.
- Risk: implied clinical/medical claim creeping in via phrasing (e.g., "clears acne," "dermatologist recommended," "clinically proven") → script locked to sensory/formulation language only ("fragrance-free," "made for oily/blemish-prone skin," "lightweight," "no sticky film"); no percentages beyond the factual 2% ingredient concentration, no before/after, no guarantee language.
- Risk: product label/bottle inconsistency across generated shots → single locked product reference reused verbatim in every shot; label text never regenerated freeform.
- Risk: identity drift (different-looking woman/room across shots) → single locked character+room reference reused across all 6 shots.
- Risk: hand/finger artifacts during product demo close-ups (shots 3–4) → flagged explicitly in negative prompt; these are the highest-scrutiny frames and should be reviewed first in QA.
- Risk: caption/text overlay claims exceeding what's spoken → text overlay limited to brand/product name only, no added claims in graphics.
