FINAL_PRODUCTION_PACKAGE

DELIVERABLE
One (1) vertical 9:16 video advertisement, target runtime ≈30 seconds (working range 28–32s), master delivered as a single continuous edit assembled from 12 generated/sourced shots. Primary use: paid social (Reels/Shorts/Stories) and WhatsApp/website embed for Indian hostel & PG owners. No alternate cuts, no campaign variants — one finished concept only.

OBJECTIVE_INTERPRETATION
RentOk needs a performance-credible brand ad that makes a specific, target-recognisable pain vivid — the manual, leak-prone way hostel/PG owners currently run rent collection, KYC, complaints and move-outs (WhatsApp threads, notebooks, loose spreadsheets) — and then resolves it with RentOk as a single system, using RentOk's own real feature set and language. Success is measured by whether an actual PG/hostel owner watching on a phone feels "this is literally my Tuesday" in the first three seconds, and "I want this" by the end — not by production polish. The ad must read as contemporary urban Indian reality, not as a translated Silicon Valley SaaS explainer: Hinglish language, real Indian PG/hostel production design (steel almirah, notice board, tubelight, rooftop water tank), and RentOk's own idiom ("jhikjhik", "Why rent like it's 1990 when you're in 2026?").

CORE_CREATIVE_IDEA
"Still running 2026 like it's 1990?" — RentOk's own site copy ("Why rent like 1990s when you are in 2026?") becomes the spine of the film. Act one shows a PG owner trapped in analog-era chaos happening today. A single hard pivot (a door closing / a notebook slamming shut) flips the film from clutter to clarity, and every pain beat shown in Act one is answered one-to-one by a named RentOk feature in Act two — same problem, same shot grammar, resolved. The device is entirely visual/behavioural (per the Fevicol-style principle that a culturally familiar situation can carry the whole argument without a lecture of copy) — dialogue and text stay minimal and Hinglish, never explainer-toned.

MESSAGE_AND_INFORMATION_HIERARCHY
1. Hook (0–3s): recognisable chaos, no brand yet — "is this normal?" jolt.
2. Escalation (3–12s): four fast, specific leakages named through image, not lecture — rent follow-ups, scattered KYC papers, lost complaint, missed dues — each one a future payoff setup.
3. Turn (12–16s): one decisive visual pivot from clutter to system; brand enters here, not before.
4. Proof-of-system (16–23.5s): four real RentOk features shown resolving the exact four earlier leakages, in the same order (rent reminders → digital KYC → complaint tracking → floor plan/room status).
5. Emotional payoff (23.5–26s): the owner, relaxed — "work less" made visible, not just claimed.
6. Credential + CTA (26–30s): RentOk logo, site-verified trust stats, tagline, single clear action.
This ordering mirrors a guide-delivers-a-plan structure (character → problem → guide → plan → call to action → success) without ever naming itself as such on screen.

VISUAL_SYSTEM
- Palette: Act one — dim tungsten/tubelight greenish-yellow, slightly desaturated, cramped framing. Act two — clean daylight, warm neutral whites, RentOk's brand blue/teal used only in UI and logo, never painted onto the real world (keeps the "real India" texture honest).
- Camera: Act one handheld, slightly unsteady, close and cluttered (35–50mm equivalent feel); Act two locked-off or slow gimbal, wider, breathing room. The visual language itself performs the "chaos → order" argument.
- Production design (deterministic authenticity anchors): steel Godrej-style almirah, hand-written rent register with cutting/overwriting, tubelight with visible flicker, notice board with peeling tape, Aadhaar photocopies, rooftop water tank, drying clothes line, plastic chair, chai glass — all common, unstaged-looking Indian PG/hostel signifiers, not generic "startup office" or Western apartment cues.
- Cast: one PG/hostel owner, Indian, mid-40s, moderate build, half-sleeve checked shirt, spectacles pushed up on head, visible wristwatch — consistent across all shots (character bible below). No voiceover-agency accent; natural Hindi-English code-switching.
- Typography/on-screen text: bold, high-contrast sans, large safe-margin captions (mobile-legible), Hinglish phrases lifted from real speech patterns, never a paragraph — one short line per beat.
- UI screens: must be real RentOk app captures or brand-approved mockups, not invented interfaces (see Deterministic section).

PRODUCTION_RECIPE
Format: 12 shots, generated as short video clips (mix of ~2–3.5s each) plus two screen-capture composites, assembled in a single vertical edit with a hard pivot cut at ~12s and a music/sound-design shift at the same point. Chaos-section cuts run fast (~2–2.5s/shot) for anxious energy; solution-section cuts run slightly longer (~2.5–3s/shot) to read as calm and controlled — pace itself carries the emotional argument, not just the picture. Where a generated cut can't satisfy every continuity criterion, protect emotion and story first, spatial/eyeline continuity last (sacrifice-upward rule). All UI beats are hard-cut into the generated live-action footage as clean insert shots (phone-in-hand framing), not blended.

FAILURE_PREVENTION
- Do not caricature tenants or portray them negatively; the friction shown is the owner's operational burden, never a tenant's fault.
- Do not invent UI, stats, pricing, or features not present on rentok.com; all product claims must trace to the site content read for this brief.
- Avoid generic "diverse open-plan startup office" imagery — every location must read as an actual Indian PG/hostel setting (this is the single biggest risk of sliding into "Silicon Valley explainer" territory).
- Avoid over-long or over-explained captions; keep each on-screen line under ~5 words per the "one message per beat" discipline.
- Guard mobile legibility: no text smaller than a large safe-margin size, nothing placed in the top/bottom UI-obscured zones of vertical social players.
- Keep the tone confident, not apologetic or gimmicky about "villainising" spreadsheets/notebooks — the humour should be recognition, not mockery of the owner.
- Do not let the pivot transition (Shot 5) become a jarring continuity break; protect emotional read and story sense first if a perfect match-cut isn't achievable in generation.
