# Family 6 — Creative / commercial instruments

**Judges:** does the ad work?
**Status: NOT QUALIFIED. Pack does not exist. Structurally different from every other family.**

---

## This family has no right answer, and that changes everything

Families 1–5 ask questions with correct answers. This one does not. "Is this ad
any good" is not a question with a ground truth, and any instrument reporting an
absolute creative score is producing a number with nothing behind it.

Two consequences, both binding:

**1 · Hard-fidelity and creative-quality checks are separate instruments and
must never be merged.** "Is the headline spelled correctly" has a right answer.
"Is this ad any good" does not. **One evaluator cannot honestly do both** — this
is a standing approved decision, not a preference.

**2 · Every capability in this family is `descriptive_only` and may never become
a hard routing constraint.** The E1 validator enforces this mechanically.

---

## What *is* measurable

Reframe from judgement to **discrimination and detection**, both of which are
testable:

| Honest form | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Pairwise comparison** — "which of these two better conveys the stated proposition?" | Relative, repeatable, and has a defensible reference in human preference |
| **Issue detection** — "does this asset have a stated problem?" | Detection has a right answer once a human panel agrees a problem exists |
| **Register discrimination** — "which of these two is the premium execution?" | Tests whether register is perceptible at all before asking whether it is achieved |

## The measurement nobody remembers to take

> **False criticism must be measured alongside detection.**

An instrument that flags problems in flawless work is as useless as one that
misses real problems — and it is *more* insidious, because it looks diligent.
The qualification pack must therefore include **known-clean assets** where the
correct answer is "no issue", and the instrument's false-criticism rate is a
headline result, not a footnote.

## Two hard sourcing rules

**Public or source preference labels are never our creative truth.** Dataset
labels are one party's observations. This is a frozen project separation, and it
has already been demonstrated concretely elsewhere: two dataset releases from
the *same* lab disagree with each other on about a third of matched regions.
Creative judgement requires **fresh, independent human review**.

**Selection must be independent of Canon's principles.** If we choose assets
that illustrate the principles Canon teaches, then test whether Canon-derived
judgement matches them, we have built a circle: *books teach hierarchy → pick
hierarchy examples → Canon "wins"*. Resources selects; Eval does not pick
flattering examples.

## Canon boundary — Eval does not own this

Canon owns **what good commercial media must accomplish**. Eval consumes those
dimensions and measures whether a workflow achieves them. If measuring a
creative dimension seems to require inventing what "good hierarchy" means, that
is a **cross-stream proposal to Canon, not a unilateral Eval definition**.

Likewise, Eval must **not** author its own commercial briefs for the later
end-to-end benchmark. Those come from Canon's 30-brief bank after integration.

routing.** That is not an argument against doing it — creative acceptance is
what "Cost per Accepted Outcome" actually means — but it should be sequenced
after the families that can gate hard constraints.
